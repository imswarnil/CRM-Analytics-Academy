# Who This Is For

Three kinds of people arrive at a CRM Analytics course, they want different things, and pretending otherwise wastes everybody's time. Read the three tracks, pick the one that describes you this month, and follow its route. You can change tracks later; most people do.

## Track A — the analyst

You work in or near a revenue team. You live in reports and dashboards, maybe Salesforce reports, maybe a spreadsheet that has outgrown itself. You have been handed CRM Analytics, or you want to be the person who gets handed it.

::note{icon="i-lucide-user"}
**Your route.** Everything, in order, sections 1 through 10. Do not skip section 2 because it looks like administration — the licence and permission-set model is the single most common reason a CRM Analytics project stalls in week one, and being the person who can unblock it is worth more than any chart skill.
::

**Where it will hurt.** Section 3, on grain and joins. It is the most abstract part of the course and it arrives early because nothing after it makes sense otherwise. Budget two evenings for it rather than one, and build every example rather than reading it.

**Where it will click.** Section 5, the first time you replace six charts with three and the dashboard becomes *more* useful. That lesson changes how people design for the rest of their career.

## Track B — the GTM engineer

You already know data. SQL is comfortable, you have opinions about dbt or BigQuery, you may have built a marketing attribution model. What you do not have is Salesforce's platform, and you are here because the pipeline data lives inside it and the company wants the analytics to live there too.

::note{icon="i-lucide-workflow"}
**Your route.** Read section 1 quickly — twenty minutes, mostly to learn the vocabulary, because Salesforce renames things. Read section 2 properly; it is not optional and it has no analogue in the tools you know. Skim section 3 for the recipe mechanics and *do not* skim the grain lesson. Then go to section 7 and work sections 7 through 10 in full. Come back to sections 4 to 6 when you need them.
::

**Where it will hurt.** The absence of things you take for granted. There is no `CREATE TABLE`, version control is a managed package away, and SAQL is not SQL — it is a stream-transformation language where the order of statements is the query plan. Section 4's SAQL lesson exists mostly for you.

**Where it will click.** Section 7's metric contracts, and section 8's attribution lesson. Those two are the parts of the job that are the same everywhere, and CRM Analytics happens to be a good place to demonstrate them because the security and the CRM join are already solved.

## Track C — the interview candidate

You have an interview in two or three weeks. You need to speak credibly about CRM Analytics, ideally with something to show.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-clock"}
**Your route, and the honest warning.** Two weeks is enough to interview well and not enough to be good. Do this: section 1 in full, section 2's licence and security lessons, section 3's grain lesson, section 5 in full, then Build 1 through Build 4 from section 8 and Build 7 from section 9. Read every section's interview questions — all ten sets — and answer them out loud before reading the model answer. Then build *one* dashboard properly and be ready to talk for ten minutes about why each widget is on it.
::

**The thing that wins interviews** is not feature recall, which the interviewer can look up. It is being able to say: "here is a dashboard I built, here is the decision it supports, here is the metric definition I had to argue about, and here is what I deliberately left off it." Section 7's *Designing for Decisions* lesson and section 10's executive board are written to give you exactly that story.

## Who should not take this course

Being told to leave is more useful than being strung along:

- **You need Tableau, not CRM Analytics.** If your data does not live in Salesforce and you have no plan to put it there, CRM Analytics is the wrong tool and this is the wrong course. Tableau Next, Power BI or a warehouse plus Looker will serve you better.
- **You need a report, once, this week.** Build a Salesforce report. It is free, instant, and adequate for a single answer. CRM Analytics earns its cost when many people need many answers on top of joined data.
- **You want a certificate by Friday.** The exam-focused study guides exist and are shorter. This course is longer on purpose.
- **You are looking for AI to do it for you.** Einstein Discovery and conversational analytics are both covered — section 1's intelligence layer, section 4's conversational lesson, and honestly in section 9's lead-score audit, where the model turns out to predict less than everyone assumed. Every one of those lessons shows the tooling working *and* shows how to check it. If you are hoping to skip understanding the data, the data will win.
