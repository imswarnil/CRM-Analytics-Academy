# Dates, History and Snapshots

Two ideas that carry most of the datasets and modelling section: getting dates *typed* right, and getting history to exist at all.

## Dates as a type, not as text

A date must be typed as a date for filters, month groupings and trend lines to work. Text dates look perfect in a table and fail everywhere else — and the failure arrives when you build your first trend chart and discover the axis is sorted alphabetically.

Three practical points:

- **Fiscal periods** rarely match calendar ones. Namilio's fiscal year is not January-to-December, and a chart labelled "Q3" that means the calendar quarter will be argued about in a meeting. Decide which you mean and put it in the title.
- **Time zones** shift a date by one day at the boundaries. A deal closed at 6pm on the 31st in one zone closed on the 1st in another, which moves revenue between months. On a monthly dashboard that is the difference between hitting and missing.
- **Multiple dates per row** is the norm. An opportunity has created, close, last-modified and stage-change dates. Every trend chart must say which one it is grouped by, because the same data grouped by a different date tells a genuinely different story.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-calendar-x"}
**The cohort error is the most common date mistake in go-to-market analytics.** Cohort leads by the date they were *created*, not by the date they converted. Cohorting by conversion date makes last month look catastrophic — its leads have not had time to convert yet — and makes a year-old month look wonderful, because everything that ever converted from it has. Both numbers are individually correct, which is why the error survives review. Section 9 makes this concrete with Namilio's 2,800 leads.
::

## Why history needs a snapshot

The Salesforce Opportunity object holds **today's** amount and **today's** stage. It has no memory. So this entire class of question is unanswerable from it:

- What did the pipeline look like at the start of the quarter?
- How much of today's closed revenue was in the forecast in March?
- Which deals slipped from last quarter into this one?
- What was this account's ARR six months ago?

::note{icon="i-lucide-camera"}
**A snapshot dataset is a dataset that appends the current state each run** rather than replacing it. Run weekly, it accumulates one row per opportunity per week — a record of what was true at each point, which no object can reconstruct afterwards. This is the single most valuable thing a CRM Analytics implementation produces that the CRM cannot, and it is worth setting up early precisely because it cannot be backfilled.
::

Namilio's `arr_snapshots.csv` is exactly this shape: 1,579 rows, one per account per month, with a `MovementType` on each. That file is why the RevOps section can build an ARR waterfall at all — new business, expansion, contraction and churn are *movements between snapshots*, and no amount of cleverness recovers them from current state.

## Period comparison

Once history exists, the questions become comparative — this month against last, this quarter against the same quarter last year, a cohort against the one before it.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-git-compare-arrows"}
**Say which comparison you mean, in the widget title.** "Up 12%" is meaningless on its own: up against last month, against the same month last year, or against plan? Each answers a different business question and they frequently disagree in sign. A title that reads "Net New ARR vs. same month last year" costs nothing and prevents the most common misreading of an executive dashboard.
::
