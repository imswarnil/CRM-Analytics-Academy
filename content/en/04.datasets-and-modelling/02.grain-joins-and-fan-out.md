# Grain, Joins and Fan-Out

This is the most important lesson in the course. Build it rather than reading it — the point is to watch a wrong number appear on your own screen, with no warning anywhere, and feel how ordinary it looks.

## What fan-out is

A one-to-many join multiplies rows on the "one" side. Any measure belonging to the "one" side is now repeated across each of its children, so summing it counts it several times.

::funnel-viz
---
stages:
  - label: "Opportunities"
    value: 608
    note: "One row per deal. Amount correct here — $13.4M"
  - label: "After joining product lines"
    value: 1192
    note: "Each deal's Amount now sits on 1–5 rows — most on the biggest deals"
  - label: "Sum of Amount"
    value: 1192
    note: "≈ $36.9M. No error, no warning, every row individually correct"
caption: "The Academy, joined at the wrong grain"
scale: linear
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Nothing about this failure is exotic**, which is exactly why it is dangerous. The join is correct. Every row is correct. The dataset builds, the dashboard renders, the chart is handsome, and the number is nearly three times too big. It passes review because reviewing means looking at a screen, and the screen looks right.
::

## The two fixes, and when each is correct

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Aggregate before joining"
    subtitle: "Collapse the many side first"
    icon: "i-lucide-combine"
    pros:
      - "Output stays at one row per deal — the grain you probably want"
      - "Parent measures stay safe to sum"
      - "You choose exactly what the lines contribute: a count, a sum, a max"
    cons:
      - "Product-level detail is gone from this dataset"
    verdict: "Right when the dataset is about DEALS."
  - title: "Keep the fine grain, drop the parent measure"
    subtitle: "Stay at line level, deliberately"
    icon: "i-lucide-scissors"
    pros:
      - "Product-level analysis stays possible"
      - "Line amounts are genuinely per-line, so they sum correctly"
    cons:
      - "Anyone using this dataset must know the grain, or they will sum the wrong thing"
      - "Deal counts need distinct counts, not row counts"
    verdict: "Right when the dataset is about PRODUCTS."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-file-text"}
**Whichever you choose, write the grain into the dataset's description.** "One row per opportunity" or "one row per opportunity per product" takes five seconds and it is the single most useful sentence anyone downstream will read. A dataset whose grain is undocumented is a trap you set for a colleague.
::

## The three symptoms, and what each means

::field-table
---
dataset: diagnosing a wrong total
grain: one row per symptom
fields:
  - name: "Exactly 2×, 3× or suspiciously round"
    type: "fan-out"
    role: "measure"
    note: "A parent measure duplicated across children. Count rows before and after each join node until the multiplication appears."
  - name: "Slightly high, not a clean multiple"
    type: "partial fan-out"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Only SOME parents have multiple children — the usual real-world case, and harder to spot precisely because the number is not a clean multiple. The Academy has deals with one line and deals with five, and the five-line deals are the largest — which is why revenue inflates by 2.75× while rows only double."
  - name: "Lower than expected"
    type: "an inner join, or permissions"
    role: "key"
    note: "An inner join silently drops rows with no match on the other side. If it is not the join, it is the integration user or the predicate — missing means permissions, as the access section said."
  - name: "Right total, wrong breakdown"
    type: "grouping on a fanned column"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "The subtle one. The total is correct because the measure was aggregated properly, but grouping by a child attribute distributes it in a way that does not mean what it appears to mean."
---
::

## Distinct counts

Once a dataset has a fine grain, **count of rows stops meaning count of deals**. At line grain, 1,192 rows is 608 opportunities. Use a distinct count on the opportunity key rather than a row count, and check it against a number you know.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-bug"}
**Debug by narrowing, not by staring.** Filter to a single opportunity, switch the lens to table mode, and count the rows by hand. Thirty seconds, and it tells you exactly which side multiplied — where an hour of looking at an aggregate total tells you nothing at all.
::
