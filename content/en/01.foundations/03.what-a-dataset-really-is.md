# What a Dataset Really Is

A dataset looks like a table and behaves like a decision. This lesson is about the decision.

## Materialised, not live

When a recipe runs, it reads its sources, does its work, and **writes a new copy** of the data in CRM Analytics' own columnar store. That copy is the dataset. Until the next run, it does not change — not when a rep edits an opportunity, not when a record is deleted, not when you reload the dashboard.

::note{icon="i-lucide-clock"}
**Every "the number is wrong" investigation starts with the same question: when did this last run?** Data Manager's Monitor tab has the answer, and roughly a third of the time that is the whole investigation. It is not a workaround — the staleness is a design property, traded for the ability to join five sources and aggregate millions of rows in under a second.
::

This also produces the most useful property a dataset has. The Salesforce Opportunity object holds **today's** amount and **today's** stage; it has no memory. A dataset run weekly, appending the current state each time, accumulates history that the object cannot give you — which is why section 10's ARR waterfall is possible at all, and why it insists on `arr_snapshots.csv` being a snapshot rather than something recomputed from current state.

## Grain: the one question

::tip{icon="i-lucide-ruler"}
**What does exactly one row represent?** Ask it of every dataset, before you build anything, and write the answer down. Almost every wrong number in analytics is a grain question that was never asked.
::

Namilio's own tables, stated as grain:

::field-table
---
dataset: Namilio — grain of each table
grain: one row per table
fields:
  - name: "accounts.csv"
    type: "one row per account"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "140 rows. A reference table: you join TO it, you rarely aggregate it. Summing anything here is almost always a mistake."
  - name: "opportunities.csv"
    type: "one row per opportunity"
    role: "fact"
    note: "677 rows. Amount lives here, once per deal. This is the grain at which revenue is correct."
  - name: "opportunity_products.csv"
    type: "one row per opportunity per product"
    role: "fact"
    note: "1,378 rows — roughly two per opportunity. Amount ALSO appears in the parent. Join these two carelessly and Namilio's $58M becomes $114M."
  - name: "usage_monthly.csv"
    type: "one row per month per account per product"
    role: "fact"
    note: "2,616 rows. Three dimensions in the grain. Aggregate over any of them without saying which and the answer is meaningless rather than wrong."
  - name: "arr_snapshots.csv"
    type: "one row per month per account"
    role: "fact"
    note: "1,579 rows. The same account appears eighteen times, once per month, each row a statement about that month. Summing ARR across all rows produces eighteen years of revenue."
  - name: "platform_health_daily.csv"
    type: "one row per day per product"
    role: "fact"
    note: "3,244 rows, and NO account key. It cannot be joined to an account at all, which is the point: it answers 'was it us' about the platform, not about a customer."
---
::

## Fan-out, the expensive one

Two tables, a one-to-many relationship, and a sum. It happens in five minutes and survives review because every individual number looks reasonable.

::funnel-viz
---
stages:
  - label: "Opportunities (correct grain)"
    value: 677
    note: "Amount appears once per deal — $58M total"
  - label: "After joining product lines"
    value: 1378
    note: "Each deal's Amount now repeats on every product row"
  - label: "Reported revenue"
    value: 1378
    note: "≈ $114M. Nothing errored, nothing warned."
caption: "Namilio, joined at the wrong grain"
scale: linear
---
::

The fix is not clever: **aggregate the many side before you join it, or do not carry the parent's measures down.** Section 3 builds it both ways and section 7 makes it a habit. What matters here is recognising the symptom — a total that is roughly 2×, 3×, or a suspiciously round multiple of what you expected.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-search"}
**The tell.** If a number doubles when you add a grouping, the grouping is not causing the problem — it is *revealing* it. The duplication was already there; the grouping just made the duplicate rows visible in separate buckets.
::

## Dimensions, measures, dates

CRM Analytics types every field as one of three things, and the type decides what the field can do:

- **Dimension** — something you group or filter by. Text, usually. `Segment`, `Region`, `StageName`, and every ID.
- **Measure** — something you aggregate. Numeric. `Amount`, `Quantity`, `UsedUnits`.
- **Date** — a date, typed as one, which is what makes date filters, month groupings and trend lines possible.

::note{icon="i-lucide-alert-circle"}
**Two typing mistakes cost real time.** A date loaded as text looks fine in a table and cannot be trended, filtered by range, or grouped by month — and fixing it means reloading. A numeric-looking ID typed as a measure means the product will happily offer to sum `AccountId`, and eventually somebody accepts the offer. Force IDs to dimensions at load time; you will never want their sum.
::

## A dataset is a decision, not a container

Because a dataset is built rather than queried, someone chooses its grain, its joins, its filters and its fields. Those choices decide which questions are answerable on it and which are not — and unlike a warehouse, you cannot escape them with a clever query later.
