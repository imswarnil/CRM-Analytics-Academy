# The Three Layers

CRM Analytics is three products that share a login. Almost every confusion a newcomer has comes from applying one layer's thinking to another, and the entire curriculum is organised along these lines.

## Layer 1 — Data

**What it owns:** where data comes from, what shape it ends up in, and what every number means.

Connections reach external systems. Data sync keeps a local copy of selected Salesforce objects and fields current. Recipes transform — join, filter, compute, aggregate — and write a **dataset**, which is the materialised thing everything above it reads.

::note{icon="i-lucide-anchor"}
**The layer's real deliverable is not the dataset. It is the definition.** "Qualified pipeline" has to mean one thing across every dashboard, or the weekly meeting becomes an argument about whose number is right instead of what to do about it. That is why the GTM engineering section spends a whole lesson on metric contracts, and why the data layer — not the dashboard — is where a metric is settled.
::

**How this layer fails:** the wrong grain. A join that fans out, silently doubling revenue. A date field loaded as text so nothing can be trended. A dataset built per-dashboard, so two dashboards disagree and both are defensible.

## Layer 2 — Design

**What it owns:** turning a dataset into a screen somebody makes a decision from.

A **lens** is a single exploration of a single dataset — disposable, fast, a workspace. A **dashboard** assembles many queries into one faceted screen for other people. An **app** is the container that holds them and carries the sharing.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-target"}
**The design layer's question is never "what could I show".** It is "what will change on Monday because of this screen". A dashboard that supports no decision is decoration, however handsome — which is why every build in this course starts with a brief naming its audience and its decision, and lists what was deliberately left off.
::

**How this layer fails:** completeness as a goal. Nine charts because nine were requested, no faceting so the filters lie, conditional formatting that colours everything so nothing stands out, and a load time of twelve seconds because every widget has its own query when four could share one.

## Layer 3 — Intelligence

**What it owns:** predictions, and — inseparably — their explanations.

Einstein Discovery takes a dataset and an outcome column, finds what correlates with the outcome, and produces a **story**: the factors, their direction, their size. A story can be deployed as a **model** that scores records and writes the score back onto the Salesforce record, so the prediction appears where the work happens.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-scale"}
**A score with no reason attached cannot be acted on, and should not be shipped.** "This opportunity is 23% likely to close" tells a rep nothing they can do. "…and the three factors pulling it down are no economic buyer identified, 47 days since last activity, and a discount above 30%" is a to-do list. Einstein Discovery produces both; deploying only the first is the most common way this layer gets distrusted and switched off.
::

The Einstein Discovery section makes this concrete in an uncomfortable way: it audits the Academy's existing lead score and finds it predicts far less than everyone assumed. Learning to run that audit matters more than learning to click *Create Story*.

## The two handoffs, which is where it actually goes wrong

---
dataset: the seams between layers

  - name: "Data → Design"
    type: "the grain contract"
    role: "key"
    note: "The designer has to know what one row of the dataset IS. Hand over a dataset at the wrong grain and every widget built on it is wrong in a way that looks plausible: sums double, averages of averages appear, and the dashboard reviews fine because nobody knows what a row was supposed to be."
  - name: "Data → Design"
    type: "the definition contract"
    role: "key"
    note: "If the metric is not defined upstream, each dashboard defines it locally, and two screens disagree. Neither is wrong, which is worse than one being wrong."
  - name: "Design → Intelligence"
    type: "the outcome column"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "A model is only as good as the outcome it is trained to predict. 'Closed Won' sounds obvious until you notice it includes renewals that were never at risk, which teaches the model that the best predictor of winning is already being a customer."
  - name: "Intelligence → Design"
    type: "the explanation"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "The score and its top factors travel together or neither should travel. A bare probability on a record page gets ignored within a month."

## Why the course is ordered this way

Bottom-up, because every layer depends on the one below it:

- **Sections 2–3** build the data layer: access, then grain, joins and recipes.
- **Sections 4–6** build the design layer: exploring, designing, sharing.
- **Sections 7–10** do all three at once on real go-to-market problems, which is what the job looks like.

The intelligence layer is introduced early — you meet it in this section and use it in the Einstein Discovery section — but it is deliberately never the first thing. A model trained on a dataset at the wrong grain is a confident wrong answer, and confident wrong answers are the most expensive output an analytics team can produce.
