# What CRM Analytics Is

One sentence, and the rest of this section unpacks it:

::note{icon="i-lucide-quote"}
**CRM Analytics is a materialised, columnar analytics engine that runs inside Salesforce and inherits its security model.**
::

Every word there is load-bearing. Take them in turn.

## "Materialised"

A dataset is not a view over your Salesforce objects. It is a **copy**, built at a point in time and rebuilt on a schedule.

That single fact explains most of what surprises newcomers: why a dashboard can show yesterday's number, why fixing a record in Salesforce does not fix the chart, why "just make it live" is not a setting, and why *when did this last run* is the first question to ask about any wrong number.

## "Columnar"

Data is stored by column rather than by row, which is why a dashboard can aggregate tens of millions of rows in a second or two. It is also why CRM Analytics is bad at what row stores are good at: retrieving one complete record. If your screen is "show me everything about this one account", that is a Salesforce page, not a dashboard.

## "Inside Salesforce"

The data is already there. No extract to a warehouse, no nightly export job, no separate identity system, no second set of user accounts to deprovision when someone leaves.

This is a smaller technical claim than it sounds and a much larger organisational one. A significant fraction of every BI project is spent on getting data out, keeping credentials in sync, and reconciling who is allowed to see what. Starting inside the CRM deletes that work rather than doing it faster.

## "Inherits its security model" — the real argument

This is the one that decides tool choice, and it is the part most comparisons skip.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-shield-check"}
**Your Salesforce org already knows, in detail, who is allowed to see which records** — roles, the hierarchy, sharing rules, territories, teams. That model took years to get right and it is enforced on every record. CRM Analytics can apply it to analytics through a security predicate and a sharing inheritance setting, rather than making you rebuild it as row-level filters in a second tool where it will drift.
::

Rebuilding that model in an external BI tool is not a one-time cost. It is a permanent reconciliation problem: every territory change, every new sharing rule, every reorganisation has to be mirrored, and nobody notices when it is not until a regional director sees another region's pipeline.

Section 2 covers exactly how this is wired, because it is also where projects stall.

## And the four it is not

Knowing this makes you more credible, not less:

- **Real-time operations.** Queue depth, live call routing, a shipping tracker. CRM Analytics refreshes on a schedule. A sub-minute requirement is an operational need and belongs on a Salesforce page, a platform event, or a purpose-built screen.
- **The single-record view.** Columnar storage is the wrong shape for "show me this one account's full history". That is a record page.
- **A general-purpose warehouse.** If most of your data lives outside Salesforce and only a slice is CRM, you are building a warehouse with a CRM attached, and a warehouse plus a BI tool will serve you better.
- **Pixel-perfect documents.** Invoices, statements, paginated regulatory reports. Wrong tool entirely.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**The most expensive mistake in this space** is choosing CRM Analytics because the data is in Salesforce, when the actual requirement was real-time. Nothing about the product will fix that, and it usually surfaces three months in, after the dashboards are built. Ask "how stale can this be" in the first conversation and write the answer down.
::

## The names, once

Wave → Einstein Analytics → Tableau CRM → **CRM Analytics**. Same product. Old documentation, Stack Exchange answers and a few corners of the UI still use the older names; this course uses the current one and mentions the old ones only where you would actually meet them.

Note that **Tableau** and **Tableau CRM** were never the same product. Tableau is the standalone acquisition; Tableau CRM was this product wearing that name for a while. Conflating them is a common interview stumble.
