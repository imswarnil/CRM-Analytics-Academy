interview:
  - q: "What is CRM Analytics, in one or two sentences?"
    a: "A materialised, columnar analytics engine that runs inside Salesforce and inherits its security model. You build datasets — snapshots rebuilt on a schedule — and explore them in lenses and dashboards, with Einstein Discovery adding prediction on top. The security inheritance is the part that distinguishes it from a general BI tool: who is allowed to see which records is already solved rather than rebuilt."
  - q: "How is it different from a Salesforce report?"
    a: "A report queries the live database, is limited in how many objects it can span, and slows on large volumes. A dataset is a pre-built snapshot that can join many objects and external sources at a chosen grain, aggregates tens of millions of rows quickly, and can hold history the object itself no longer has. The trade is currency: a report is always now, a dataset is as current as its last run."
  - q: "What does 'grain' mean and why do you care?"
    a: "The grain is what exactly one row represents. I care because almost every wrong number in analytics is a grain problem. If I join opportunities to opportunity line items and sum the opportunity amount, the amount repeats on every line and total revenue roughly doubles — nothing errors, and the number looks plausible. So the first thing I ask about any dataset is what one row is, and I aggregate the many side before joining rather than after."
  - q: "Walk me through how data gets into a dataset."
    a: "Five stages. A connection defines the source. Data sync pulls selected objects and selected fields into CRM Analytics' own store on a schedule. A recipe transforms them — joins, filters, formulas, aggregations — and writes a dataset. The dataset is materialised and columnar, and lenses and dashboards read it. A schedule drives sync and recipes. The subtle failure is scheduling a recipe before the sync it depends on has finished: it succeeds every night using yesterday's data, and never errors."
  - q: "A user says the dashboard is missing records they know exist. Where do you look?"
    a: "Missing rather than duplicated points upstream, and to permissions rather than to the dashboard. First the integration user: sync runs as that user, so an object or field it cannot see never arrives, silently. Then the security predicate and sharing inheritance, which filter what each viewer sees. Then the recipe, for an inner join that dropped unmatched records or a filter that is narrower than intended. The dashboard is last — it usually reports faithfully what it was given."
  - q: "What is the difference between a lens and a dashboard?"
    a: "A lens is one exploration of one dataset — a grouping, a measure, a chart — and it is a disposable workspace. A dashboard assembles many queries into one screen, facets them together, and is built for other people. Practically: I build in lenses and I ship dashboards."
  - q: "Recipes or dataflows?"
    a: "Recipes for anything new. They are the current tool, visual, with a preview at every node, so a grain problem localises in about a minute. Dataflows are the JSON predecessor — still supported and common in older orgs, so I can read and maintain one, but I would not start one."
  - q: "When would you tell a stakeholder CRM Analytics is the wrong tool?"
    a: "Five cases. Genuine real-time needs, because it refreshes on a schedule. A single-record view, because columnar storage is the wrong shape for that. Pixel-perfect documents. A one-off question, where a report is free and instant. And when most of the data lives outside Salesforce — at that point you are running a warehouse project with a CRM attached, and a warehouse with a BI tool on it will serve better. The question I ask early is what decision this drives and how stale it can be, because that surfaces the real-time case in week one instead of month three."
  - q: "What does Einstein Discovery actually produce?"
    a: "A story is the analysis: what correlates with an outcome, in what direction, how strongly. A model is a deployed story that scores records and can write the score back onto a Salesforce record. The important part is that the prediction and its top factors travel together — a bare probability on a record page gives a rep nothing to act on and gets ignored within a month."
  - q: "How would you sanity-check a dashboard you just built?"
    a: "Against a number I can verify independently: a known total, a finance figure, a count someone else already published. Then I check the grain of every join, look at whether any total is a suspiciously round multiple of what I expected, and confirm what the refresh timestamp says. And I check it as a user with restricted access, because a dashboard that is correct for me and empty for the audience is a failure I would rather find before they do."

# Foundations: Interview Questions

Answer each one out loud before reading the model answer. The gap between recognising a good answer and producing one under pressure is real, and only the second one gets you hired.

::note{icon="i-lucide-mic"}
**How these are marked, in practice.** An interviewer is listening for three things: whether you know what the product *is* rather than which buttons it has, whether you reach for grain and permissions when something is wrong, and whether you can say "wrong tool" when it is. Feature recall is the least interesting thing you can offer — they can look features up.
::

## The three sentences worth memorising

1. **"A dataset is materialised, so the first question about a wrong number is when it last ran."**
2. **"The grain is what one row represents, and almost every wrong number is a grain problem."**
3. **"Rows missing means permissions; rows duplicated means a join."**

Those three carry a surprising amount of a technical screen, because each one demonstrates a habit rather than a fact.
