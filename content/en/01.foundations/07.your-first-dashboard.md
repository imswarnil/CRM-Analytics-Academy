# Your First Dashboard

One question, three widgets, about forty minutes. Build it in your own org — the walkthrough above is the script, and this page is the reasoning behind it.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "Account Base Overview"
audience: "You, and in a real org a VP of Sales wanting a Monday-morning orientation"
question: "Which segments hold our ARR, and is any of it concentrated in too few accounts?"
decision: "Whether a segment needs a named-account retention plan before a renewal cycle starts"
cadence: "Monthly — the account base does not move faster than that"
success: "A viewer can name the largest segment and the single largest account in under ten seconds"
datasets:
  - "accounts.csv"
notInScope:
  - "Trends over time. accounts.csv is a snapshot with no history — showing a trend would require inventing one"
  - "Pipeline and opportunities. That is the pipeline section, and mixing sold revenue with unsold pipeline on one screen is the most common way an exec dashboard misleads"
  - "Health scores. accounts.csv carries one, and it is not audited until the data preparation section — putting an unaudited score on a dashboard is how it becomes trusted"
---
::

## Why this question

Because it has a decision attached, and because the answer might be uncomfortable — which is what makes it worth building.

::note{icon="i-lucide-help-circle"}
**"Show me the account base" is not a question.** It has no answer, no decision, and no way to tell when the dashboard is finished. "Which segments hold our ARR, and is any of it concentrated?" can be answered, can be wrong, and tells you exactly which three widgets you need. Every build in this course starts this way, and the brief above is the form it takes.
::

## The three widgets, and why only three

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "The headline"
    icon: "i-lucide-hash"
    description: "Total ARR as one number. It sets the scale for everything under it: without it, a bar of $18M means nothing, because the reader does not know whether that is most of the business or a rounding error."
  - title: "The breakdown"
    icon: "i-lucide-bar-chart-3"
    description: "ARR by segment, sorted by the measure. This answers the first half of the question directly, and it is the widget people will actually click."
  - title: "The concentration"
    icon: "i-lucide-table-2"
    description: "Top ten accounts with their segment. Answers the second half: if the top three accounts are a third of a segment, that is the finding, and it is invisible in the bar chart."
---
::

Three widgets is not a beginner's limit. It is what the question needs. Adding a donut of accounts by region, a count of contacts and a map would all be *true* and none of them would serve the question — they would make the screen busier and the answer slower to find.

## The metrics, written down

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Total ARR"
    formula: "sum(ARR) over all accounts in the dataset"
    grain: "one value for the whole account base"
    source: "accounts.csv"
    watchout: "accounts.csv includes non-paying accounts — Self-Serve trials and churned logos with ARR of zero. They do not inflate the sum, but they do deflate every average you compute later, so decide now whether 'the account base' means all 140 or the 108 paying ones, and put the answer in the dashboard's title."
  - name: "ARR by Segment"
    formula: "sum(ARR) grouped by Segment, ordered descending"
    grain: "one value per segment"
    source: "accounts.csv"
    watchout: "Sort by the measure, not the label. CRM Analytics defaults to sorting by the grouping, which puts your segments in alphabetical order and hides the ranking that is the entire point of the chart."
  - name: "Account concentration"
    formula: "top 10 accounts by ARR, with Segment, ordered descending"
    grain: "one row per account"
    source: "accounts.csv"
    watchout: "A table is the right widget here precisely because the account NAME matters — a bar chart of ten account names is unreadable, and this is one of the few cases where a table beats a chart outright."
---
::

## What you just learned without noticing

- **A lens is a workspace.** You changed the measure, added a grouping, changed the sort, and read the query. None of that was committed to anything until you chose to save.
- **Faceting is the default between widgets on one dataset.** One click filtered the whole screen. Section 5 covers controlling it.
- **A saved lens can seed a widget**, so exploration and dashboard-building are the same activity at different levels of commitment.
- **Checking against a known total is a habit, not a step.** It is the only reason you can trust anything you build later, when no README exists.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-repeat-2"}
**Do it again tomorrow, with the lesson closed.** Rebuilding this from the brief alone, without the walkthrough, takes fifteen minutes and is worth more than reading the next three lessons. Recognition is not possession.
::
