# Choosing a Chart

Start from the sentence the reader should be able to say after four seconds. The chart type follows from it almost mechanically.

## The decision path

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Is the x-axis time?"
    description: "If yes, you are showing a trend: line for a continuous measure, column for discrete periods people count (deals closed per month). Keep time in time order — this is the one axis you never sort by value."
  - title: "Is it one number against a reference?"
    description: "A KPI tile with a comparison, or a gauge if the reference is a hard target. Never a single-bar chart: a bar with nothing beside it communicates less than the number written plainly."
  - title: "Are you ranking categories?"
    description: "Horizontal bars, sorted by value, limited to the top N with the limit in the title. This is the workhorse and it is right far more often than the alternatives people reach for."
  - title: "Is it composition — parts of a whole?"
    description: "Two or three parts: a stacked bar. Four or more, or when the exact values matter: a table with a share column. A donut only when there are genuinely two or three slices and the shape itself is the message."
  - title: "Is it two measures against each other?"
    description: "Scatter. One measure per axis, one point per entity. This is the only family that answers 'do these move together', and it is badly under-used."
  - title: "Is it a spread rather than a total?"
    description: "A distribution view — bands, a histogram-style column chart. Any time you are about to report an average, ask whether the shape is the finding."
  - title: "Is it a process with stages?"
    description: "A funnel if the stages are sequential and shrinking, a waterfall if things are added and subtracted to reach an end value."
---
::

## The three questions that settle arguments

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "\"What comparison is this making?\""
    subtitle: "Ask it first, every time"
    icon: "i-lucide-help-circle"
    pros:
      - "Turns chart choice from taste into a requirement"
      - "Usually eliminates all but two candidates immediately"
      - "Exposes the charts that are making no comparison at all"
    cons:
      - "Occasionally reveals that the widget has no purpose, which is awkward and useful"
    verdict: "The single most useful question in dashboard design."
  - title: "\"What would a reader conclude in four seconds?\""
    subtitle: "Ask it after building"
    icon: "i-lucide-timer"
    pros:
      - "Catches the true-number-wrong-conclusion failure"
      - "Catches sorting, scale and colour mistakes at once"
    cons:
      - "Needs somebody who has not seen it, to answer honestly"
    verdict: "Ask a colleague. Ten seconds of their time."
  - title: "\"Would a table be better?\""
    subtitle: "Ask it when unsure"
    icon: "i-lucide-table-2"
    pros:
      - "Often yes — when identity and exact values matter"
      - "Instantly settles the ten-slice donut argument"
    cons:
      - "A table of forty rows is a data export, not an answer"
    verdict: "Honest, and unfashionable."
---
::

## The default that is right too often

**A horizontal bar chart, sorted descending, limited to the top ten, with the limit in the title.**

::tip{icon="i-lucide-bar-chart-3"}
**If you cannot decide, build that and move on.** It answers the most common question (which is biggest), it is readable at any size, it survives greyscale and a projector, and it is very hard to misread. Reach for something else when you have a reason — a trend, a relationship, a composition — not because the dashboard has too many bars on it. A dashboard of bars that each answer a real question beats a varied one where three widgets exist for visual interest.
::

## What CRM Analytics gives you

The chart list is long, and the useful subset is short. In practice, the families in this section cover essentially all production dashboards:

---
dataset: the working set
grain: one row per family
fields:
  - name: "Bar / column"
    type: "rank, and discrete time"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "The most used, and the one whose sort order is most often wrong."
  - name: "Line / area"
    type: "trend"
    role: "date"
    note: "Continuous change over time. Area only when the filled quantity is meaningful — a stacked area of unrelated series is decoration."
  - name: "Donut / stacked / treemap"

  - name: "Number / gauge"

    role: "measure"
    note: "The headline of most dashboards. Needs a comparison to mean anything."
