# Funnels and Waterfalls

Two charts where the **layout carries the meaning**. Reorder either one and it stops being the same claim.

## Funnels

Sequential stages, each a subset of the one before. Sessions → leads → qualified → opportunities → closed won. The drop between adjacent stages is the finding.

::note{icon="i-lucide-arrow-down-narrow-wide"}
**Process order, never size order.** A funnel sorted by size is just a bar chart, and it destroys the one thing a funnel does: putting adjacent stages next to each other so the drop between them is visible without arithmetic. This is the most consequential sort setting in the course after the bar chart's.
::

### The scale problem

Real funnels span orders of magnitude, and that breaks the linear version:

::funnel-viz
---
stages:
  - label: "Web sessions"
    value: 4932
    note: "The top of the funnel dwarfs everything under it"
  - label: "Leads"
    value: 2800
    note: "Still legible"
  - label: "Qualified"
    value: 1120
    note: "Shrinking fast"
  - label: "Opportunities"
    value: 608
    note: "On a linear scale this is a stub"
  - label: "Closed won"
    value: 275
    note: "And this is nearly invisible"
caption: "The Academy, log scale — every stage stays readable"
scale: log
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-ruler"}
**A log scale is honest here provided you say so.** On a linear axis, a funnel from hundreds of thousands to hundreds renders the last four stages as identical slivers — and those four are where the selling happens. Log keeps them all legible. What makes it honest rather than sneaky is labelling the axis as logarithmic and putting the conversion percentages on the chart, so nobody reads bar length as a ratio.
::

### The rate between stages is the point

The absolute counts are context; the **conversion between adjacent stages** is what somebody acts on. Put the percentage between each pair, and be explicit about which denominator you used — step-to-step, or each stage against the top. They are different numbers and both are called "conversion rate".

::field-table
---
dataset: funnel conventions
grain: one row per decision
fields:
  - name: "Step conversion"
    type: "stage ÷ previous stage"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Diagnostic: where is the leak. This is the one a team acts on week to week."
  - name: "Cumulative conversion"
    type: "stage ÷ top of funnel"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Strategic: what fraction of demand becomes revenue. Useful for planning and useless for finding the leak."
  - name: "Cohorting"
    type: "by entry date"
    role: "date"
    note: "Every stage must count the SAME cohort, tracked forward. A funnel where each stage counts whatever happened in the period is not a funnel — it mixes people who entered at different times and the conversion rates mean nothing."
---
::

## Waterfalls

A bridge: start at one total, add and subtract, arrive at another. The Academy's ARR waterfall is the canonical case — opening ARR, plus new, plus expansion, minus contraction, minus churn, equals closing.

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "The bridge"
    icon: "i-lucide-waves"
    description: "Each bar floats at the level the previous one left off, so the eye follows the running total. This is what makes it different from a stacked column of the same numbers."
  - title: "Signs matter visually"
    icon: "i-lucide-plus-minus"
    description: "Additions and subtractions need to read as opposite at a glance — direction and colour together, never colour alone."
  - title: "Start and end are anchors"
    icon: "i-lucide-anchor"
    description: "Opening and closing bars sit on the baseline, not floating. Without those anchors the chart is a set of deltas with no scale."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-calculator"}
**Check the arithmetic on one period before trusting the chart.** Opening plus the movements should equal closing exactly. If it does not, either a movement is missing from the data or a filter is excluding rows — and a waterfall that does not reconcile is the single most embarrassing chart to present, because the reader can do the addition in their head.
::

CRM Analytics has no dedicated waterfall widget in every release, so this is often built as a stacked column with an invisible base series, or as a compare table with the running total computed. Either is fine; what matters is that the bars float and the anchors sit on the baseline.
