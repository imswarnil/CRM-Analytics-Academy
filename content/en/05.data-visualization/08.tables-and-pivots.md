# Tables and Pivot Tables

The most under-rated widget in analytics, and the one most often used badly.

## Charts show shape; tables show identity

A chart answers "how much" and "which is bigger". A table answers "which one, exactly, and what else do I know about it".

::note{icon="i-lucide-list"}
**When the answer is a list of things somebody will act on, it is a table.** "Which ten accounts are at consumption risk" needs names, ARR, utilisation, trend and an owner — five attributes per row. A chart can encode two of those. The table is not a fallback here; it is the correct widget, and dressing the data up as a bar chart of ten account names actively makes it harder to use.
::

## The scrollbar test

::warning{icon="i-lucide-scroll"}
**If a table needs scrolling, it has stopped being a dashboard widget.** A dashboard is glanced at; an export is worked through. Both are legitimate needs and they are not the same object. Keep the widget to what fits — ten rows, five columns — and provide a download beside it for the full list. A 140-row scrolling table on a dashboard gets used by nobody: too long to scan, too awkward to work in.
::

## Formatting that decides readability

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Right-align numbers, consistent decimals"
    description: "Digits line up, magnitudes become comparable without reading. Mixed precision in one column reads as carelessness and slows every comparison."
  - title: "Sort by what matters, descending"
    description: "Almost never alphabetical. For a risk list, sort by the ARR at stake rather than by how bad the percentage is — otherwise the list is ordered by severity of a ratio, which is rarely the order anyone should work in."
  - title: "Colour the exceptions only"
    description: "Three red cells in a table of forty is instantly readable. Forty coloured cells is a decorated table with no signal left in it."
  - title: "Label the limit in the title"
    description: "'Top 10 accounts at risk' rather than 'Accounts at risk'. Otherwise somebody sums the column and reports the total as wrong."
  - title: "Give it an empty state"
    description: "An exception table is empty when things are going well — which is the ideal state, and looks like a broken widget without a line of text saying so."
---
::

## Pivot tables, and when they stop working

A pivot is at its best with a small matrix: five segments by six months, or four products by five regions. Beyond roughly eight columns the reader is scrolling horizontally and comparing across a distance, which is exactly what a chart does better.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-shrink"}
**If a pivot is too wide, you are asking two questions.** Split it: one widget for the trend over time, one for the breakdown by category. Two readable widgets beat one complete one, and the split usually reveals that only one of the two questions actually mattered.
::
