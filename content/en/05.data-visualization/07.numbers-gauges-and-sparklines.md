# Numbers, Gauges and Sparklines

Most dashboards open with a single value. Most single values are not actionable, and the fix costs nothing.

## A number alone means very little

"Net new ARR: $1.42M" — is that good? The reader cannot know, so they either ask you or assume. Three ingredients fix it:

::lesson-kpis
---
columns: 3
items:
  - label: "Bare number"
    value: "$1.42M"
    hint: "Cannot be acted on — no scale, no direction"
  - label: "With a comparison"
    value: "$1.42M"
    delta: "+8.3% vs plan"
    trend: "up"
    good: true
    hint: "Now it has a verdict attached"
  - label: "With a sparkline too"
    value: "$1.42M"
    delta: "+8.3% vs plan"
    trend: "up"
    good: true
    hint: "And now the reader knows whether it is normal"
    spark: [820, 910, 1040, 980, 1180, 1310, 1420]
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-gauge"}
**Number, comparison, direction.** The comparison gives scale — against plan, against last period, against the same period last year. The direction gives a verdict. Say which comparison it is, in the label: "up 8%" against three different references gives three different answers, and they frequently disagree in sign.
::

## Goodness belongs to the metric

::warning{icon="i-lucide-arrow-down"}
**Down is not always bad.** Churn falling, CAC payback shortening, time-to-close dropping, cases past SLA declining, discount rate falling — all good news. A default palette that paints every decrease red turns a strong quarter into a wall of alarm and teaches the reader to ignore colour. Set the direction of goodness per metric, and sanity-check it by asking: *if this number halved, would I be pleased?*
::

## Sparklines

A tiny chart inside the tile, showing the last several periods. It answers "is this normal?", which is the question a single number always provokes and can never answer.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-activity"}
**Add sparklines before you add another chart.** They cost almost no space and no attention, and they convert every KPI from a snapshot into a trend. 112% NRR means little; 112% at the end of a line that has climbed for seven straight months means something specific — and the same 112% at the end of a line that fell from 121% means something urgent.
::

## Gauges

A gauge shows one value against a scale, usually with coloured bands. It earns its space in exactly one situation: **a genuine fixed target where "how close are we" is the question**.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Good gauge"
    subtitle: "A real target exists"
    icon: "i-lucide-target"
    pros:
      - "Pipeline coverage against 3x — the target is the point"
      - "SLA attainment against a contractual 95%"
      - "Bands come from the business, not from thirds of the range"
    cons:
      - "Uses much more space than the number plus a delta"
    verdict: "One per dashboard at most."
  - title: "Bad gauge"
    subtitle: "A number wearing a dial"
    icon: "i-lucide-circle-slash"
    pros:
      - "Looks like a cockpit"
    cons:
      - "No meaningful maximum, so the scale is invented"
      - "Bands split evenly into thirds, which is arithmetic pretending to be judgement"
      - "Occupies four times the space of the KPI tile it replaced"
    verdict: "Use a number with a comparison."
---
::

## Formatting the number itself

::field-table
---
dataset: number formatting
grain: one row per rule
fields:
  - name: "Abbreviate on tiles"
    type: "$1.42M not $1,420,000"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Nobody decides on the last four digits of a headline. Keep full precision in the table underneath for whoever needs it."
  - name: "Round to the decision"
    type: "one decimal on rates"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Six decimal places on a conversion rate implies a confidence the data does not have. Whole currency for totals, one decimal for rates."
  - name: "Tabular figures"
    type: "in any column"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Monospaced digits so a column of numbers lines up at the decimal point and magnitudes are comparable at a glance."
  - name: "State the period"
    type: "in the label"
    role: "date"
    note: "'Net New ARR — Aug FY26, vs plan'. The most common misreading of a dashboard is about which period a headline covers, and it is free to prevent."
---
::
