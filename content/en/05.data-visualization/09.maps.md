# Maps

A map is the right chart when **position on the earth is part of the message**. It is the wrong one — and it is chosen anyway — whenever the geography is merely a label.

## The test

::note{icon="i-lucide-map-pin"}
**Would the reader's conclusion change if the regions were relabelled A, B, C?** If yes, geography is doing real work: adjacency, distance, territory boundaries, travel, coverage gaps. If no, geography is just a category name, and a sorted bar chart ranks those categories far more precisely than a colour scale ever will.
::

The Academy has four regions: AMER, EMEA, APAC, LATAM. Four categories. A sorted bar chart answers "which region is biggest, and by how much" exactly. A map makes the reader compare two shades of blue on shapes of wildly different size, and it takes four times the space to do it worse.

## The population problem

::warning{icon="i-lucide-users"}
**A choropleth of any raw total is mostly a population map.** Colour the United States, Germany and India by revenue and the biggest economies light up, which you knew before opening the dashboard. Almost every business map needs normalising: revenue per account, penetration against addressable market, growth rate rather than level. The unnormalised version is not wrong — it just answers a question nobody asked.
::

There is a second, quieter version of the same problem: **area is not value**. A large sparsely-populated region occupies far more pixels than a small dense one, so the eye weights it more heavily no matter what colour it is.

## Practical rules

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Normalise before colouring"
    description: "Per account, per capita, per opportunity — some denominator that removes size. Say which in the title, because a normalised map and a raw one look identical."
  - title: "Use a sequential scale, and say what the ends mean"
    description: "One hue, light to dark, with the range labelled. A red-to-green scale on a map compounds the colour-vision problem with an area problem."
  - title: "Pair it with a ranked list"
    description: "The map for the pattern, a small sorted table beside it for the actual order. Readers get the shape and the precision, and nobody has to estimate a shade."
  - title: "Check it at the size it will be viewed"
    description: "A map that works full-screen in Studio is often unreadable as a quarter-width widget inside the Analytics tab."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-bar-chart-3"}
**The honest default for regional business data is a sorted bar chart.** Reach for a map when somebody needs to see *where*, not when the dimension happens to have place names in it. This is one of the few chart decisions where the more impressive-looking option is usually the worse one.
::
