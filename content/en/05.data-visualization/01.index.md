## What this section will cover

Every chart makes a claim about the data before the reader has read a single number — what that claim is, how the wrong chart type makes a true number lie, and what this section covers.

## Planned lessons

- **Charts are arguments** — Every chart makes a claim about the data before the reader has read a single number — what that claim is, how the wrong chart type makes a true number lie, and what this section covers.
- **Choosing a chart** — A decision path from the comparison a reader must make to the chart that makes it — plus the three questions that settle most arguments about chart type, and the default that is right more often than it should be.
- **Bar & column** — The workhorse of every dashboard: when to use horizontal versus vertical, why sorting is the most consequential setting on the widget, and how to choose between grouped and stacked.
- **Line & area** — Trends over time: how many series is too many, what a gap in the data should look like, when a non-zero baseline is honest, and why a dual axis invents relationships that are not there.
- **Part of whole** — Donuts, pies, stacked bars and treemaps — what composition charts are genuinely good at, the slice count where they stop working, and why a sorted table with a share column usually wins.
- **Scatter & distribution** — The two comparisons an average destroys — whether two measures move together, and what the spread actually looks like — with the Academy's utilisation as the worked example.
- **Numbers & gauges** — The single-value family: what turns a number into something actionable, when a gauge earns its space, and why a sparkline is worth more than a second chart.
- **Tables & pivots** — When the right answer is not a chart: values tables, compare tables and pivots, the scrollbar that means you have built an export, and the formatting that makes a table readable.
- **Maps** — Geographic charts: when position on the earth genuinely carries the message, why a map is usually a worse bar chart, and the population problem that makes most business maps misleading.
- **Funnels & waterfalls** — Two charts where the layout is the argument: sequential stages that shrink, and a bridge from one total to another — plus the log-scale decision that makes a real funnel readable.
- **Chart honesty** — The formatting decisions that change what a reader concludes without changing a single value — baselines, scales, aggregation, colour and the missing denominator — and the review checklist that catches them.
- **Interview questions** — Chart choice, honesty and the reasoning behind both — with model answers, and the distinction between a candidate with taste and one with a method.
- **Graded quiz** — Ten questions on chart choice, each family's strengths and limits, and the formatting decisions that change a reader's conclusion.
