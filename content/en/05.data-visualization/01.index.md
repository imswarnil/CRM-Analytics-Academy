# Charts Are Arguments

A chart is not a neutral container for numbers. Before anyone reads a value, the *shape* has already made a claim: these things are comparable, this one is bigger, this went up, this is part of that.

::note{icon="i-lucide-message-square-quote"}
**Pick the chart from the comparison, not from the data type.** "I have a measure and a dimension, so — bar chart" is how most charts get chosen, and it is why so many dashboards are a wall of bars. The question is what comparison the reader is supposed to make: *rank*, *trend*, *composition*, *distribution*, *relationship*, or *a single value against a target*. Each has a right answer and several wrong ones.
::

## How a true number lies

Nothing in this section is about making charts prettier. It is about the specific ways a correct number gets misread:

::field-table
---
dataset: common misreadings
grain: one row per failure
fields:
  - name: "A bar chart sorted alphabetically"
    type: "hides the ranking"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Readers assume bars are ordered by size. When they are not, the eye reads the first bar as the biggest anyway, and nobody double-checks."
  - name: "A line chart with a non-zero baseline"
    type: "exaggerates change"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Legitimate for a trend where small movements matter, and dishonest when the reader assumes zero. Say which you did in the axis label."
  - name: "A donut with nine slices"
    type: "shows nothing"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Humans compare angles badly past about four slices. Nine slices is a table wearing a circle."
  - name: "An average with no distribution"
    type: "hides the shape"
    role: "measure"
    note: "The Academy's company utilisation of 88% is the average of accounts at 30% and accounts at 140%. Both need action, in opposite directions, and the average shows neither."
  - name: "A dual axis"
    type: "invents a relationship"
    role: "key"
    note: "Two measures on two scales cross wherever you choose to put the axes. The crossing point is a formatting decision that readers interpret as a finding."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-eye"}
**One habit for the whole section.** After building any chart, ask: *what would somebody conclude from this in four seconds, and is that conclusion true?* Not "is the number right" — the number is nearly always right. The conclusion is the thing that goes wrong.
