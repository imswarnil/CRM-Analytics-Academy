# Drill-Down and Navigation

Navigation is what lets a dashboard be small. Build 14's executive board survives on nine numbers precisely because everything else is one click away.

::note{icon="i-lucide-scissors"}
**Every "can we also add…" has two answers.** Add the widget, or link to the dashboard that already has it. The second keeps the screen readable and costs nothing — and it is available far more often than people reach for it, because most requested widgets already exist somewhere.
::

## Three destinations

  - name: "A filtered lens"
    type: "for the curious"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "For people who want to explore rather than act. Useful, and not the default — most viewers want either more detail or the record."
---

## Pass the context

::warning{icon="i-lucide-link-2-off"}
**A drill that does not carry its filters is worse than no drill.** The viewer clicked EMEA on a summary, landed on a detail dashboard showing every region, and now has to reproduce the selection by hand. They will do it once, be mildly annoyed, and not click again. Passing the selection through is the entire mechanism — if it is not wired, leave the link out.
::

## Designing the path

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Name the three questions a viewer asks next"
    description: "From the exec board: which segment, which deals, which accounts at risk. Three destinations, three links. More than about four and the dashboard is a menu rather than an answer."
  - title: "Make the link look like a link"
    description: "A clickable table row that gives no sign of being clickable gets clicked by the person who built it and nobody else. An icon in the row, or a clear affordance, is the whole difference."
  - title: "Keep the destination shallow"
    description: "Summary to detail to record is a comfortable depth. Four levels and people lose track of where they are and how to get back."
  - title: "Check the destination's own security"
    description: "A viewer may be able to open the summary and not the detail, or reach a record page they cannot see. Test the whole path as a restricted user, not just the first screen."
---
::

## The back problem

Web navigation makes going back easy; dashboard navigation frequently does not — especially when the drill opened a new context.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-corner-up-left"}
**Give a detail dashboard a visible way home.** A link back to the summary it is usually reached from. It sounds trivially obvious and is missing from most dashboard sets, and its absence is why people reach for the browser's back button and end up somewhere unexpected.
::

## When not to link

If the destination would be a dashboard with one widget on it, put the widget on the source screen instead. Navigation is for keeping *substantial* detail off a summary — not for splitting a screen that was already the right size.
