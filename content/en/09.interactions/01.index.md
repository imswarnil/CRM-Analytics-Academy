# Interaction Is a Design Decision

A static dashboard answers the questions you thought of. An interactive one answers the follow-ups — and the follow-ups are where the value is.

::note{icon="i-lucide-mouse-pointer-click"}
**Nearly nobody will build a lens. Nearly everybody will click a bar.** That asymmetry is the whole argument for taking interaction seriously: it is the only self-service most of your audience will ever use. If clicking anything on your dashboard produces a sensible narrowing, the dashboard answers a hundred questions you never anticipated. If clicking does nothing, or does something surprising, it answers the six you built.
::

## The four mechanisms

::field-table
---
dataset: how a dashboard responds
grain: one row per mechanism

    type: "automatic, same dataset"
    role: "key"
    note: "Widgets built on one dataset filter each other by default. The mechanism that makes a dashboard feel alive, and the one most often left entirely on defaults."
  - name: "Filter widgets"
    type: "explicit controls"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Month, segment, region along the top. Deliberate, discoverable, and the thing people look for when they do not realise they could click the chart."
  - name: "Selection state"
    type: "what is chosen at open"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Initial selection decides what everybody sees first. A dashboard that opens unfiltered and enormous makes every viewer do the same three clicks every morning."
  - name: "Navigation"
    type: "between screens"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Links from a summary to a detail dashboard, or to a Salesforce record. The escape hatch that lets a dashboard stay small."
---
::

## The two failures

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Everything facets everything"
    subtitle: "The default, unexamined"
    icon: "i-lucide-shuffle"
    pros:
      - "Feels responsive"
      - "No configuration"
    cons:
      - "The reference widget moves, so the comparison it exists for disappears"
      - "Widgets at a different grain narrow in ways the data cannot support"
      - "A stray click leaves the whole screen filtered with no obvious way back"
    verdict: "Decide per widget, not per dashboard."
  - title: "Nothing facets anything"
    subtitle: "Widgets on separate datasets, unwired"
    icon: "i-lucide-unlink"
    pros:
      - "Predictable"
    cons:
      - "It is a page of pictures — the reader must re-ask every question with the filters"
      - "Half-wired is worse: four widgets narrow, three keep showing the global figure, and every number is individually correct"
    verdict: "Check every widget against a filter before shipping."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Half-wired is the dangerous state.** A viewer filters to EMEA, four widgets narrow and three do not, and the screen as a whole now says something false while every individual widget is right. Nobody reports this, because nothing looks broken. Testing it takes thirty seconds: apply a filter and check that every widget either changed or has a deliberate reason not to.
::
