# Selection and Initial State

Two questions most dashboards never answer deliberately: what does this show before anyone touches it, and what happens after they do.

## The opening state

::note{icon="i-lucide-door-open"}
**Default to the common case, not to everything.** A dashboard that opens on the current quarter, the viewer's own region and all segments is immediately useful. One that opens unfiltered and company-wide is technically complete and makes every viewer perform the same narrowing every single morning — a cost they pay silently and resent slowly.
::

Three things to set deliberately:

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "The period"
    description: "Current quarter or current month, almost always — and exclude or clearly mark the partial current period, because a dashboard that shows an incomplete month beside complete ones manufactures a decline every month."
  - title: "The scope"
    description: "All regions is right for an exec board and wrong for a regional team's screen. If one dashboard serves both, a saved view per audience is cheaper than two dashboards."
  - title: "The sort and limit"
    description: "Top ten by value, not all 140 alphabetically. The opening state should already be the answer to the most common question."
---
::

## Selection, once they click

::warning{icon="i-lucide-eye"}
**A filtered dashboard that does not say it is filtered is the single most common way a screenshot misleads.** Someone clicks EMEA, screenshots the screen, and the number becomes company-wide in a message thread by Thursday. Bind the current selection into the widget titles — this is the first and best use of a binding — and make sure clearing the selection is obvious, because a viewer who cannot get back to the full picture will reload the page and lose everything else too.
::

## Saved views

A **view** remembers a state of a dashboard — filters, selections, a date range — so somebody returns to their slice without re-clicking it.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-bookmark"}
**Suggest a saved view explicitly when you hand over a dashboard.** Most people never discover the feature. For anyone whose slice is always the same — a regional director, a product owner — it removes a daily cost entirely and materially raises how often the dashboard gets opened. It is the cheapest adoption win in this course.
::

## Selection that spans datasets

Faceting is automatic only between widgets on the same dataset. When a dashboard mixes datasets — Namilio's pipeline alongside its usage data — a click on one side does nothing to the other unless you wire it.

Two honest options:

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Wire it with a binding"
    subtitle: "One selection drives both"
    icon: "i-lucide-link"
    pros:
      - "The dashboard behaves as one thing"
      - "Right when the two datasets share a real key, like AccountId"
    cons:
      - "Logic in widget configuration that nobody can read at a glance"
      - "Needs documenting, or the next person rebuilds the dashboard"
    verdict: "Worth it when the key is genuine."
  - title: "Separate the two visually"
    subtitle: "And say they are separate"
    icon: "i-lucide-columns-2"
    pros:
      - "No hidden logic, nothing to misread"
      - "Honest when the datasets genuinely do not share a key"
    cons:
      - "The reader has to filter twice"
    verdict: "Correct for Namilio's platform health, which has no account key at all."
---
::

## The clear-everything problem

A viewer clicks three things, gets somewhere confusing, and wants to start again. If the only route back is a page reload, they will reload — and lose their saved view, their scroll position and their place.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-rotate-ccw"}
**Give every dashboard an obvious reset.** A single visible control that returns it to its opening state. It costs one widget and it is the difference between a screen people explore confidently and one they poke at cautiously.
::
