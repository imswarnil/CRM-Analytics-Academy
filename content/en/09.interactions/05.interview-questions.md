interview:
  - q: "What is faceting and when would you turn it off?"
    a: "Widgets built on the same dataset filter each other by default, which is what makes a dashboard a system rather than a page of pictures — most people will never build a lens, but nearly everyone clicks a bar. I turn it off for a reference widget: a company total or a quarter target that must stay fixed while everything else filters, because if it narrows with the selection the comparison it exists for disappears exactly when it becomes useful. Also for widgets at a different grain — our platform health data has no account key, so faceting it by an account selection would imply a relationship the data cannot support."
  - q: "What is the failure mode you check for before shipping?"
    a: "Half-wired faceting. A viewer filters to one region, four widgets narrow and three keep showing the global figure, and the screen as a whole is now false while every individual widget is correct. Nobody reports it because nothing looks broken. The test is thirty seconds: apply a filter and confirm every widget either changed or has a deliberate reason not to."
  - q: "How do you decide what a dashboard shows when it opens?"
    a: "Default to the common case rather than to everything. Current period, the viewer's own scope, sorted and limited to the top few — so the opening state is already the answer to the most common question. A dashboard that opens unfiltered and company-wide makes every viewer perform the same three clicks every morning, which is a cost they pay silently. And I exclude or clearly mark the partial current period, because showing an incomplete month beside complete ones manufactures a decline every month."
  - q: "Why does a filtered dashboard need to say so?"
    a: "Because it will be screenshotted. Someone clicks one region, pastes the screen into a message, and the number becomes company-wide in somebody else's argument by Thursday. Binding the current selection into the widget titles is the cheapest fix in dashboard design and it is the first binding I add to anything."
  - q: "When do you use navigation rather than another widget?"
    a: "Whenever the request is for substantial detail. Every 'can we also add' has two answers — add it, or link to the dashboard that already has it — and the second keeps the screen readable. Our executive board survives on nine numbers precisely because the other hundred-odd widgets are one click away. What I do not do is link to a destination with one widget on it; that should just be on the source screen."
  - q: "What makes a drill-down bad?"
    a: "Not passing the context. The viewer selected a segment, landed on a detail dashboard showing everything, and has to reproduce the selection by hand. They do that once and then stop clicking. If the filters are not carried through, I would rather leave the link out. I also give detail dashboards a visible way home — people otherwise use the browser back button and end up somewhere unexpected."
  - q: "How do you handle a dashboard that mixes datasets?"
    a: "Either wire the selection across with a binding, when the two genuinely share a key like AccountId, or separate them visually and say they are separate. What I avoid is the middle ground where some widgets respond and some do not without any signal. For data with no shared key — platform health against accounts — separating them is the honest choice, because wiring it would imply a link that does not exist."

# Interactions: Interview Questions

Interaction questions look like feature questions and are really design questions. The strong answers are about what should *not* respond.

::note{icon="i-lucide-star"}
**The answer that distinguishes experience** is half-wired faceting. It is the failure that reaches production most often, it produces a false screen out of correct widgets, and describing the thirty-second test for it shows you have shipped something and been caught by it.
::

## The three sentences worth memorising

1. **"Same-dataset widgets facet by default; the reference widget must not."**
2. **"If a dashboard is filtered, its titles must say so — it will be screenshotted."**
3. **"A drill that does not carry its context gets clicked once."**
