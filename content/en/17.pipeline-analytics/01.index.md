# Pipeline Analytics

From here on, every row has a name attached. That changes the work: demand analytics was about estimating, and pipeline analytics is about **accounting**. The numbers can be reconciled, so they will be — usually in a meeting, usually by someone who disagrees with them.

::note{icon="i-lucide-users"}
**This module is where you meet the politics.** Marketing's MQL number and sales' accepted number sit on either side of one handoff, and both teams are measured on it. Every dashboard here touches somebody's compensation.
::

## Three rules that apply to everything in this module

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "Filter by Type, always"
    icon: "i-lucide-filter"
    description: "Renewals win ~88% and New Business ~33%. A blended win rate is a statement about your renewal book, not about selling. Almost every chart here filters to New Business or splits by Type."
  - title: "Filter by Segment, always"
    icon: "i-lucide-layers"
    description: "Self-Serve closes in days for $4K; Strategic takes six months for $1.4M. Averaging them produces numbers describing no deal that exists."
  - title: "Use the stage's own date"
    icon: "i-lucide-calendar-clock"
    description: "MQLs by MQLDate, pipeline by CreatedDate, revenue by CloseDate. Mixing them is the single most common cause of two teams reporting different numbers."
---
::
