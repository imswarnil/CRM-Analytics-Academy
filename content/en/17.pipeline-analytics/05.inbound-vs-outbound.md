# Build: Inbound vs Outbound

Inbound produces more leads. Outbound produces bigger deals. Both sides are right, both cite volume, and the argument never resolves because nobody brings the right chart.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "Motion Comparison"
audience: "CRO, VP Marketing, VP Sales Development"
cadence: "Quarterly, at planning"
question: "Per dollar and per rep hour, which motion produces revenue — and in which segment?"
decision: "The split of SDR headcount and demand-gen budget for next quarter."
success: "The planning argument is about the chart rather than about anecdotes; headcount follows the segment where each motion wins."
datasets: [leads.csv, opportunities.csv, campaigns.csv, marketing_spend.csv]
notInScope: ["Individual rep performance", "Brand measurement"]
---
::

## Define the motions before you compare them

The whole analysis is worthless if the buckets are wrong, and the classification is genuinely contestable.

::field-table
---
dataset: leads.csv — SourceCategory
grain: four motions
fields:
  - name: "Inbound Marketing"
    type: "paid, organic, webinar, conference"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "They came to us because of something we published or paid for."
  - name: "Product"
    type: "free course, community, self-serve"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Separate from inbound on purpose — PLG behaves completely differently and blending it into 'inbound' is the most common classification error."
  - name: "Outbound"
    type: "SDR sequences, cold"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "We initiated contact. No prior interest expressed."
  - name: "Partner / Referral"
    type: "SI partners, customer referral"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Small volume, high conversion. Usually gets ignored because it is not owned by either team."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-split"}
**Split Product out of Inbound.** A learner who worked through three sections of the free course is not the same as someone who filled in a webinar form. Lumped together, PLG's excellent conversion hides inbound marketing's mediocre conversion, and the budget argument gets decided on a blended number that flatters the loser.
::

## The finding that matters

::note{icon="i-lucide-crosshair"}
**Neither motion wins overall. Each wins a segment.** The heat map in band 3 is the deliverable: Product dominates Self-Serve and SMB, Outbound dominates Enterprise and Strategic, and Inbound Marketing sits in the middle doing useful work in Mid-Market. Presented as a company-wide winner, this analysis is wrong whichever answer it gives.
::

## Two objections to pre-empt

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "\"Outbound leads were already warm\""
    icon: "i-lucide-thermometer"
    pros:
      - "Often true — they'd seen content first"
      - "FirstTouchChannel proves or disproves it"
    cons:
      - "Undermines outbound's apparent efficiency"
    verdict: "Check it. Filter outbound leads by FirstTouchChannel and see how many had a prior marketing touch — this is the strongest card marketing holds."
  - title: "\"Inbound would dry up without brand spend\""
    icon: "i-lucide-sprout"
    pros:
      - "Also probably true"
      - "Unprovable on this data"
    cons:
      - "Can justify any budget indefinitely"
    verdict: "Acknowledge it as unmeasured rather than dismissing it. Then hold the line that unmeasured is not the same as proven."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-handshake"}
**Bring both objections yourself.** Presenting a motion comparison without them makes you look like you are carrying one team's brief. Raising them unprompted makes you the neutral party, which is the only position from which this dashboard changes a decision.
::
