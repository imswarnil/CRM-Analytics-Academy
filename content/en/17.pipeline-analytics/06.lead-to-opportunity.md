## Reconcile before you analyse

The first job is making both numbers appear on one screen with their definitions attached. Do this and half the conflict evaporates.

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "MQL → SQL Acceptance Rate"
    formula: "count(IsSQL = true) / count(IsMQL = true)"
    grain: "Per month by MQLDate, per source, per persona"
    source: "lead_funnel"
    watchout: "Both sides must use MQLDate for the cohort, or marketing counts an MQL in March that sales accepts in April and the monthly rates never tie. State the cohorting rule on the dashboard face."
  - name: "Time in Handoff"
    formula: "SQLDate − MQLDate"
    grain: "Per lead"
    source: "lead_funnel"
    watchout: "Averages around 13 days at the Academy, which is long enough to lose a deal. A long tail here often means leads are being accepted in a batch at month end rather than worked as they arrive — check the distribution, not the mean."
  - name: "Rejection Rate by Source"
    formula: "count(IsMQL and not IsSQL) / count(IsMQL)"
    grain: "Per source"
    source: "lead_funnel"
    watchout: "High rejection concentrated in one source is a targeting finding. Rejection spread evenly across all sources is a definition problem — the MQL threshold is simply set too low."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-scan"}
**That last distinction is the whole diagnosis.** Concentrated rejection means fix the channel. Uniform rejection means fix the definition. One chart — rejection rate by source, sorted — tells you which conversation to have, and it is almost never the conversation either team came in expecting.
::

## The four reasons sales rejects an MQL

Give the rejection a taxonomy, or it stays an opinion.

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Wrong fit"
    icon: "i-lucide-puzzle"
    description: "Company size, geography, use case. A real targeting problem and the easiest to act on — it shows up as one source dominating the rejection chart."
  - title: "Too early"
    icon: "i-lucide-clock"
    description: "Genuine interest, no timeline. Should go to nurture, not be rejected. At the Academy these show up as Status = Nurture, and counting them as failures understates marketing's work."
  - title: "No capacity"
    icon: "i-lucide-user-x"
    description: "The lead was fine, nobody had time. This is the one nobody admits to, and the tell is rejection rate correlating with leads-per-SDR rather than with source."
  - title: "Definition mismatch"
    icon: "i-lucide-ruler"
    description: "Marketing's MQL bar is lower than sales' acceptance bar. Uniform rejection across every source. Fixed by changing the threshold, not by blaming anyone."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Watch for the third one.** If rejection rate rises in the same weeks that leads-per-SDR rises, the leads were not worse — the team was busier. That is a capacity finding disguised as a quality complaint, and the routing dashboard from lesson 3 is where it gets confirmed.
::

## Post-acceptance validation

One more chart, and it is the one that ends the argument for good.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Take accepted leads only"
    description: "Filter to IsSQL = true."
  - title: "Group by the ORIGINAL score band"
    description: "The band assigned before sales touched it."
  - title: "Measure win rate on the resulting opportunities"
    description: "Join through ConvertedOpportunityId to the opportunity's IsWon."
  - title: "Read the result honestly"
    description: "If A-band leads still win better after sales has filtered, marketing's scoring adds information beyond sales' judgement. If the bands flatten out, sales' filter has already captured everything the score knew — which means the argument about MQL volume was never the real issue."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-trophy"}
**This is the most valuable chart in the pipeline section.** It reframes the debate from "are these leads good" — which is unfalsifiable and personal — to "does the score add information after the human filter", which is measurable and impersonal. It is also the analysis most likely to get you invited back to the meeting.
::
