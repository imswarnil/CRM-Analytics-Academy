# Build: Funnel Conversion and Sales Velocity

Opportunity management asks "which deals". This asks "how well does the machine work, and where is the constraint".

::note{icon="i-lucide-gauge"}
**The counter-intuitive finding you should expect.** The stage with the worst conversion rate is usually not the one costing you the most revenue. The expensive stage is the one with the worst conversion *and* the most value flowing through it, and only a value-weighted funnel shows that.
::

::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-calendar-x"}
**The incomplete-cohort trap.** An Enterprise cohort created two months ago cannot have closed — the cycle is 97 days. Include it and late-stage conversion appears to collapse. Grey out or exclude cohorts younger than one cycle length **per segment**, since 28 days for SMB and 186 for Strategic are wildly different windows.
::

## The velocity equation

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Sales Velocity"
    formula: "(qualified opportunities × avg deal size × win rate) / avg cycle length in days"
    grain: "Per segment, per quarter"
    source: "opp_enriched"
    watchout: "Revenue per day. Its value is not the number — it is that it has exactly four levers, so 'we need more pipeline' becomes a choice between four measurable actions. Never compute it blended across segments: dividing Strategic's deal size by Self-Serve's cycle produces a number describing nothing."
---
::

## Build it

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Build the count funnel"
    description: "For one created-cohort, count opportunities that ever reached each stage. Note 'ever reached' — a deal now in Negotiation passed through Discovery, and counting only current stage understates every early stage."
  - title: "Build the value funnel beside it"
    description: "Same cohort, sum of Amount instead of count. Compare the two shapes. A stage that loses 30% of deals but only 10% of value is losing small deals, which may be fine."
  - title: "Median days per stage"
    description: "For closed deals, the time between entering each stage and the next. Median, not mean — one 400-day deal distorts the average and hides a healthy process."
  - title: "Compute velocity per segment"
    description: "The four inputs, per segment, per quarter. Display the components beside the result so the reader can see which lever moved."
  - title: "The heat map last"
    description: "Stage × segment, coloured by conversion. This is where you find that Enterprise loses deals in Business Case while SMB loses them in Discovery — two different problems that a blended funnel averages into one meaningless number."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-lightbulb"}
**Discovery versus Business Case is the lesson.** Discovery loses the most deals and Business Case loses the most money, because the deals reaching Business Case are larger and better qualified. A team optimising Discovery is working on volume; a team optimising Business Case is working on revenue. The value funnel is the only chart that distinguishes them, and most companies have never built it.
::
