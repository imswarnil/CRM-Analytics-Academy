interview:
  - q: "What have you automated in CRM Analytics?"
    a: "Mostly data loads and reconciliation. A nightly push of data that has no connector through the external data API, and a check that compares the dataset's total against the source system afterwards. The reconciliation is the part I would keep if I could only keep one — it turns 'check your work against something independently true' from a habit into a job that runs whether anyone remembers it or not."
  - q: "Does the API bypass the security model?"
    a: "No. Requests authenticate as a user and that user's permissions, sharing and row-level security apply exactly as in the interface. It is worth saying plainly because 'use the API' occasionally gets offered as a workaround for an access problem, and it is not one. It is also the first thing I check when a script's number differs from a dashboard's: usually they are running as different users and both numbers are right."
  - q: "Describe the external data upload flow."
    a: "Three steps. Declare a job with the dataset name, the operation and the field metadata; upload the rows as numbered parts; then signal completion, which is when processing actually begins. The failure everyone hits once is forgetting the third step — every part uploads successfully, nothing errors, and no dataset appears, because an un-signalled job is indistinguishable from one still in progress."
  - q: "Overwrite or incremental?"
    a: "Overwrite until volume makes it wrong. It is self-correcting — a bad run is fixed by the next good one — needs no key and cannot drift. Incremental upsert is necessary at scale and it drifts silently when a change is missed: nothing errors, the dataset is just slightly wrong indefinitely. So if I go incremental I add a periodic full reconciliation, otherwise I have traded a visible cost for an invisible one."
  - q: "What would you not automate?"
    a: "Anything that happens twice a year — the script rots, the credentials expire, and the annual task becomes debugging something nobody has looked at in twelve months instead of an hour of clicking. And design work: generating forty dashboards from a template gives you forty consistently mediocre screens rather than one that is right. I automate what is frequent or must be identical every time."
  - q: "How do you handle credentials?"
    a: "A dedicated integration user with narrow permissions, never a person's account — a person leaves, their account is deactivated, and every job authenticating as them stops on a day nobody predicted. Credentials from environment variables sourced from a secret store, never in the script or the repository, and rotation that somebody owns."
  - q: "A scheduled job fails overnight. What matters most in how you built it?"
    a: "Whether anybody sees it. A job that fails into a log directory nobody opens is a job that is not running, and you find out weeks later from a dashboard. So: non-zero exit so the scheduler knows, an alert into a channel the team already watches, retry with backoff for transient errors, and resumability for long loads so a failure at part 40 of 60 costs minutes rather than the whole run."
  - q: "How do you give dashboards a change history?"
    a: "Export the metadata on a schedule and commit it to a repository, one file per dashboard. The diff is the product: 'the pipeline dashboard changed on the 14th and here are the four lines that moved' answers the question people actually ask, which is why a number is different from last week. It is a record rather than a deployment pipeline — restoring from it should be a deliberate act, because conflating backup with deployment is how an automation overwrites somebody's afternoon."

# APIs and Automation: Interview Questions

These questions are operational rather than technical. Anyone can call an endpoint; the interviewer is checking whether you have run something on a schedule and been woken up by it.

::note{icon="i-lucide-star"}
**The answer that signals experience** is the one about failure being *seen*. Candidates describe retries and error handling; far fewer say that a job failing into an unread log is indistinguishable from a job that is not running. That sentence only comes from having found out the slow way.
::

## The three sentences worth memorising

1. **"The API authenticates as a user, so security applies exactly as in the UI."**
2. **"Declare, upload in parts, signal completion — and the third step is the one that gets forgotten."**
3. **"Overwrite until volume makes it wrong; incremental drifts silently."**
