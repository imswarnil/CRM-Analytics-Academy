interview:
  - q: "A model reports 97% accuracy. Are you impressed?"
    a: "Not until I see the baseline and class balance. With rare positives, accuracy is meaningless; I look at precision, recall at the chosen threshold, AUC, and whether one variable explains suspiciously much."
  - q: "How do you decide whether a story is worth deploying?"
    a: "It must clearly beat the baseline, use only variables known at prediction time, have stable top factors that make business sense, pass a bias check, and have a user who will act on the score."

# Reading a Model Honestly

Never judge a model without its baseline.

::lesson-hero{figure="pipeline"}
---
points:
  - "Compare every metric with the baseline"
  - "Read the confusion matrix"
  - "Trade precision against recall"
  - "Know when not to deploy"
---
::

| | **Won** | **Lost** |
|---|---|---|
| **Predicted win** | ✓ Hit | ✗ False alarm |
| **Predicted lose** | ✗ Miss | ✓ Correct rejection |

| Metric | Question |
|---|---|
| **Baseline** | What if we always guess the common answer? |
| **Precision** | Of the predicted wins, how many won? |
| **Recall** | Of the wins, how many did we predict? |
| **AUC** | How well does it rank deals? (0.5 coin, 1.0 perfect) |

**Don't deploy** when it barely beats the baseline, one variable explains everything, or outcomes are too few.
