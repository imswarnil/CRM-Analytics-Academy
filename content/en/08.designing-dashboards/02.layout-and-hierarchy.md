# Layout and Hierarchy

A dashboard's layout is an argument about what matters. Readers follow it whether or not you meant to make one.

## Reading order

Left to right, top to bottom, in most of the languages this course is published in — and right to left in Arabic and Urdu, which the site handles by mirroring. The important consequence is constant: **the top-left corner is the most valuable space on the screen and should hold the headline.**

::warning{icon="i-lucide-move"}
**The most common layout mistake is putting filters in the top-left.** They are the first thing the builder configures, so they end up first on the screen, and the most valuable space on the dashboard is spent on controls nobody needs until their second visit. Filters belong along the top edge as a strip, or in a narrow left rail — present, unmissable, and not occupying the corner where the answer should be.
::

## Proximity means relationship

Widgets placed close together are read as related. Space between groups says "these are different things". This happens automatically, so the only question is whether it happens on purpose.

::lesson-steps

  - title: "Group by question, not by chart type"
    description: "Everything answering 'how is demand doing' together; everything answering 'how is pipeline doing' together. Grouping all the bar charts in one row because they are bar charts is organising for the builder rather than the reader."
  - title: "Leave real space between groups"
    description: "A gap is a sentence break. Without it, eight widgets read as one undifferentiated wall and the reader has to do the grouping themselves, every time."
  - title: "Let the most important thing be bigger"
    description: "Size is the clearest signal available. If everything is the same size, nothing is important — and the reader picks their own priority, which is usually not yours."

## Titles do more work than any widget

A widget title is the cheapest clarity available and the most commonly wasted:

- **"Revenue"** — which revenue, over what period, compared to what?
- **"Closed-won revenue, FY26 Q3, vs. same quarter last year"** — no ambiguity, no follow-up question, no meeting spent establishing what the chart shows.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-type"}
**Put the period, the filter and the comparison in the title.** Including a limit: "Top 10 accounts by ARR" rather than "Accounts by ARR". It costs a few words and removes a whole class of misreading — including the one where somebody sums the visible bars and reports that the total is wrong.
::

## Blank space is not waste

A dashboard with room to breathe reads faster than one packed to every edge. The instinct to fill space comes from a feeling that empty pixels are unused — but the reader's attention, not the pixel, is the scarce resource, and every extra widget spends some of it.
