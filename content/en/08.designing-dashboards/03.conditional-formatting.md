# Conditional Formatting

Colour is the strongest signal on a dashboard and the easiest to spend. This lesson is mostly about spending it slowly.

## Thresholds come from the business

A red/amber/green scale split into even thirds of the range is arithmetic pretending to be judgement. The threshold has to be the number at which somebody does something differently.

::note{icon="i-lucide-ruler"}
**Namilio's utilisation bands are a worked example.** Under 55% an account is under-consuming and at genuine churn risk — that is a red with an action attached, because someone calls the customer. Over 105% it is in overage, which is revenue and also a renewal conversation. Between them is healthy. Those numbers came from how the business behaves, not from dividing a range into three, and they are written down in the RevOps section alongside the metric definitions for exactly that reason.
::

If nobody can tell you the threshold, that is useful information: the metric may not be actionable yet, and colouring it implies a certainty that does not exist.

## Colour alone is never enough

::warning{icon="i-lucide-eye"}
**Roughly one in twelve men has a colour vision deficiency, and red-green is the common one** — which is precisely the pair every analytics tool reaches for first. A dashboard that signals good and bad only by colour is unreadable to them, and it degrades the same way in a greyscale print, a projector with poor contrast, or a screenshot pasted into a document. Every coloured delta in this course carries a direction arrow as well, and that is not decoration; it is the actual signal, with colour as reinforcement.
::

The rule: **colour plus one other channel.** An arrow, a plus or minus sign, a label, a position. Colour is the fastest of the channels, and it should never be the only one.

## One more trap

::tip{icon="i-lucide-arrow-down"}
**Down is not always bad.** Churn falling, cases falling, time-to-close falling, discount falling — all good news, and a default palette that paints every decrease red will label a good quarter as a crisis. Set the direction of goodness per metric rather than accepting the default, and check it by asking "if this number halved, would I be pleased?"
::
