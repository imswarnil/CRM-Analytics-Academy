# What a Story Is

You give it a dataset and name the column you care about — won or lost, churned or retained, converted or not. It searches for patterns in the other columns that separate the outcomes, and reports them ranked by strength.

## What the output actually says

A story gives you factors, each with a **direction** (pushes towards or away from the outcome), a **magnitude**, and the **segments** where it applies most strongly.

::note{icon="i-lucide-quote"}
**The claim is associative, and the phrasing invites you to forget that.** "Deals with no economic buyer identified are 34% less likely to close" is a statement about what co-occurred in your historical data. It does not establish that identifying an economic buyer *causes* deals to close — it is at least as plausible that deals already going well are the ones where someone bothered to fill the field in. The story is a map of where to look, not a set of instructions.
::

## Three kinds of factor

::field-table
---
dataset: reading a factor
grain: one row per kind
fields:
  - name: "Actionable"
    type: "you can change it"
    role: "key"
    note: "Days since last activity, discount level, whether a vendor review has started. These are the factors worth putting in front of a rep, because there is a next action attached."
  - name: "Descriptive"
    type: "true and unchangeable"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Segment, industry, company size, region. Real signal and nothing to do about it — useful for targeting and territory design, useless on a record page as advice."
  - name: "Suspicious"
    type: "too good"
    role: "measure"
    note: "A factor that predicts almost perfectly is usually leakage: something recorded at or after the outcome. 'Has a signed contract date' predicts closed-won with stunning accuracy and tells you nothing at all."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**The strongest factor is often the least useful.** Stories rank by strength, so the top of the list is frequently something descriptive or leaked rather than something a person can act on. Read the whole list, and judge each factor by whether a next action exists — a rep does not need to be told that Enterprise deals close better than Self-Serve ones.
::

## What a story is good at

      - "It will happily rank factors as if they were a plan"
    cons:
      - "It cannot distinguish cause from correlation"
      - "It does not know which fields are filled in only when things go well"
      - "It has no idea what your team is able to change"
    verdict: "Human judgement, informed by the story."
---

## Small data makes confident nonsense

The Academy has 608 opportunities. That is enough for a story and nowhere near enough for confidence about narrow segments.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-microscope"}
**Check how many rows sit behind a factor before believing it.** "Deals in LATAM with a technical evaluation close 60% more often" is a finding when it rests on four hundred deals and a coincidence when it rests on nine. A story will report both with the same tone of voice, and the segment sizes are the only thing that tells them apart.
::
