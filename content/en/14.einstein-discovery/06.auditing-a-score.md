# Auditing a Score

The Academy's leads carry a `LeadScore` and its accounts carry a `HealthScore`. Both are trusted. Neither has been tested. This lesson is that test, and it is the most transferable hour in the section — every company you work at will have a score like this.

::note{icon="i-lucide-search-check"}
**A score is a claim: higher means more likely.** That is testable against what actually happened, and the test takes under an hour. The reason it is so rarely run is not difficulty — it is that the score arrives with a vendor or a departed admin, it has been on the dashboard for two years, and questioning it feels like questioning the people who use it.
::

## The method

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Write down the claim"
    description: "'Leads scoring above 70 convert more often than leads below 40.' Specific enough to be wrong."
  - title: "Check the distribution first"
    description: "If most records sit in two bands, the score has little resolution to offer regardless of how well it predicts."
  - title: "Join the outcome"
    description: "Conversion for leads; contraction or churn for accounts. The outcome must be something that happened AFTER the score was assigned, or you are measuring leakage."
  - title: "Plot the rate by band, with volumes"
    description: "Climbing steadily means it works. Flat means it does not. Climbing then flattening means it separates bad from average and says nothing about the top — which is where the attention goes."
  - title: "Compare against a trivial baseline"
    description: "Lead source, segment, company size — a field you already had. If the simple thing separates outcomes as well, the score adds nothing."
  - title: "Write the paragraph"
    description: "What it claims, what the test showed, what you recommend. Keep, keep-with-caveat, or replace."
---
::

## The three results, and what to do with each

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "It predicts"
    subtitle: "The line climbs"
    icon: "i-lucide-trending-up"
    pros:
      - "Keep it, and now you can say how strongly rather than hoping"
      - "The audit becomes a periodic check for decay"
    cons:
      - "Still worth confirming it beats the trivial baseline"
    verdict: "Good outcome. Re-run it in six months."
  - title: "It predicts at the bottom only"
    subtitle: "Climbs, then flattens"
    icon: "i-lucide-chart-no-axes-combined"
    pros:
      - "Genuinely useful for filtering out the worst"
      - "Honest framing: a disqualifier, not a prioritiser"
    cons:
      - "It is being used to rank the top, where it has no signal"
      - "That is exactly where the sales team spends its time"
    verdict: "The most common result, and the most useful finding."
  - title: "It does not predict"
    subtitle: "Flat line"
    icon: "i-lucide-minus"
    pros:
      - "Clear result, cheaply obtained"
    cons:
      - "Somebody built it, and people have been routing work with it"
      - "The conversation is political, not analytical"
    verdict: "Report it plainly, with the chart. The chart does the arguing."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-users"}
**The uncomfortable part is not the analysis.** It takes an hour. The difficulty is that a score which does not predict has usually been used to prioritise people's work for years, and saying so implicates decisions other people made. Lead with the method and the chart rather than the conclusion, offer the trivial-baseline comparison as the alternative, and let the line on the chart make the case.
::
