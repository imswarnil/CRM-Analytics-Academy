# Build: Acquisition

A visitor arrives, reads the docs, signs up, and calls the API. Three of those four are measurable in GA4 and one of them is the only one that predicts revenue.

::note{icon="i-lucide-terminal"}
**At a developer-first company the qualification event is technical, not commercial.** A pricing-page view means someone is curious. A successful first API call means someone has integrated you, and the switching cost just went up. Build the dashboard around that.
::

::lesson-steps

items:
  - title: "Find the referrers"
    description: "chatgpt.com, claude.ai, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com. The dataset's SourceMedium column already carries these as ' / referral'."
  - title: "Make a channel group for them"
    description: "In the recipe, a formula field: if SourceMedium contains any of those hosts, Channel = 'AI Assistant'. Do this in the recipe, not per dashboard, so every chart agrees."
  - title: "Expect it to be undercounted"
    description: "Some assistants strip the referrer, so a share of real AI traffic lands in Direct regardless. Say this on the dashboard — the number is a floor, not a total."
  - title: "Watch the ramp separately"
    description: "This channel is growing from a small base. On a stacked chart it is invisible; give it its own trend line or it will be ignored for another year."

## Two traps specific to this data

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "The weekend cliff"
    icon: "i-lucide-calendar-x"
    description: "Developer traffic drops ~58% on Saturdays. Any day-over-day comparison reads as a crisis every weekend. Use 7-day rolling averages, or compare Tuesday to Tuesday, and say which you did."
  - title: "Docs pages distort averages"
    icon: "i-lucide-file-code"
    description: "Docs sessions run ~210 seconds and 3.4 pages against 74 seconds and 1.9 for marketing pages. A site-wide 'average engagement' number is the ratio of your two audiences, not a measure of quality."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-split"}
**Segment by PageType before you conclude anything.** Docs, Pricing, Product, Blog, Compare and Trust behave like six different websites. A single conversion rate across them is an average of things that should never have been averaged.
::

## Where this dashboard has to stop

::note{icon="i-lucide-hand"}
`web_sessions.csv` has **no account or lead key**. It cannot tell you that this session became that customer. The bridge is a signup event carrying a channel into `leads.csv` — which is exactly what `FirstTouchChannel` is, and exactly why it is a single stored opinion rather than a fact. The attribution lesson deals with this properly.
::
