# Self-Service Exploration

Here is a scene every dashboard designer knows. You ship a dashboard. Within a week you get an email: *"This is great — could you add a version broken down by product instead of region?"* You add it. Next week: *"...and one by owner?"*

**Explore** is the feature that ends that queue.

## What Explore does

Every widget's action menu includes **Explore**. Choosing it takes the widget's query — with the dashboard's current filters and selections applied — and opens it in a **lens**: the full exploration interface, with the grouping, measure, and chart-type controls exposed.

From there, the user is no longer looking at a picture someone else designed. They're holding the query.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Open Explore on the widget"
    description: "The widget's current query and the dashboard's active filters come along with it, so the starting point is exactly what they were looking at."
  - title: "Change the question"
    description: "Swap the grouping from region to product, add a second grouping, switch the measure, change the chart type — all point-and-click in the lens."
  - title: "Read the answer"
    description: "The question they emailed you about is now answered, by them, in about thirty seconds."
  - title: "Save it as their own lens"
    description: "If it's a cut they'll want again, they save it as a lens in their own app. It becomes theirs to keep, without touching your dashboard."
---
::

That last step is the quiet win. The dashboard stays focused on the story you designed; the one-off variations live as personal lenses belonging to the people who need them. Your dashboard doesn't slowly bloat into forty charts because forty people each asked for one.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-users"}
Explore is the highest-leverage thing to demo in a user training session. Most business users have no idea their license lets them build their own analysis — they assume every new view is a request ticket. Showing them this one action changes how they use the platform.
::

## When you should turn it off

Explore isn't always the right call. Reasonable reasons to disable it:

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Curated executive dashboards"
    icon: "i-lucide-crown"
    description: "A board-level dashboard where every number is deliberately framed. Letting a viewer regroup it invites misinterpretation of metrics that only mean what they mean in context."
  - title: "Embedded and mobile surfaces"
    icon: "i-lucide-smartphone"
    description: "A dashboard embedded on a Lightning record page or built tight for mobile — dropping a user into a full lens interface there is jarring and often broken visually."
---
::

Note the trade-off is genuinely a trade-off. Turning Explore off protects the narrative, but it also puts you back on the receiving end of those emails. Default to leaving it on unless you have a reason.

## How to turn it off

Both switches are widget **properties**, set in the dashboard editor with the widget selected:

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Show widget actions"
    icon: "i-lucide-list-x"
    description: "The master switch. Uncheck it and the widget's whole action menu disappears — no Explore, no Share, no Set Notification, no Annotate."
  - title: "Show explore actions"
    icon: "i-lucide-search-x"
    description: "The targeted switch. Leaves the action menu in place but removes the exploration options, so users keep sharing and downloading without being able to regroup the query."
---
::

Reach for **show explore actions** first. Switching off widget actions entirely is a blunt instrument — it takes away notifications, annotations, and downloads at the same time, and users rarely connect the missing menu to a deliberate design decision. They just assume the dashboard is broken.

Explore lets a user build their own cut of the data. The next lesson covers how they keep and share a cut of *your* dashboard, without leaving it at all: **views**.
