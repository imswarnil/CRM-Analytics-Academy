# Onboarding Videos and the Learn Action

We've spent this section on features that push data to users. The last one pushes *understanding* to them — and it addresses the most common reason a dashboard quietly dies: nobody was ever sure what they were looking at.

## The onboarding property

Every widget has an **onboarding** property in the dashboard editor. Set it, and the widget gains a **Learn** action in its menu. A user who clicks it gets a video explaining that widget.

  - title: "Set the onboarding property on the widget"
    description: "Select the widget in the dashboard editor, find the onboarding property in the panel, and paste the video URL."
  - title: "Save and test as a viewer"
    description: "Open the widget's action menu, choose Learn, and confirm the video actually plays in the panel — not just that the action appears."
---
::

## The gotcha: it must be an embed URL

::tip{icon="i-lucide-triangle-alert"}
The URL you paste has to be a YouTube **embed** URL, not the ordinary watch link from your browser's address bar.

Get it from the video's **Share → Embed** option — that gives you the `youtube.com/embed/...` form. Pasting the normal `youtube.com/watch?v=...` link (or the `youtu.be/...` short link) results in a Learn action that opens to nothing, with no obvious error explaining why.
::

This is worth double-checking every single time, because the failure is silent. The property saves happily, the Learn action appears in the menu, and the whole thing looks configured — right up until a user clicks it and gets an empty panel. Always test the action after saving, from a viewer's perspective rather than the editor's.

The temptation is to explain the chart. Users can usually see the chart. What they can't see is everything around it:

## Why this belongs in a collaboration section

Onboarding videos are documentation that lives **where the question gets asked**. A wiki page explaining your dashboard is read by nobody; a Learn action on the widget someone is confused by right now is read by exactly the person who needs it.

Reserve it for the widgets that earn it — the calculated metrics, the ones with non-obvious business rules, the ones you've already had to explain twice. Attaching a video to a straightforward count of open cases is effort spent where nobody was confused.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-graduation-cap"}
A practical pattern: record one video per *dashboard* covering the two or three genuinely tricky widgets, attach it to each of them, and you've built a self-service help layer in about half an hour. It doesn't have to be one video per widget to be useful.
::

## Wrapping up the section

Look back over what we've covered: sharing and print, notifications, annotations, exports, explore, views, subscriptions, watchlists, and now in-dashboard help. Not one of them required a line of code, a new license, or a change to your data model. They were all sitting there the whole time.

That's the theme worth carrying out of this section. Adoption is rarely a build problem — it's a **communication** problem. The designer who spends thirty minutes walking their audience through these features will get more usage out of an average dashboard than a designer who spends thirty hours perfecting a beautiful one nobody was taught to use.
