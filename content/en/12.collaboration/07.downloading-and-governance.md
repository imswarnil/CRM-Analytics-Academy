# Downloading and Governance

Two unglamorous topics that decide whether the work survives a year.

## Downloads

People export. They export to do something the dashboard does not do, and the export is usually a signal rather than a problem.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Ask what they do with it"
    description: "Usually one of three things: a calculation the dashboard does not offer, a list to work through, or a format somebody else demands. Each has a different fix, and two of the three are things you can build."
  - title: "Build the thing they are recreating"
    description: "A weekly export that always gets the same pivot applied is a widget you have not built yet. This is the single most reliable source of genuinely wanted features."
  - title: "Give them the list properly"
    description: "If the need is to work through rows, that is legitimate. Keep the dashboard to the top ten and provide the full download beside it rather than putting 140 scrolling rows on screen."
  - title: "Accept the rest"
    description: "Some exports exist because a bank, an auditor or a board pack needs a file. That is fine — the goal is not zero exports, it is zero exports that recreate the dashboard."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-file-spreadsheet"}
**The competing-truth problem is the real risk.** An exported spreadsheet is a snapshot that never refreshes, gets edited, gets shared, and is quoted six weeks later against a dashboard that has since been corrected. Nobody is acting in bad faith and both numbers have a provenance — which makes the disagreement much harder to resolve than a simple error. Downloads are not the enemy; downloads that become somebody's permanent working copy are.
::

Row-level security applies to exports as it does to queries: people download what they can see. That is correct, and it means two people exporting the same widget can produce different files, legitimately.

## Governance, in the smallest useful form

Governance in a small org does not need a committee. It needs four things written down and one person responsible:

::field-table
---
dataset: the minimum that actually works
grain: one row per artefact
fields:
  - name: "An owner per dashboard"
    type: "a name"
    role: "key"
    note: "Someone who answers questions, maintains the definitions and decides on changes. Without this the dashboard degrades silently — the schedule drifts, a field changes meaning upstream, and trust goes long before anybody files a ticket."
  - name: "Metric definitions"
    type: "one page"
    role: "key"
    note: "Formula, grain, source, known trap — the metric-spec shape used throughout this course. It answers 'why is this different from my number', which is the first question anyone asks."
  - name: "The security note"
    type: "four lines"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Which datasets carry predicates, what each says in English, who is exempt, what the fallback is. Security configuration is unreadable as configuration."
  - name: "A deprecation habit"
    type: "quarterly"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Delete or archive dashboards nobody opens. Four pipeline dashboards with slightly different definitions is worse than one, because it guarantees an argument that no amount of correctness resolves."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-trash-2"}
**Deleting is a governance activity.** Every unused dashboard is a future argument, a maintenance cost, and a thing someone will find and trust at the wrong moment. Reviewing what nobody opens and removing it is unglamorous and it is the highest-leverage governance anybody does — far more than approval processes for new work.
::

## The ownership sentence

::note{icon="i-lucide-user-check"}
**"This dashboard is owned by X, the definitions are here, and it refreshes nightly at 03:00."** That sentence, attached to the dashboard, is most of governance. It answers who to ask, what the numbers mean and how current they are — which are the only three questions anyone actually has.
::
