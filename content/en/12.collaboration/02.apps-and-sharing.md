# Apps and Sharing

An **app** is a container with sharing attached — closer to a folder than to an application. Datasets, lenses and dashboards live inside one, and sharing the app is how people get to its contents.

That makes app design a governance decision rather than a filing preference.

## The three access levels

::field-table
---
dataset: app access
grain: one row per level
fields:
  - name: "Viewer"
    type: "see and explore"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Open dashboards, click, facet, explore, save their own personal views. What almost everyone should have. Still fully subject to row-level security on the datasets underneath."
  - name: "Editor"
    type: "change things"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Create and modify content in the app. A small list — every editor can change what everyone else sees, and can do it without telling anyone."
  - name: "Manager"
    type: "change sharing"
    role: "key"
    note: "Everything an editor can do, plus deciding who else gets in. Usually one or two people. This is the level that quietly grows if nobody is watching."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-shield-off"}
**Sharing is not security.** Giving someone Viewer access does not expose data their row-level security would hide — the predicate still applies to every query they make. The two are independent grants, and both are required: app sharing decides whether they can open the screen, row-level security decides what is on it when they do. A user with neither and a user with only one both see nothing, which is why "they can't see the dashboard" always needs both questions asked.
::

## The two-app pattern

Most orgs converge on the same shape, and it is worth starting there rather than discovering it:

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "A working app"
    subtitle: "Where things are built"
    icon: "i-lucide-hammer"
    pros:
      - "Shared narrowly — the analytics team"
      - "Half-finished work is expected here"
      - "Nobody outside sees a dashboard mid-rebuild"
    cons:
      - "Requires the discipline to actually promote things out of it"
    verdict: "Every org needs one."
  - title: "A published app"
    subtitle: "What the business sees"
    icon: "i-lucide-send"
    pros:
      - "Shared widely, with one sharing list to keep consistent"
      - "Everything in it is finished and owned"
      - "Moving something in is a deliberate act"
    cons:
      - "Somebody has to decide when a thing is ready"
    verdict: "The decision to publish is the value."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-folder-tree"}
**Avoid one app per dashboard.** It looks tidy and it means the sharing list is maintained in twenty places, which drifts within a quarter — someone joins the team and is added to eleven of the twenty. Group by audience instead: the app is the set of people who should see the same things, and the dashboards inside it follow from that.
::

## Sharing to groups, not to people

Share with a public group, a role or a territory rather than a list of names. Named-user sharing works on the day it is set up and decays every time someone joins, leaves or moves — and the decay is silent, because nobody reports the access they never had.

## What to check before you share

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Open it as a restricted user"
    description: "The same check from the access section, and the same reason: an administrator sees everything, so every security mistake is invisible to precisely the person responsible for it."
  - title: "Check both grants"
    description: "Can they open it — that is app sharing. Is what they see their slice — that is row-level security. Both can fail, and both look like a blank screen."
  - title: "Confirm the datasets travelled"
    description: "A dashboard in a shared app reading a dataset that lives elsewhere will fail for people who cannot reach that dataset. Keep a dashboard and its datasets in the same app, or verify the access explicitly."
  - title: "Say what it is for when you share it"
    description: "One sentence — the decision it supports and who owns it. A dashboard that arrives with no explanation gets opened once out of curiosity and never again."
---
