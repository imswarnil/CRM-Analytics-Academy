# Annotations

A number that surprises someone generates a question. The question is asked somewhere — and where it gets asked decides whether the answer survives.

::note{icon="i-lucide-message-circle"}
**Without annotations, the conversation happens in a thread you are not in.** Someone screenshots a widget, posts it with "this looks wrong", three people speculate, one of them is right, and the explanation lives in a channel nobody will search in four months. The next person to notice the same spike starts the same thread. Annotations put the question and the answer where the number is.
::

## What to annotate

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "One-off events"
    icon: "i-lucide-zap"
    description: "'March is high because of the platform migration — 40 accounts re-contracted in one week.' Without this, every subsequent trend chart invites the same question, forever."
  - title: "Definition changes"
    icon: "i-lucide-file-diff"
    description: "'From Q3 we count qualified at stage 2 rather than stage 3, so the conversion rate steps up here.' A step change with no note is read as a real trend, every time."
  - title: "Known gaps"
    icon: "i-lucide-circle-slash"
    description: "'Only 44% of closed cases have a survey.' Annotating the limitation beside the number is more honest — and far more effective — than a footnote nobody reads."
---
::

## What annotations are not

They are not documentation. A metric definition belongs in the recipe and in the project notes; an annotation is a *note on a moment* — this month, this widget, this anomaly.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-archive"}
**Annotations accumulate and go stale.** A note explaining a spike from two years ago is clutter once nobody remembers the event, and a dashboard carrying fifteen historical notes is harder to read than one carrying none. Clear them when they stop being relevant — that is part of owning a dashboard, and it is the part that never gets scheduled.
::

## Why this matters more than it sounds

The alternative to an annotation is not silence. It is a private explanation, given once, to one person, verbally.

That has a specific cost: **the number's credibility depends on whoever answered being available.** When they are on holiday, the dashboard is unexplainable, and an unexplainable number stops being used. Writing the explanation where the number lives is how a dashboard becomes independent of the person who built it — which is the difference between an asset and a dependency.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-repeat"}
**The rule of thumb: if you explain something twice, write it down where it happened.** Twice is the threshold at which a verbal answer has proven it will be needed again.
::
