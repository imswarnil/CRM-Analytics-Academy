# Subscriptions and Notifications

Two different mechanisms, often confused, with different failure modes.

## Subscriptions: on a schedule

A snapshot of a dashboard or widget, delivered on a rhythm. Monday morning, month-end, every day at seven.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-calendar"}
**Match the rhythm to the decision, not to the data.** A number that changes daily does not need a daily email if the decision it feeds is made monthly. Daily delivery of a monthly decision trains people to ignore the message, which means they also ignore it in the month something moved.
::

Subscriptions work best where a dashboard works worst: for people who genuinely will not open another tool. An executive who reads email and nothing else is better served by a correct number in their inbox than by a beautiful dashboard they never visit.

## Notifications: on a threshold

Fire when a condition is met — coverage falls below target, an account drops under 55% utilisation, cases past SLA exceed a count.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-bell-off"}
**A threshold that fires most days is noise, and noise is worse than silence** because it consumes the channel. People build a filter rule, the alert moves to a folder nobody opens, and the one time the threshold genuinely mattered it is in that folder too. If an alert fires more than a few times a month, either the threshold is wrong or the thing it watches is not exceptional — and in both cases the fix is upstream, not a stronger warning.
::

Set thresholds where somebody would actually do something. That is the same test as conditional formatting, and it has the same answer: if nobody can tell you the number, the metric may not be actionable yet.

## What a good subscription contains

::field-table
---
dataset: subscription content
grain: one row per element
fields:
  - name: "The number, and its comparison"
    type: "essential"
    role: "key"
    note: "A bare figure cannot be acted on. Against plan, against last period, with a direction — the same three ingredients that make a KPI tile useful."
  - name: "What changed"
    type: "high value"
    role: "measure"
    note: "The difference from the last delivery is usually the whole message. 'Coverage 3.1×, down from 3.4×' is read; 'Coverage 3.1×' is skimmed."
  - name: "A link back"
    type: "essential"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Every recipient who cares will want the detail. Without a link they ask you instead, which converts an automated message into a manual support request."
  - name: "Who owns it"
    type: "often missed"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "A name to ask. An anonymous automated email about a number people disagree with generates a thread that never reaches anyone who can resolve it."
---
::

## Security still applies

A subscription is rendered for a recipient, so row-level security applies to what they receive. That is correct behaviour and it has a consequence worth stating out loud: **two people can receive the same subscription and see different numbers**, legitimately.

::note{icon="i-lucide-users"}
**Say so in the message.** "Figures reflect your access" prevents the meeting where two people quote the same report at each other with different totals. It costs four words and it forestalls an argument that is otherwise genuinely confusing, because both parties are right.
::

## When not to use either

If the decision is made in a meeting that already happens, put the dashboard on the screen in that meeting. A subscription that arrives four days before the conversation is read once and forgotten by the time it matters — the rhythm of the delivery should match the rhythm of the decision, and sometimes the right delivery mechanism is a person opening a screen while everyone watches.
