# Getting the Work to People

Everything until now produced something correct. This section is about it being *used*, which is a different problem and usually the one that decides whether the project was worth doing.

::note{icon="i-lucide-users"}
**The failure this section prevents** is the dashboard that is accurate, fast, well-designed, and opened four times in its first month — twice by the person who built it. Nothing in the tooling reports that. It is the most common way analytics work is quietly wasted, and none of the causes are technical.
::

## The four ways work reaches people

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "They come to it"
    icon: "i-lucide-door-open"
    description: "The app is shared, they know it exists, and it is worth the trip. Works when the dashboard answers a question they already ask on a rhythm — the Monday pipeline call."
  - title: "It comes to them"
    icon: "i-lucide-mail"
    description: "Subscriptions and notifications push a number into email or Slack on a schedule or a threshold. Works for people who will never open a tool, which is most executives."
  - title: "It is already where they work"
    icon: "i-lucide-layout"
    description: "Embedded on a Salesforce record page or in the Analytics tab, beside the records it describes. Works because it removes the decision to go and look."
  - title: "They explore it themselves"
    icon: "i-lucide-compass"
    description: "Faceting, saved views and a well-labelled dataset let people answer their own follow-up questions. Works when the metadata is good, and fails invisibly when it is not."
---
::

## The sentence to carry through

::tip{icon="i-lucide-map-pin"}
**Put the analytics where the decision is already being made.** Not where it is architecturally tidiest. A dashboard that requires opening a different tool, at a different time, competes with everything else in the day and mostly loses — which is why embedding and subscriptions are in this section rather than in an appendix, and why the last lesson is about ownership rather than about features.
::
