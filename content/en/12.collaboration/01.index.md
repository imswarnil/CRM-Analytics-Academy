## What this section will cover

A dashboard nobody opens is a dashboard that does not exist — sharing, subscriptions, annotations, embedding and self-service, and the ownership that keeps any of it alive.

## Planned lessons

- **Getting it to people** — A dashboard nobody opens is a dashboard that does not exist — sharing, subscriptions, annotations, embedding and self-service, and the ownership that keeps any of it alive.
- **Apps & sharing** — The app is the unit of sharing, so app design is a governance decision — the three access levels, the two-app pattern that solves most orgs, and why sharing is not security.
- **Subscriptions** — Pushing a number to people who will never open a tool — scheduled subscriptions versus threshold notifications, and the discipline that stops both becoming mail nobody reads.
- **Annotations** — Letting people argue with a number in the place the number lives — why the conversation belongs on the dashboard rather than in a thread you never see.
- **Embedding** — Putting analytics on a record page and in the Analytics tab — removing the decision to go and look, and the discipline an embedded dashboard needs that a standalone one does not.
- **Self-service & views** — Letting people answer their own follow-up questions — what actually makes a dataset explorable, saved views, and the honest limits of self-service.
- **Downloads & governance** — Exports, the spreadsheet that becomes a competing truth, and the ownership that decides whether a dashboard is still trusted in a year.
- **Interview questions** — Sharing, adoption and governance — the questions that distinguish someone who builds dashboards from someone who owns them.
- **Graded quiz** — Eight questions on apps and sharing, subscriptions, annotations, embedding, self-service and governance.
