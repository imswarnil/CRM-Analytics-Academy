# The GTM Data Model

Twenty-one files. Before you load any of them, you need a map — because the mistakes that cost you a week are not chart mistakes, they are **grain** mistakes, and grain is decided here.

::note{icon="i-lucide-ruler"}
**Grain** is the question "what does one row mean?" Answer it out loud for every table before you join anything. `accounts.csv` is one row per account. `usage_monthly.csv` is one row per account **per product per month**. Join those two carelessly and the account's ARR is counted thirty-six times.
::

## The joins that matter

Four keys carry the whole model.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "AccountId — the spine"
    description: "accounts ← opportunities, usage_monthly, arr_snapshots, cases, contacts, and corporate rows in enrollments. This is the key that lets you ask 'did support cost us this renewal'. Everything post-lead hangs off it."
  - title: "OpportunityId — header to detail"
    description: "opportunities ← opportunity_products, quotes. One-to-many both times, so aggregate the detail before joining, or the header's Amount multiplies."
  - title: "CampaignId — demand to pipeline"
    description: "campaigns ← leads → opportunities. The only bridge from marketing spend to closed revenue, and it is a weak one: it holds a single opinion about attribution."
  - title: "OriginatingLeadId — the handoff"
    description: "opportunities → leads. Present on new-business deals only. Expansion and renewal have no lead, which is correct and breaks any funnel that assumes otherwise."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-triangle-alert"}
**The fan-out trap, concretely.** Join `accounts` to `usage_monthly` and ask for `sum(ARR)`. Lumen Motors, a Strategic account with three offerings across 18 months, now contributes its $455,400 ARR **54 times**. Across the whole base the chart reads $341 million — for a company with $13M of account ARR — and somebody believes it for an hour. The next lesson is entirely about avoiding this.
::

## Where the model deliberately has no join

This is as important as the joins, and it is the thing that separates a GTM engineer from someone who has read a Salesforce schema.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Demand tables"
    subtitle: "web_sessions, seo_keywords, llm_visibility"
    icon: "i-lucide-eye-off"
    cons:
      - "No AccountId. No LeadId. No person at all."
      - "Cannot be joined to revenue, ever"
    verdict: "Analyse these on their own grain and bridge with dates and channel names, not keys."
  - title: "Pipeline tables"
    subtitle: "leads, opportunities, quotes"
    icon: "i-lucide-user-check"
    pros:
      - "Identified. Every row is a named party."
      - "Joinable end to end"
    verdict: "Where attribution becomes possible — and still an argument."
---
::

Someone will ask you to show revenue by keyword. You cannot, not honestly: the session that read the page was anonymous, and the deal closed 97 days later through a different channel. **The correct answer is a modelled one with its assumptions stated**, and the demand section builds exactly that.

## Two conventions worth stealing

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Dates, not statuses"
    icon: "i-lucide-calendar-clock"
    description: "leads.csv has MQLDate, SQLDate and ConvertedDate rather than one Status field. A status says where a lead is now and destroys how long it took — and velocity is what people actually ask about."
  - title: "Snapshots, not recomputation"
    icon: "i-lucide-camera"
    description: "arr_snapshots.csv stores ARR as at each month end. The moment someone asks for 'ARR as at March', a table that only knows today cannot answer, and backfilling it is guesswork."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-check-circle-2"}
**What reconciles, by construction.** Campaign lead counts equal the lead rows naming them. Opportunity ARR equals the sum of its line items. `usage_monthly` only draws down an offering the account actually bought. A batch's `SeatsSold` equals its enrollment rows. The ARR waterfall ties to close dates. If your build disagrees with one of these, your build is wrong — which makes them a free test suite.
::
