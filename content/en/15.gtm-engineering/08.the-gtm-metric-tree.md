# The GTM Metric Tree

Ten dashboards is a lot to hold in your head. They are not ten separate things — they are one chain, and each dashboard watches one link.

::note{icon="i-lucide-git-branch"}
**The chain:** attention → visit → signup → lead → qualified → opportunity → quote → won → consumed → renewed. Money appears at step 8. Everything before it is a leading indicator, and everything after it decides whether the money stays.
::

## Why the tree matters more than any single metric

Two reasons, and they are the two most useful things to say in an interview.

::lesson-slides
---
items:
  - kicker: "Reason 1"
    title: "ARR tells you about last quarter"
    icon: "i-lucide-clock"
    bullets:
      - "An Enterprise cycle is ~118 days"
      - "Today's closed-won reflects demand from four months ago"
      - "By the time ARR moves, the cause is cold"
      - "Leading indicators are where you can still act"
  - kicker: "Reason 2"
    title: "A broken link is invisible downstream"
    icon: "i-lucide-unlink"
    bullets:
      - "Speed-to-lead slips from 0.6 to 4 days"
      - "Lead volume unchanged — marketing looks fine"
      - "Pipeline dips a month later, blamed on demand"
      - "Only the routing dashboard could have caught it"
  - kicker: "Consequence"
    title: "Watch ratios, not volumes"
    icon: "i-lucide-percent"
    bullets:
      - "Volume moves with spend and seasonality"
      - "A conversion rate moves when something broke"
      - "Every step in the tree has a ratio — that is the alert"
  - kicker: "Consequence"
    title: "Diagnose upward, not sideways"
    icon: "i-lucide-arrow-up"
    bullets:
      - "Pipeline down? Check SQL, then MQL, then sessions"
      - "The tree gives you the order"
      - "Without it, you check whatever chart is open"
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-search"}
**A diagnostic habit.** When a number is bad, walk one step up the tree and ask whether the ratio into it also moved. If the ratio held and only the volume fell, the problem is upstream. If the ratio fell, the problem is right there. Two questions, and you have localised most issues before opening a second dashboard.
::

## One warning about the tree

It is a model, and it is tidier than reality. Three ways Namilio breaks it:

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "Expansion skips the top"
    icon: "i-lucide-skip-forward"
    description: "An account doubling its messaging volume generates revenue with no session, no lead, no opportunity. A funnel-only view misses the largest growth source at a usage company."
  - title: "Steps run backwards"
    icon: "i-lucide-undo-2"
    description: "A developer hits the API before anyone knows their name. Signup follows usage. The 'funnel' is a loop with a sales motion attached to part of it."
  - title: "Losses re-enter"
    icon: "i-lucide-rotate-ccw"
    description: "A deal lost on 'Timing / budget freeze' comes back in two quarters as a new opportunity. Counting it as a loss and then as a new lead double-counts the demand."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-info"}
Use the tree to organise your dashboards and your diagnosis. Don't use it to claim a clean conversion path exists — at an AI company with self-serve and usage pricing, it doesn't, and pretending otherwise is how attribution models start lying.
::
