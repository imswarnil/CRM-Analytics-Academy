# Designing for Decisions, Not for Completeness

The most common request you will get is *"can you add..."*. Said yes to forty times, it produces a dashboard with forty widgets that answers nothing, because the reader cannot tell which four matter.

::note{icon="i-lucide-target"}
**Completeness is not the goal.** A dashboard with six widgets that changes a decision beats one with forty that documents a department.
::

## The brief comes before the canvas

Four questions. If any cannot be answered, the build is premature — and saying so is the job.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "The brief itself"
audience: "Whoever asked — named, not a department"
cadence: "When they will actually open it"
question: "The one question they are trying to answer"
decision: "What they do differently when the number is bad"
success: "How you will know in a month that it worked"
datasets: [any]
notInScope: ["Everything they mentioned that fails the decision test"]
---
::

The `decision` field is the one that does the work. Test it against two real requests:

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "\"Show me leads by source\""
    icon: "i-lucide-help-circle"
    cons:
      - "Decision: none stated"
      - "Audience: unclear"
      - "Likely outcome: looked at once"
    verdict: "Ask what they would do if one source were underperforming. If the answer is 'shift budget', the real dashboard is spend efficiency — a different build."
  - title: "\"Which deals are stuck?\""
    icon: "i-lucide-check-circle"
    pros:
      - "Decision: who the manager calls on Monday"
      - "Audience: front-line sales managers"
      - "Cadence: weekly, before forecast"
    verdict: "Build it. The decision is explicit and the cadence is real."
---
::

## The layout that keeps working

Every dashboard in modules 8 to 10 uses this shape. Not because it is clever — because readers learn it once and then navigate every dashboard you build.

::dashboard-mock
---
title: "The standard GTM layout"
subtitle: "Reads top-left to bottom-right, in decreasing urgency"
filters: [Date Range, Segment, Region, Owner]
caption: "Four bands: answer, trend, breakdown, action."
widgets:
  - label: "Headline KPI"
    type: kpi
    span: 3
    note: "vs target"
  - label: "Second KPI"
    type: kpi
    span: 3
  - label: "Third KPI"
    type: kpi
    span: 3
  - label: "Fourth KPI"
    type: kpi
    span: 3
  - label: "The trend — is it getting better?"
    type: line
    span: 8
    note: "Time on X, always"
  - label: "Mix"
    type: donut
    span: 4
  - label: "Breakdown by the dimension they act on"
    type: bar
    span: 6
    note: "Segment, channel, or rep"
  - label: "Second cut"
    type: column
    span: 6
  - label: "The action list — named rows, sorted by urgency"
    type: table
    span: 12
    note: "This is the widget people actually use"
---
::

    title: "What do I do now?"
    icon: "i-lucide-list-checks"

::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-list"}
**Band 4 is why dashboards get reopened.** Charts inform; a sorted list of named accounts with an owner beside each one gets acted on. If your dashboard has no action list, ask what the reader is supposed to *do* — you may find the answer is nothing.
::

## Five rules worth following

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "One question per dashboard"
    icon: "i-lucide-help-circle"
    description: "Two questions means two dashboards, linked. A dashboard serving marketing and sales at once usually serves neither, because the filters they need are different."
  - title: "Colour means one thing"
    icon: "i-lucide-palette"
    description: "If red is 'behind target' at the top, it cannot be 'high volume' below. Readers learn your colour language in three seconds and then trust it."
  - title: "Segment or say you didn't"
    icon: "i-lucide-layers"
    description: "The Academy's segments differ by 100×. Any blended average is a number describing no customer. Filter, or label the blend explicitly."
  - title: "Time on the X axis"
    icon: "i-lucide-calendar"
    description: "And name the date field in the title. 'Pipeline by Close Date' and 'by Created Date' are different dashboards that look identical."
  - title: "Fewer widgets, deliberately"
    icon: "i-lucide-minus-circle"
    description: "Every widget you remove makes the remaining ones easier to read. Push the detail into a linked drill-down rather than the front page."
  - title: "Put the definition on the face"
    icon: "i-lucide-file-text"
    description: "Formula in a text widget, filters stated. It is the difference between a dashboard people trust and one they quietly rebuild in a spreadsheet."
---
::

## Say no properly

You will conclude a requested dashboard should not exist. Handle that in one move: don't refuse, **reframe**.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Name what they asked for"
    description: "'You asked for revenue by keyword.' Show you understood before you complicate it."
  - title: "Say plainly why it cannot be honest"
    description: "'The session that read the page was anonymous, and the deal closed 97 days later through a different channel. Any number I put there is modelled, not measured.'"
  - title: "Offer the nearest true thing"
    description: "'I can show which keyword clusters precede a signup, and model influenced pipeline with the assumption written on the chart.'"
  - title: "Let them choose"
    description: "Most people take the honest version once it exists. The ones who insist on the first version now own the assumption, which is the right place for it."
---
::
