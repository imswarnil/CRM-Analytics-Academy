# GTM Engineering on CRM Analytics

Everything up to here taught you the tool. This section teaches you the **job**.

A GTM engineer sits between revenue teams and the systems they run on. Nobody asks you for a dataflow — they ask why marketing's lead number doesn't match sales', why the forecast moved on a Thursday, whether the AI-referral traffic is real pipeline. Those are analytics questions wearing a business costume, and answering them is most of the work.

::note{icon="i-lucide-compass"}
**The thread through this section:** one company — the Academy itself — one warehouse, and a dashboard built for every stage of its funnel, from an assistant recommending the course to a team renewal that nearly churned.
::

## Why the Academy's own business

The sections before this used simple slices of the data because it was a clean way to learn charts. It is a bad way to learn GTM, because it has none of the problems that make GTM hard.

So the company here is **CRM Analytics Academy** itself: a free course at the top of the funnel, a $240 individual subscription, team plans sold to L&D leaders, classroom batches at six centers, certification vouchers and implementation projects. It is a small business, but it has every problem the companies hiring for this role have — self-serve and sales-led motions meeting in the middle, three pricing models, one-off revenue beside recurring revenue, and a funnel that starts long before anyone fills in a form.

## The one idea to take from this section

A dashboard is not a report. A report presents numbers; a dashboard **changes a decision**. The test is uncomfortable and worth applying to everything you build:

> Name the decision this changes, the person who makes it, and what they do differently when the number is red.

If you cannot fill in all three, building it is the wrong move — and saying so is the most valuable thing a GTM engineer does. Every build lesson here opens with that brief, and one of them concludes you should not build the dashboard at all.
