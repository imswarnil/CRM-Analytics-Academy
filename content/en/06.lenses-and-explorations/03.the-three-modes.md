# The Three Modes

One exploration, three representations. The mode switcher is the most underused control in the product.

## Chart mode

What you build dashboards from, and what misleads most easily. A chart is a *rendering* of a query — it inherits every decision made upstream, including the wrong ones, and it renders them handsomely.

## Table mode

The rows behind the chart. This is where arguments end.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-table-2"}
**Whenever a chart is surprising, go to table mode before you go anywhere else.** A surprising chart is either an insight or a grain problem, and the rows tell you which in about ten seconds. In my experience of this dataset and most others, it is the grain problem more often than it is the insight — and the ten seconds is the difference between reporting a finding and reporting a bug.
::

Table mode also answers the questions a chart physically cannot: which specific account is that outlier, what is the exact value, are there two rows here where I expected one.

## SAQL mode

The query your clicks generated. Two distinct uses:

**As a learning tool.** You already know what the answer should be, so reading the query teaches the language for free. This is the single best way to learn SAQL and it costs nothing but the habit.

**As an escape hatch.** When clicking genuinely runs out — multi-step calculations, windowing, comparing a group against its own total — you edit the query directly. The next lesson is about that.

::note{icon="i-lucide-arrow-left-right"}
**The modes are not a one-way trip.** Click to get close, drop into SAQL for the part that needs it, and go back to chart mode to see the result. The common misconception is that hand-editing SAQL locks you out of the visual builder permanently — in practice you will spend most of your time clicking and occasionally reaching for the query.
::

## A worked example

The Academy's opportunities, grouped by segment, summing Amount. One segment shows far more revenue than its account count suggests it should.

    description: "The bar is wrong-looking. That is all a chart can tell you, and it is genuinely useful — noticing is a skill."
  - title: "Table mode answers it"
    description: "Sort descending, look at the top rows. Is one enormous deal doing it — a real finding — or is the same deal appearing several times, which is fan-out from a join upstream?"
  - title: "SAQL mode tells you what was asked"
    description: "Read the query. Sometimes the grouping or filter is not what you thought you clicked, and the query is the unambiguous statement of what the chart actually shows."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-image"}
**A chart is the most persuasive way to show a wrong number.** Nothing about a bar chart signals that its underlying rows were duplicated; it renders a doubled total with exactly the same confidence as a correct one. That is why table mode is not a beginner's crutch — it is the verification step, and skipping it is how a wrong number reaches a meeting.
::
