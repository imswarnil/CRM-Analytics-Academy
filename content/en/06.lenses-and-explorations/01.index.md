# Exploring Is the Work

Dashboards get the attention. Lenses are where the understanding actually happens, and a person who explores well produces better dashboards than a person who designs well but explores badly.

::note{icon="i-lucide-recycle"}
**A lens is disposable on purpose.** You are meant to make twenty in an hour and keep none of them. That is not waste — it is what asking a question of data looks like. The habit to avoid is treating each exploration as a thing to be preserved, which quietly converts a fast investigative tool into a filing problem.
::

## What a lens is

One dataset. One or more groupings. One or more measures. A chart, or a table, or the query behind both. Nothing is saved unless you save it.

## The one habit from this section

::tip{icon="i-lucide-repeat-2"}
**Click, then read the SAQL. Every time, for one afternoon.** Add a grouping and read it. Change the measure and read it. Add a filter, sort, limit. That is the language's entire grammar learned against answers you can already verify by eye — and when section 8's attribution build genuinely needs hand-written SAQL, it will be familiar rather than new. Twenty minutes of this beats any amount of syntax reference.
::
