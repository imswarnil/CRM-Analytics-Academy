# Conversational Analytics

Type a question, get a chart. It works, it is useful, and it has one failure mode worth understanding before anyone relies on it.

## The failure mode

::warning{icon="i-lucide-message-square-warning"}
**It answers fluently, including when it has answered a different question.** Ask for "revenue by rep last quarter" and you might get the right measure grouped by owner but cohorted on close date when you meant created date, or filtered to the calendar quarter when the company runs a different fiscal one. The chart is correct for *some* question. Nothing on screen indicates that it is not yours. Fluency is not accuracy, and a fluent wrong answer is more dangerous than an obviously broken one.
::

The defence is the one from earlier in this section: **check it in table mode, or read the generated query.** A natural-language result is a starting point to verify, not an answer to quote. That is a reasonable working posture and it is not a criticism of the feature — the same is true of a chart somebody else built.

## Why field metadata decides the quality

Natural language has to map your words onto fields. That mapping is only as good as the labels:

- A field labelled `Amount__c` is hard to match to "revenue". Labelled "Deal Amount", it is easy.
- Two similar fields with vague labels produce confident wrong matches — `Amount` versus `LineAmount` is exactly this hazard.
- Sixty visible columns, half of them keys, make the search space noisy and the matches worse.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-link"}
**The previous lesson is the prerequisite for this one.** Label the fields, hide the plumbing, set the formats — and natural-language querying gets noticeably better without anyone touching a setting called "AI". Most complaints that it "does not understand our data" are complaints about metadata nobody maintained.
::

## The general principle

This is the same position the Einstein Discovery section took on Einstein Discovery and the Einstein Discovery section takes on the lead score: **the tooling is genuinely useful, and every output needs a check that the output itself will not provide.** That is not scepticism about AI in analytics — it is the same standard applied to a chart a colleague built, a spreadsheet a vendor sent, or a number in an email.
