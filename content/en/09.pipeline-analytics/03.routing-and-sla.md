# Routing, SLA and Speed to Lead

This is the least glamorous dashboard in the section and probably the highest return. Nothing here is analytically clever; it just makes an operational failure visible while it is still fixable.

::note{icon="i-lucide-timer"}
**Why it matters more than lead volume.** If speed-to-lead slips from under a day to four days, lead volume is unchanged — marketing looks fine, and pipeline dips a month later and gets blamed on demand. Only this dashboard catches it in week one.
::

## Prove the SLA matters before you enforce it

Do not open with a target. Open with the evidence, or the team will treat the SLA as an arbitrary stick.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Bucket leads by DaysToFirstTouch"
    description: "Same day, 1 day, 2-3 days, 4-7 days, 8+ days. Five buckets from a recipe formula field."
  - title: "Compute conversion rate per bucket"
    description: "IsSQL rate and IsConverted rate. Bar chart, buckets on the X axis ascending."
  - title: "Show the curve, then set the target"
    description: "The rate falls as the bucket rises. Now the SLA is derived from your own data rather than borrowed from a blog post, and the conversation changes completely."
  - title: "Control for score band"
    description: "Fast-touched leads may also be higher-scoring, which would confound the whole chart. Repeat the analysis within one score band — if the pattern survives, the effect is real."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-flask-conical"}
**That fourth step is what separates analysis from a chart.** The obvious objection to a speed-to-lead curve is that reps call the good leads first, so speed is an effect rather than a cause. Facet by score band before anyone raises it, and you have answered the only question that matters.
::

## Coverage, argued properly

"We need more SDRs" is a claim. Here is how to turn it into evidence.

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Lead Coverage Ratio"
    formula: "count(routable leads) / count(active SDRs)"
    grain: "Per week × region"
    source: "leads.csv + reps.csv"
    watchout: "Exclude self-serve signups from the numerator — they are not routed. Include Ramping reps in the denominator but show them separately: a region covered by two reps in week three is not covered, and a headline ratio hides that."
  - name: "Effective Capacity"
    formula: "count(active SDRs, weighted by ramp) × touches per SDR per day"
    grain: "Per week × region"
    source: "reps.csv + leads.csv"
    watchout: "Weight Ramping at ~0.4 and Partial at ~0.7 rather than counting every rep as one. State the weights on the dashboard. They are estimates, and being transparent about them is what makes the capacity argument credible instead of convenient."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-search"}
**Check routing before coverage, every time.** A regional or shift-shaped gap is free to fix and a headcount gap costs six figures. Presenting the expensive answer when the cheap one was available is how an analytics function loses credibility with a CFO.
::
