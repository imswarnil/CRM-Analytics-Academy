# Designing Dashboards in CRM Analytics

You can build a functional CRM Analytics dashboard in about ten minutes. Drag a few charts on, wire up a filter, save. It works. It also looks like every other dashboard in the org, and nobody opens it twice.

This section is about the **other 90%** — the widget craft that turns a working dashboard into one an executive keeps pinned. None of it is hard. It's mostly a matter of knowing which property panel holds the setting you want, and having seen the trick once before.

## What "design" means here

Design in CRM Analytics is not about picking prettier colors. It's about **reducing the distance between a question and its answer**. Every technique in this section does one of three things:

::lesson-cards
---

items:
  - title: "Make meaning instant"
    icon: "i-lucide-palette"

  - title: "Make filtering effortless"

  - title: "Make context available"

    description: "Custom tooltips, images in tables, and links to related dashboards put the supporting detail one hover or one click away."

## What's ahead in this section

::tip{icon="i-lucide-lightbulb"}
Before you start experimenting, **clone your dashboard**. CRM Analytics has no meaningful undo once you've saved, and half of design work is trying something, hating it, and backing out. A throwaway copy makes you brave. We show the clone-to-a-new-tab flow in the KPI lesson.
::

By the end you'll have a vocabulary of small, reusable moves you can apply to any dashboard you inherit. First up, the one that pays off fastest: **conditional formatting**.
