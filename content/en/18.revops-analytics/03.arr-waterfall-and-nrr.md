# Build: The ARR Waterfall and Net Retention

**Build 14.** Most of the Academy's ARR arrives as new logos, but the base it already has grew on its own by nearly 40% in a year — teams adding seats, buying another classroom block, topping up vouchers — and in September 2026 a single churned account took $354K of it away again. This dashboard is how you see both.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "ARR Waterfall & Retention"
audience: "The CRO, the CFO, and the VP of Customer Success"
question: "Where did this month's ARR come from, where did it go, and is the existing base growing on its own?"
decision: "Whether to fund expansion motions or retention motions next quarter — and which segment gets the headcount"
cadence: "Monthly, at close"
success: "The movement between any two months is explainable in one sentence without opening Salesforce"
datasets: [arr_snapshots.csv, accounts.csv]
notInScope:
  - "Pipeline. This dashboard is strictly post-signature; mixing unsold pipeline into a retention view is how expansion targets get double-counted against new-business targets"
  - "Per-account detail. That is Build 15, where consumption explains WHY the movements happened"
---
::

## Why the snapshot is the whole build

::note{icon="i-lucide-camera"}
**`arr_snapshots.csv` is 1,579 rows: one per account per month, eighteen months.** The same account appears eighteen times, and each row states what was true that month. This is what the datasets and modelling section called a snapshot dataset, and it is the only reason this build exists — new, expansion, contraction and churn are *movements between states*, and no amount of cleverness recovers them from an account record that only knows today's number.
::

## The metrics

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Net New ARR"
    formula: "NewARR + ExpansionARR − ContractionARR − ChurnedARR, for the month"
    grain: "one value per month, or per month per segment"
    source: "arr_snapshots.csv"
    watchout: "The movement columns are recorded in the snapshot, not derived at query time. If they are all zero, the field was not synced — the chart still renders and every movement collapses into one net number with nothing on screen to say so."
  - name: "Net Revenue Retention"
    formula: "(OpeningARR + ExpansionARR − ContractionARR − ChurnedARR) ÷ OpeningARR, over accounts present at the start of the period"
    grain: "one value per period per cohort"
    source: "arr_snapshots.csv"
    watchout: "Excludes new business deliberately. Also fix the cohort: accounts present at the START of the window. Letting accounts that arrived mid-window into the denominator inflates NRR and the error is invisible because the number still looks plausible."
  - name: "Gross Revenue Retention"
    formula: "(OpeningARR − ContractionARR − ChurnedARR) ÷ OpeningARR"
    grain: "one value per period per cohort"
    source: "arr_snapshots.csv"
    watchout: "Cannot exceed 100% by construction. If yours does, expansion has leaked into it — which is the most common implementation error in this whole section."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-eye-off"}
**Those two scenarios have the same NRR and completely different futures.** That is the argument for putting gross retention on the screen beside it, permanently — the most common executive-dashboard mistake in a subscription business is reporting NRR alone and congratulating a company that is losing a fifteenth of its base every period.
::

## The Academy's own reading

The cohort of 20 accounts that were paying in September 2025 opened at $912K of ARR. Twelve months later they had added $386K of expansion and lost $25K to contraction and churn: **NRR of 139.5% and GRR of 97.2%.** That is the healthy case on the left — but it rests on twenty accounts, and the company's biggest single movement of the whole period arrived in September 2026 from outside that cohort: one account churning $354K, which turned the month's net new ARR negative. A retention dashboard that only showed the cohort NRR would have reported an excellent year and missed the month that mattered.

## The last widget is the most used

The movements table — account, movement type, amount — is what turns this from a reporting screen into a working one. When the CRO asks why contraction doubled, the answer is three account names, and it is on the screen already.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-table-2"}
**Sort it by absolute movement, not by signed value.** The largest contraction and the largest expansion are both interesting; sorting by signed amount buries the contractions at the bottom where nobody looks, which is exactly the wrong end of the table to hide.
::
