# Build: Forecast and Commit

The forecast is the most politically loaded number you will ever publish. It is also the one where being right matters less than being **consistently wrong in a measurable direction**.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "Forecast & Commit"
audience: "CRO, VP Sales, front-line managers, finance"
cadence: "Weekly during the quarter, daily in the last two weeks"
question: "What will we close this quarter, and how much confidence does that deserve?"
decision: "Whether to escalate specific deals, and what number finance plans against."
success: "Forecast accuracy improves and, more importantly, becomes a tracked metric instead of a memory."
datasets: [opp_enriched, reps.csv, arr_snapshots.csv]
notInScope: ["Quota setting", "Commission calculation"]
---
::

## Three forecasts, three assumptions

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Category forecast"
    subtitle: "Sum of Commit + Best Case"
    icon: "i-lucide-user-check"
    pros:
      - "Reflects rep judgement, which is real information"
      - "What the sales team will defend"
    cons:
      - "Systematically optimistic"
      - "Moves for political reasons near quarter end"
    verdict: "Publish it. It is the team's number and excluding it makes the dashboard adversarial."
  - title: "Weighted forecast"
    subtitle: "Sum of Amount × Probability"
    icon: "i-lucide-sigma"
    pros:
      - "Mechanical, unbiased by mood"
      - "Easy to reproduce"
    cons:
      - "Only as good as the probabilities"
      - "CRM default probabilities are usually 10-20 points optimistic"
    verdict: "Publish it — with probabilities derived from your own stage-to-win history, not the template."
  - title: "Historical forecast"
    subtitle: "Stage counts × observed conversion"
    icon: "i-lucide-history"
    pros:
      - "Derived entirely from what actually happened"
      - "The most defensible of the three"
    cons:
      - "Ignores deal-specific knowledge"
      - "Needs enough closed history per segment"
    verdict: "The anchor. When it disagrees with the category forecast, that gap is the meeting."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-move-horizontal"}
**The spread is the product.** Three numbers within 8% of each other means high confidence. Category at $14M against historical at $9M means the team is carrying $5M of optimism, and *that* is the conversation — not which single number is correct.
::

## Track your own accuracy

Almost nobody does this, and it is the thing that makes the forecast trustworthy over time.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Snapshot the forecast weekly"
    description: "Every Monday, store each forecast method's number for the current quarter. Without a stored history you cannot measure accuracy, and you cannot reconstruct it later."
  - title: "At quarter close, compare to actual"
    description: "Error percentage per method, per week-of-quarter."
  - title: "Plot the convergence curve"
    description: "Error against weeks remaining. Every method converges as the quarter closes — what matters is how early each becomes reliable, and by how much each over- or under-shoots."
  - title: "Publish the bias"
    description: "If the category forecast runs 18% high in week 3 and 6% high in week 11, say so. Now the CRO can mentally discount it, which is far more useful than pretending accuracy."
---
::

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Forecast Accuracy"
    formula: "abs(forecast − actual) / actual"
    grain: "Per method × week-of-quarter × segment"
    source: "stored weekly snapshots + opp_enriched"
    watchout: "Report signed error as well as absolute — a method that is always 15% high is far more useful than one randomly off by 15%, because the first can be corrected and the second cannot. Most accuracy reporting uses absolute error only and loses exactly the information you need."
---
::

## When the number moves

It will, and how you handle it decides whether the dashboard survives.

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Make movement explainable"
    icon: "i-lucide-git-compare"
    description: "The slippage table is the answer to 'why did the forecast drop $2M on Thursday'. Deals that moved out, were downgraded, or shrank — named, with owners. Without it every change looks like a data problem."
  - title: "Never silently restate"
    icon: "i-lucide-file-clock"
    description: "If a definition or probability set changes, date-stamp it and keep the old series visible. A forecast history that quietly rewrites itself destroys trust in every other number you publish."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**The quarter-end pattern to watch for.** Category forecasts firming up sharply in the last ten days while the historical forecast stays flat usually means deals are being recategorised rather than progressed. Check whether `StageEnteredDate` actually moved — if the category changed and the stage did not, the optimism is administrative.
::
