# Build: The Executive GTM Board

**Build 14.** Nine numbers, one screen, no scrolling. This is the hardest lesson in the course and it contains less than any other build.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "Namilio GTM Board"
audience: "The CEO, CRO, CFO and CMO, monthly"
question: "Is the go-to-market machine working, and where is it not?"
decision: "Where the next quarter's money and headcount go"
cadence: "Monthly, in the exec review — on screen, in the room"
success: "A viewer who has never seen it can state the company's situation in four seconds, and the meeting argues about what to do rather than about whose number is right"
datasets: [arr_snapshots.csv, usage_monthly.csv, opp_enriched, marketing_spend.csv, accounts.csv]
notInScope:
  - "Every rep, campaign, keyword, case and account detail — 111 of the ~120 widgets built across Builds 1–13. Each one lives on its own dashboard and is one click away"
  - "Anything nobody would act on this quarter. A number that cannot change a decision in the next ninety days is a number for a different meeting"
  - "Anything that needs a paragraph to interpret. If it cannot be read in a glance it will be misread in a glance"
---
::

## Why the smallest dashboard is the hardest

::note{icon="i-lucide-scissors"}
**You arrive here with roughly 120 widgets across fifteen dashboards, and every one has somebody who built it and cares about it.** Nine survive. The work of this build is not selecting the nine — most people would pick a similar nine — it is being able to say, to the person who built the campaign-performance screen, why their work is not on the board and why that is not a judgement about its value. That conversation is the senior skill this entire course has been building towards.
::

      - "Often genuinely important to its own team"
    cons:
      - "Downstream of a number already shown"

      - "Needs a paragraph, so it will be misread in a glance"
    verdict: "Link to it. Do not shrink it onto this screen."

## What to say when somebody asks why their widget is not on it

::tip{icon="i-lucide-handshake"}
**"This board answers one question — is the machine working — and yours answers a better question than that one, so it gets a screen of its own rather than a corner of this one."** That is true, it is not a brush-off, and it usually ends the conversation well. What does not work is agreeing to add it "just for this quarter", because a board with fourteen numbers cannot be read in four seconds and nobody ever proposes removing one.
::
