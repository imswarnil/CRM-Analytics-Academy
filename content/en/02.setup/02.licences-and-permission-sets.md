# Licences and Permission Sets

Two layers, and they fail differently. A **licence** is what the org bought. A **permission set** is how that entitlement reaches a person. Confusing them wastes an afternoon; separating them makes every access problem a two-minute diagnosis.

## The licence layer

CRM Analytics is licensed separately from Sales or Service Cloud, in tiers. The names have changed with the product's names and will change again, so treat the shape as stable and the labels as something to verify:

::field-table
---
dataset: entitlement tiers
grain: one row per tier
fields:
  - name: "CRM Analytics Growth"
    type: "the base tier"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Datasets, recipes, lenses, dashboards, apps, sharing. Everything in sections 3 to 6 of this course, and every build in 8 and 9."
  - name: "CRM Analytics Plus"
    type: "the higher tier"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Everything above, plus Einstein Discovery — training stories and deploying models that score records and write back. Sections 1, 4 and 9 use it."
  - name: "Row and data limits"
    type: "attached to the tier"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Each tier carries its own limits on rows per dataset and total data. On a large org this is a sizing exercise BEFORE the project starts. Check the current figures for your licence rather than any number written a year ago, including this one."
  - name: "The Developer Edition org"
    type: "everything, tiny"
    role: "key"
    note: "The free practice org from the introduction carries the features with small limits. Perfect for this course and useless as a guide to what your production org can hold."
---
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-receipt"}
**You cannot permission your way past a licence.** If the org has no CRM Analytics entitlement, there is no permission set to assign, no setting to toggle, and no workaround. This is worth confirming in the first conversation of any new project — "what are we licensed for, and for how many users" — because the answer changes the design, not just the paperwork.
::

## The permission-set layer

Access is granted by **permission set**, not by profile. This is the single most common first-day obstacle.

::note{icon="i-lucide-shield-alert"}
**System Administrator grants no CRM Analytics access whatsoever.** An admin can see every record in the org and still have no Analytics Studio tile, because the analytics permissions arrive through permission set licences that must be assigned like any other. It is not a bug and it is not an oversight; it is what lets an org give analytics to people who are not admins, and withhold it from admins who do not need it.
::

The sets pair up as admin and user, per tier:

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "…Plus Admin"
    subtitle: "The builder"
    icon: "i-lucide-hammer"
    pros:
      - "Create and manage apps, datasets, recipes, dashboards"
      - "Manage data sync and connections"
      - "Typically sees all data through the platform"
    cons:
      - "Broad. In production this is a small, deliberate list of people"
    verdict: "You, in your practice org. Two or three people in a real one."
  - title: "…Plus User"
    subtitle: "The consumer"
    icon: "i-lucide-eye"
    pros:
      - "View and explore what has been shared with them"
      - "Save their own lenses and views"
      - "Subject to row-level security, as they must be"
    cons:
      - "Cannot build datasets, which is the point"
    verdict: "Almost everybody. This is the one that scales."
---
::

In your own practice org, assign yourself both and move on. In a real org, the split matters: the admin set is a licence to change what everyone else sees, and it should be granted like one.

## Diagnosing a missing tile in two minutes

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Look at the user, not the dashboard"
    description: "Setup → Users → the user → Permission Set Assignments. If neither analytics set is there, that is the whole answer and it takes twenty seconds."
  - title: "Check the org's entitlement"
    description: "If the set cannot be assigned because no licences remain, this is a purchasing conversation, not a configuration one. Say so early and precisely."
  - title: "Have them log out and back in"
    description: "Permission set assignments sometimes need a fresh session before the App Launcher notices. This resolves 'I assigned it and it is still not there' more often than anything else."
  - title: "Only then look at the app"
    description: "A user with access to CRM Analytics still sees nothing until an APP has been shared with them. Access to the product and access to your content are two different grants, and confusing them costs an hour per occurrence."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-split"}
**Say the two grants out loud, because they are genuinely separate.** "Can you get into CRM Analytics at all?" is the permission set. "Can you see MY dashboard?" is app sharing, covered in section 6. A user can pass one and fail the other, and the symptom — a blank screen — looks identical.
::

## What to write down for your own org

Three facts, in whatever passes for your project notes. You will be asked all three repeatedly:

1. **Which tier** the org is licensed for, and therefore whether Einstein Discovery is on the table.
2. **How many user licences** exist, and how many are assigned — because "roll it out to the whole sales team" has a ceiling.
3. **Who holds the admin set**, since that list is who can change what everyone else sees.
