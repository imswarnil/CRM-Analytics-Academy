# Filters and Nulls

Two topics that look like housekeeping and account for a surprising share of wrong numbers.

## Filter early

Because SAQL is a stream, a filter placed after a group has already paid to aggregate rows it then throws away.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Right after load, wherever possible"
    description: "Narrow to the rows the question is about before anything else happens. This is both faster and closer to what you meant."
  - title: "Then group and compute"
    description: "Aggregating a smaller stream is the entire performance story for most queries."
  - title: "Filter after aggregation only when the condition is about the aggregate"
    description: "'Segments with more than $1M of revenue' can only be evaluated once revenue exists, so that filter genuinely belongs after the foreach."
---
::

## The inequality trap

::warning{icon="i-lucide-ban"}
**A `!=` comparison drops nulls along with the values you excluded.** `'Stage' != "Closed Lost"` removes closed-lost deals *and* every deal whose stage is blank — the ones most likely to be data-quality problems worth seeing. The number falls, nothing errors, and the missing rows are exactly the ones nobody is watching. When you mean "everything except X, including unknowns", write it explicitly: `'Stage' != "Closed Lost" || 'Stage' is null`.
::

This is the SAQL version of the recipe filter warning from the data preparation section, and it is the same mistake in a different place.

## Zero is not unknown

A null means "we do not know". A zero means "we know, and it is none". They behave differently in every aggregation and they are frequently confused:

::field-table
---
dataset: null behaviour
grain: one row per function
fields:
  - name: "sum"
    type: "skips nulls"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Returns the total of the rows that had a value. Over a sparse column this is a total of a subset, presented as a total."
  - name: "avg"
    type: "skips nulls in both parts"
    role: "measure"
    note: "The mean of the rows that had a value. Usually right, rarely checked — and materially different from the mean over all rows if you intended nulls to count as zero."
  - name: "count vs unique"
    type: "rows vs values"
    role: "key"
    note: "count() counts rows at the grain; unique() counts distinct non-null values. On a sparse column the difference is large and neither is wrong."
  - name: "Grouping by a nullable field"
    type: "produces a null bucket"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Which is honest and often the most interesting bucket on the chart — 'unassigned' is a real category. Label it rather than hiding it."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-search"}
**Check null density before trusting a column.** One query — count of rows against `unique` or a count of non-null values — tells you whether a field is 5% empty or 60% empty. The Academy's CSAT is the standing example: only about 44% of closed cases carry a survey, so every satisfaction number describes respondents rather than customers. The figure is not wrong; it answers a narrower question than its label claims.
::

## Filters in dashboards versus filters in queries

Three different things, and confusing them produces dashboards where half the widgets respond to a control and half quietly do not:

- **A query filter** is part of the widget's SAQL — always applied, invisible to the viewer.
- **A dashboard filter or facet** is applied on top, per interaction, and only reaches widgets wired to it.
- **A security predicate** is applied by the platform, per viewer, and cannot be bypassed from either.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-list-checks"}
**A widget that must always exclude something should exclude it in the query, not in a filter the viewer can clear.** "Open pipeline" should never be able to show closed-lost deals because somebody reset a filter. Anything structural belongs in the query; anything exploratory belongs in the filter.
::
