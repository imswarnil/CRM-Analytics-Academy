Gross retention, and the split by segment. NRR of 112 with gross of 108 is a healthy base; NRR of 112 with gross of 94 is a bucket losing six points a period, refilled by a handful of expanding accounts, and one of those churning flips the picture. The two have identical headlines and completely different futures, which is why I put gross beside NRR permanently. Then segment, because a blended figure lets one bleeding segment hide behind another's growth for quarters.
