# Build: Spend Efficiency, CAC and Payback

Campaign performance answers "which campaign". This answers "are we buying growth at a sensible price" — and it is the dashboard that gets quoted in a board meeting, so the definitions have to be airtight.

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "Spend Efficiency & CAC"

question: "What does it cost to acquire a customer, and how long until we get it back?"
decision: "Whether to increase or hold total acquisition spend next quarter."
success: "One agreed CAC definition per segment that survives a board question; payback tracked as a trend rather than recomputed each time."

## Three CACs, all legitimate

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Blended CAC"
    formula: "total acquisition spend / count(new customers)"
    grain: "Per month or quarter, all segments"
    source: "marketing_spend.csv + accounts.csv"
    watchout: "At the Academy this is close to meaningless on its own. Roughly a third of new customers are Self-Serve arriving at near-zero cost, which drags the average far below what it actually costs to win a Mid-Market deal. Publish it because the board asks for it — never use it for a decision."
  - name: "Paid CAC"
    formula: "sum(Spend where IsPaid = true) / count(new customers from paid channels)"
    grain: "Per month × segment"
    source: "marketing_spend.csv + leads.csv"
    watchout: "Numerator and denominator must cover the same channels. Dividing total spend by paid-sourced customers is the most common error and understates CAC badly — it is worth checking any inherited dashboard for exactly this."
  - name: "Segment CAC"
    formula: "segment-attributed spend / new customers in that segment"
    grain: "Per segment"
    source: "as above, filtered"
    watchout: "The only version that supports a decision. Requires allocating channel spend to segments, which is a judgement — state the allocation rule on the dashboard so it is auditable rather than magic."
---
::

## Payback, and why it is the real number

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "CAC alone says nothing"
    icon: "i-lucide-help-circle"
    description: "$40,000 to win a Mid-Market account is either excellent or ruinous. The number is meaningless without knowing how fast it comes back."
  - title: "Payback months"
    icon: "i-lucide-hourglass"
    description: "CAC ÷ (monthly gross profit per new customer). Under 12 months is comfortable, 12–24 needs funding, over 24 usually means the segment is wrong."
  - title: "Gross profit, not revenue"
    icon: "i-lucide-percent"
    description: "The Academy's margins run 35% on certification vouchers to 88% on Academy Pro. Using revenue rather than gross profit makes payback look about twice as good as it is."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-calculator"}
**Use the product mix, not a company-average margin.** An account buying classroom seat-days at 46% margin pays back far slower than one on the Team Plan at 84%. `opportunity_products.csv` plus `products.csv` gives you the real blend per account, and the difference is large enough to change which segment you invest in.
::

## LTV when renewal depends on usage

The textbook formula assumes a stable contract and a churn rate. The Academy's prepaid pools have neither in the usual sense: a customer renews for whatever they actually used, and one-off classroom seats do not renew at all.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Textbook LTV"
    subtitle: "ARPA × gross margin ÷ churn rate"
    icon: "i-lucide-book"
    pros:
      - "Simple, and what the board expects"
    cons:
      - "Assumes stable revenue per account"
      - "Prepaid-pool accounts renew ±40% on usage"
      - "\"Churn rate\" is undefined for one-off classroom seats"
    verdict: "Publish it for the seat plans. Label it an estimate elsewhere."
  - title: "Cohort-realised value"
    subtitle: "Actual revenue per signup cohort over time"
    icon: "i-lucide-layers"
    pros:
      - "Measured, not modelled"
      - "Captures expansion, which is the main event here"
    cons:
      - "Needs 18+ months of history"
      - "Truncated for recent cohorts"
    verdict: "The honest version. Build it from arr_snapshots.csv and show it beside the textbook figure."
---
::

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Group accounts into signup-month cohorts"
    description: "From accounts.csv SignupDate. Twelve to eighteen cohorts is plenty."
  - title: "Sum each cohort's ARR by month since signup"
    description: "From arr_snapshots.csv, indexed by months-since-signup rather than calendar month, so cohorts are comparable."
  - title: "Plot cumulative revenue per cohort"
    description: "Lines that keep climbing mean expansion is outrunning churn — which is the single most important fact about a business that sells into teams, and invisible in a churn-rate number."
  - title: "Mark where each cohort crosses its CAC"
    description: "That crossing point is realised payback. It is a measurement rather than a projection, and it is far more defensible in a board meeting."
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Recent cohorts always look bad.** A cohort three months old has three months of revenue against its full CAC. That is not underperformance, it is truncation. Either grey out cohorts younger than your payback target or state the truncation on the chart — otherwise somebody reads a normal ramp as a collapse.
::
