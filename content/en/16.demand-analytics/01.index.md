# Demand Analytics for an AI Company

Everything in this module happens **before anyone has a name**. No account, no lead, no owner — just attention, and the question of whether it is the right kind.

::note{icon="i-lucide-eye-off"}
**The structural problem:** the demand tables carry no `AccountId` and no `LeadId`. They cannot be joined to revenue. Anyone who tells you they have connected keyword to closed-won has built a model and called it a measurement.
::

## The CRM Analytics you will use here

Six builds, and each one is an exercise in a product skill you already have. If a line below is unfamiliar, go back to that lesson — these builds assume it rather than re-teaching it.

::field-table
---
dataset: product features this section exercises
grain: one row per feature
fields:
  - name: "Joining sources with no shared key"
    type: "section 3"
    role: "key"
    note: "Web sessions, spend, keywords and LLM visibility do not share an id with the CRM. Joining on date and channel instead of on a key is the whole difficulty of demand analytics."
  - name: "Cohorting by the right date"
    type: "section 3"
    role: "date"
    note: "Group by the date demand was created, not the date it converted. The wrong choice makes last month look catastrophic and both numbers stay individually correct."
  - name: "Share of total, in SAQL"
    type: "section 4"
    role: "measure"
    note: "Channel mix, keyword share and attribution splits all need each group compared against the whole. This is the pattern that most often sends you into SAQL."
  - name: "Bucketing in a transform node"
    type: "section 3"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Spend bands, position bands, session-depth bands. Bucket once in the recipe so Large means the same thing on every screen."
  - name: "Conditional formatting with a business threshold"
    type: "section 5"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "Payback over a number somebody chose, not over a third of the range — and always with a second channel beside the colour."

## The honest framing for every chart in this module

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "Correlation, stated"
    icon: "i-lucide-link"
    description: "Docs traffic rises, signups rise. Almost certainly related. Not proof, and worth saying so on the chart rather than in a footnote nobody reads."
  - title: "Direction over magnitude"
    icon: "i-lucide-trending-up"
    description: "Whether AI visibility is improving is answerable and useful. 'AI search drove $4.2M' is not, and claiming it costs you credibility the first time someone checks."
  - title: "Ratios, not volumes"
    icon: "i-lucide-percent"
    description: "Sessions move with spend. Session-to-signup moves when something broke or improved. The ratio is the alert; the volume is the context."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-shield"}
**The credibility rule.** In this module you will be asked for numbers that cannot honestly exist. Every time you publish a modelled number, put the assumption on the face of the chart. It feels like weakening your work. It is the opposite — it is the thing that makes the rest of your numbers trusted.
::
