# Build: Acquisition

A visitor arrives, reads a free lesson, signs up, and starts working through the course. All four are measurable in GA4, and only the last one predicts revenue.

::note{icon="i-lucide-terminal"}
**At a product-led company the qualification event is usage, not interest.** A pricing-page view means someone is curious. A learner who has started the course properly has invested hours in your material — and is the person who will tell their manager which training to buy. Build the dashboard around that.
::

## Two traps specific to this data

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "The weekend cliff"
    icon: "i-lucide-calendar-x"
    description: "B2B traffic drops ~58% at the weekend — buyers do not shop for team training on a Saturday, even if their learners study then. Any day-over-day comparison reads as a crisis every weekend. Use 7-day rolling averages, or compare Tuesday to Tuesday, and say which you did."
  - title: "Lesson pages distort averages"
    icon: "i-lucide-file-code"
    description: "Lesson sessions run ~260 seconds and 3.8 pages against ~75 seconds and 1.9 for marketing pages. A site-wide 'average engagement' number is the ratio of your two audiences, not a measure of quality."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-split"}
**Segment by PageType before you conclude anything.** Course, Pricing, Product, Blog, Compare and Certification behave like six different websites. A single conversion rate across them is an average of things that should never have been averaged.
::

## Where this dashboard has to stop

::note{icon="i-lucide-hand"}
`web_sessions.csv` has **no account or lead key**. It cannot tell you that this session became that customer. The bridge is a signup event carrying a channel into `leads.csv` — which is exactly what `FirstTouchChannel` is, and exactly why it is a single stored opinion rather than a fact. The attribution lesson deals with this properly.
::
