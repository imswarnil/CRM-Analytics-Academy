# Build: AI Search Visibility

A Salesforce admin asks Claude *"best course to learn salesforce crm analytics"*. Either the Academy is in that answer or it is not. There is no page two, no rank 11, no long tail to fall back on.

::note{icon="i-lucide-sparkles"}
**Generative Engine Optimisation** is what SEO becomes when the interface is an answer instead of a list. The measurement problem is different in kind: there is no results page to scrape, the output changes between runs, and you are not competing for position — you are competing for **inclusion**.
::

## Why the denominator is the whole design

Ask an assistant the same question five times and you may get the Academy in three answers. A single check would have told you "yes" or "no" — both misleading.

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "One run = an anecdote"
    icon: "i-lucide-dice-3"
    description: "Non-determinism means a single check is a coin flip you mistook for a measurement. Teams build whole strategies on one lucky screenshot."
  - title: "Five runs = a rate"
    icon: "i-lucide-repeat"
    description: "3 of 5 is 60% Share of Voice. Now it is comparable across months, engines and prompts — and a change means something."
  - title: "The dataset does this for you"
    icon: "i-lucide-database"
    description: "RunId 1–5 per prompt per engine per month, with ShareOfVoiceRuns and ShareOfVoicePct stamped on every row of the group."
---
::

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Share of Voice"
    formula: "count(BrandMentioned = true) / count(all runs)"
    grain: "Per month × engine × prompt cluster"
    source: "llm_visibility.csv"
    watchout: "Because ShareOfVoicePct is pre-stamped on all five rows of a group, averaging it is correct but summing it is nonsense. If you aggregate across clusters, recompute from the boolean rather than averaging an average — unequal group sizes will skew it."
  - name: "Recommendation Rate"
    formula: "count(IsRecommended = true) / count(BrandMentioned = true)"
    grain: "Per month × cluster"
    source: "llm_visibility.csv"
    watchout: "Denominator is mentions, not runs. Two different failures need separating: not being named at all is a content problem; being named but never recommended is a positioning problem, and the fix is different."
  - name: "Citation Rate"
    formula: "count(CitedUrl is not empty) / count(BrandMentioned = true)"
    grain: "Per month × engine"
    source: "llm_visibility.csv"
    watchout: "Engines differ enormously — Perplexity cites ~88% of the time, Gemini ~49%. Blending them produces a number that describes no engine. Always split by engine or say you didn't."
---
::

## Build it

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Share of Voice KPI"
    description: "Number widget. Count rows where BrandMentioned = true, divide by total rows, in the Month filter's context. Compact formatting, one decimal."
  - title: "Trend by engine"
    description: "Timeline chart. Group by Month, then by Engine. Measure = the same ratio. Six lines is at the edge of readable — offer an engine filter rather than a legend with six entries."
  - title: "Recommendation rate, deliberately separate"
    description: "A second number widget filtered to BrandMentioned = true. Resist combining it with SoV into one 'visibility score' — the two failures have different fixes and a blended score hides which one you have."
  - title: "Cluster bar, sorted ascending"
    description: "Sort weakest first. This dashboard exists to find gaps, so the thing you want to act on should be at the top, not buried under your successes."
  - title: "Buyer stage heat map"
    description: "Cluster × BuyerStage, coloured by SoV. Being strong on Problem-stage prompts and absent on Vendor-stage is the single most common and most expensive pattern — you are teaching the market and losing the shortlist."
  - title: "The absence table"
    description: "Values table filtered to ShareOfVoicePct = 0, showing Prompt, Cluster, BuyerStage, CompetitorMentioned. This is the content brief, generated."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-crosshair"}
**Read the stage heat map first.** A Problem-stage prompt (*"how do i write a saql query with group by"*) is worth less than a Vendor-stage one (*"crm analytics training providers for a team"*), because the second buyer is already choosing — and choosing for forty people. Strong at the top, absent at the bottom, is a strategy that generates awareness for your competitors.
::

::

::note{icon="i-lucide-help-circle"}
**There is no industry benchmark, and you should say so.** This discipline is roughly two years old. Nobody can tell you what good Share of Voice looks like for a CRM Analytics training provider in EMEA. What you can do is set a baseline this month and measure movement — which is more useful anyway, because the only comparison that matters is you against yourself and against the competitors named beside you.
::

## How to collect this in real life

The dataset hands you the table. Producing it is a job in itself, and worth knowing how to scope.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Buy a GEO tool"
    icon: "i-lucide-shopping-cart"
    pros:
      - "Running in a day"
      - "Multi-engine out of the box"
    cons:
      - "Their prompt list, not your buyers'"
      - "Monthly cost per tracked prompt"
    verdict: "Fine to start. Replace the prompt list with your own sales-call language immediately."
  - title: "Build it yourself"
    icon: "i-lucide-code"
    pros:
      - "Your prompts, your clusters, your schema"
      - "A scheduled job, five runs, parse and store"
    cons:
      - "You own the maintenance"
      - "Terms of service vary by engine — check them"
    verdict: "Where most serious teams end up, because the prompt list is the actual strategy."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-mic-2"}
**Source your prompts from sales calls, not from a keyword tool.** The sentences that matter are the ones prospects actually say — *"we're comparing you with BootcampPro for our admin team"*. Ten of those beat a hundred generated by a volume estimator, because they carry buying intent by construction.
::
