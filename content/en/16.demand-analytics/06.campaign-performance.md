# Build: Campaign Performance

Forty-six campaigns, eleven channels, and a marketing leader who needs to cut 20% of budget. This is the dashboard that decides which 20%.

::note{icon="i-lucide-scale"}
**Volume is the enemy here.** Organic Search produces the most leads and the free course produces the cheapest ones — about $8 each. Rank by count and you cut the wrong thing. Every chart on this dashboard is a rate or a ratio for that reason.
::

## The two grains that never reconcile

This trips people up and is worth being explicit about, because the CFO will notice.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "campaigns.csv"
    subtitle: "Marketing's grain"
    icon: "i-lucide-megaphone"
    pros:
      - "One row per campaign, with its own spend"
      - "Directly attributable to leads"
    cons:
      - "Campaigns span months, so monthly totals blur"
      - "Excludes overheads, agency fees, salaries"
    verdict: "Use for campaign-level decisions: keep, kill, scale."
  - title: "marketing_spend.csv"
    subtitle: "Finance's grain"
    icon: "i-lucide-receipt"
    pros:
      - "Month × channel × region — matches the ledger"
      - "Includes spend no campaign carries"
    cons:
      - "Cannot attribute to a specific campaign"
    verdict: "Use for CAC and budget reporting. It is the bigger, truer number."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-equal-not"}
**They will not add up, and that is correct.** Campaign spend is a subset of channel spend. Put both on the dashboard with a label — *"campaign-attributed spend"* and *"total channel spend"* — and state the gap. Trying to force them to match is how people end up inventing allocations they then have to defend.
::

## The metrics, in the order they matter

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Cost per MQL"
    formula: "Spend / count(leads where IsMQL = true)"
    grain: "Per campaign, or per channel × month"
    source: "campaigns.csv"
    watchout: "Aggregate MQLs by MQLDate, not CreatedDate, or a campaign that ended last month keeps accruing MQLs into the wrong period. Also: a campaign with 2 MQLs has a meaningless cost per MQL — set a minimum volume before ranking, or noise wins."
  - name: "Pipeline ROI"
    formula: "sum(opportunity Amount) / Spend"
    grain: "Per campaign"
    source: "campaigns.csv + opportunities.csv"
    watchout: "Pipeline is not revenue. A 12× pipeline ROI on a channel with a 20% win rate is worse than 5× on one that wins 60%. Always show win rate beside it."
  - name: "Marketing ROI"
    formula: "sum(closed-won Amount) / Spend"
    grain: "Per campaign"
    source: "campaigns.csv + opportunities.csv"
    watchout: "The honest metric, and the one that lags. Enterprise cycles are ~97 days, so recent campaigns will always look terrible. Never rank campaigns on this without filtering to cohorts older than one cycle length."
---
::

## Build it

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Set a volume floor"
    description: "Filter the ranking charts to campaigns with at least 10 MQLs. Without it the top of your efficiency chart is a campaign that got two lucky leads, and somebody will try to triple its budget."
  - title: "Cohort by campaign start"
    description: "Add a filter for campaigns that started more than 120 days ago when looking at Marketing ROI. Recent campaigns have pipeline but no closed revenue yet — that is physics, not performance."
  - title: "Plot cost against quality"
    description: "Cost per MQL on one axis, lead→opportunity rate on the other. Four quadrants: cheap and good (scale), cheap and bad (the trap), expensive and good (keep), expensive and bad (cut). This single chart does most of the work."
  - title: "Show win rate in the table"
    description: "A channel can produce abundant cheap pipeline that never closes, or scarce expensive pipeline that closes well. Pipeline ROI alone cannot tell those apart; win rate beside it tells the truth."
  - title: "Trust the pre-computed columns"
    description: "campaigns.csv already carries Leads, MQLs, PipelineAmount and ClosedWonAmount computed from the detail rows. Use them rather than re-aggregating — and if your recomputation disagrees, you have fanned out."
---
::

    value: "$175"
    trend: down
    good: false

  - label: "Conference"
    value: "$363"
    trend: flat
    hint: "Worst per lead and worst pipeline ROI"
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-lightbulb"}
**The outbound paradox is the most useful thing on this dashboard.** Outbound is the most expensive paid channel per lead after conferences, and it converts the fewest leads to opportunities — 8.7% against about 20% for the free course. Rank on either and you cut it. But the opportunities it does create win 44% of the time, and 93% of its pipeline is Enterprise and Strategic: L&D leaders who would never have found a free SAQL lesson. Cut outbound on cost per lead and you lose the team deals. This is why the dashboard needs a Segment filter and a win-rate column — the ranking changes shape completely when you filter to Enterprise and Strategic.
::

## Two mistakes to avoid publishing

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Ranking on lead volume"
    icon: "i-lucide-bar-chart"
    description: "Produces a chart where Organic Search wins everything and the free course looks like a rounding error. It is the most common campaign dashboard in existence and it has misallocated a great deal of budget."
  - title: "Blending segments"
    icon: "i-lucide-blend"
    description: "Self-Serve deals close in days for a few hundred dollars; Strategic takes four and a half months for $600K. A single cost-per-acquisition across both is a number with no referent. Filter, always."
---
