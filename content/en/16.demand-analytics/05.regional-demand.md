# Build: Regional and Geographic Demand

The Academy gets 20% of its sessions from APAC and 13% of its closed-won revenue — yet 45% of its classroom revenue, because three of its six centers are in India. Somebody will read the first gap as an opportunity and somebody else as a warning, and nobody will think to mention the third number. The dashboard has to support the argument rather than settle it with a single map.

::note{icon="i-lucide-map"}
**One map is never enough.** Demand, conversion and revenue each have a geography, and the interesting insight is always in the *gap* between two of them.
::

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Regional Conversion Gap"
    formula: "(region's % of revenue) − (region's % of sessions)"
    grain: "Per region, per period"
    source: "opportunities.csv + web_sessions.csv, aggregated separately"
    watchout: "Two different denominators, so this is a share comparison, not a rate. A negative gap means attention is not converting — but it could be coverage, fit, pricing, language or payment methods, and the metric cannot tell you which. It is a question generator, not an answer."
  - name: "Coverage Ratio"
    formula: "count(SQL leads in region) / count(active AEs in region)"
    grain: "Per region"
    source: "leads.csv + reps.csv"
    watchout: "Filter reps to IsActive = true and to AE role. Include Ramping reps in the count but flag them — a region 'covered' by two reps in week three is not covered."
---
::

## Build it

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Compute shares, not counts, for the comparison chart"
    description: "Sessions and revenue differ by orders of magnitude, so plotting raw values hides everything. Convert each to a percentage of its own total, then plot both — now the gap is visible at a glance."
  - title: "Never join the three sources"
    description: "Sessions have no account key. Aggregate each to region separately and place them as faceted widgets sharing the Region filter. This is the 'keep them apart' pattern from the GTM engineering section, and it is the right call here."
  - title: "Use a rate for the second band"
    description: "Lead → won rate by region rather than won count, or AMER's size buries every other region and the chart only tells you AMER is big."
  - title: "Add cycle length"
    description: "SalesCycleDays by region. EMEA and APAC run longer for structural reasons — procurement, language, time zones. A region that looks slow may be normal for its geography, and comparing it to AMER without this column is how a good rep gets managed out."
  - title: "Build the shortlist table last"
    description: "Countries with sessions above a floor and zero closed-won. Sort by sessions. This is the only widget the VP will screenshot."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-globe"}
**Region and country are different questions.** Region is how the company is organised; country is where the demand actually is. A region can look healthy while its second-largest country has no coverage at all. Keep both dimensions, and make the country table drillable.
::

## The four explanations for a conversion gap

Offer these explicitly. Without them the gap gets read as "the team there is weak", which is usually wrong and always expensive.

::lesson-slides
---
items:
  - kicker: "Cause 1"
    title: "Coverage"
    icon: "i-lucide-user-x"
    bullets:
      - "Demand arrives, nobody follows up"
      - "Check speed-to-lead by region"
      - "Fixable with headcount — the expensive answer"
  - kicker: "Cause 2"
    title: "Fit"
    icon: "i-lucide-puzzle"
    bullets:
      - "Traffic is students and hobbyists"
      - "Check DisqualifiedReason by region"
      - "\"Student / hobby project\" clustering is the tell"
      - "Not fixable with headcount at all"
  - kicker: "Cause 3"
    title: "Commercial friction"
    icon: "i-lucide-credit-card"
    bullets:
      - "Currency, payment methods, local entity"
      - "Data residency and compliance"
      - "Shows up as losses on 'Failed vendor review'"
  - kicker: "Cause 4"
    title: "Timing"
    icon: "i-lucide-hourglass"
    bullets:
      - "Longer cycles mean revenue lags demand"
      - "A region opened 4 months ago SHOULD look bad"
      - "Cohort by first-touch month before judging"
---
::

::note{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**The cohort point is the one people get wrong.** APAC's revenue share looks poor partly because half its pipeline was created inside the last cycle length. Comparing a young region's revenue to a mature one's is comparing a partial year to a full one. Filter to deals created more than one cycle ago before drawing conclusions — and put that filter on the face of the dashboard.
::
