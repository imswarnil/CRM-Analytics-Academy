# Objects, Fields and Relationships

Open any sales dashboard and read a number off it — pipeline this quarter, say. That one number quietly depends on four decisions: which **object** the rows come from, which **records** were included, which **field** holds the amount, and which **relationship** was followed to group it by account or region. Get those four right and the dashboard is trustworthy. Get one wrong and it is confidently, beautifully wrong.

This lesson is about those four words.

## Objects are tables, records are rows

Salesforce stores everything in **objects**. An object is a table: *Opportunity* is the table of deals, *Account* the table of companies. Each **record** is a row — one deal, one company — and each **field** is a column: *Amount*, *Stage*, *Close Date*.

Salesforce ships **standard objects** that almost every company uses, and lets you add **custom objects** (their API names end in `__c`, as in `Subscription__c`). For go-to-market analytics, six standard objects carry most of the weight:

| Object | One record is… | Typical questions it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | a person who has shown interest but is not yet qualified | How many leads did each channel bring, and how fast were they worked? |
| **Account** | a company you sell to | Which segments and regions hold the most revenue? |
| **Contact** | a person at an account | Who are we talking to, and in which roles? |
| **Opportunity** | a potential deal, with an amount and a stage | What is in the pipeline, and what will close? |
| **Campaign** | a marketing effort | Which campaigns influenced pipeline? |
| **Case** | a customer support request | How healthy are our customers? |

## Fields have types, and types matter

Every field has a **type**, and the type decides what a dashboard can do with it. CRM Analytics sorts fields into three families:

- **Dimensions** — text and picklists (*Stage*, *Industry*, *Lead Source*). You group and filter by them.
- **Measures** — numbers and currency (*Amount*, *Number of Employees*). You sum, average and count them.
- **Dates** — (*Close Date*, *Created Date*). You put them on a time axis and compare periods.

A surprising number of broken dashboards come from a number stored as text — a "revenue" field typed as a string cannot be summed. Check the type before you build.

## Relationships connect the tables

Records point at other records through **relationship fields**. An Opportunity has an `AccountId` field that holds the Id of the Account it belongs to. That single field is what lets you show *pipeline by industry*: the amount lives on the Opportunity, the industry lives on the Account, and `AccountId` is the bridge.

There are two kinds you need to know:

- **Lookup** — a loose link. The child can exist without the parent and keeps its own sharing. *Opportunity → Campaign* is a lookup: plenty of deals have no campaign.
- **Master-detail** — a tight link. The child cannot exist without its parent, inherits the parent's sharing and is deleted with it, and the parent can carry **roll-up summary** fields such as a count of its children.

Some relationships are **many-to-many** and need a junction object in between. An opportunity can involve several contacts, and a contact can sit on several opportunities, so Salesforce connects them through **Opportunity Contact Role** records.

## Conversion: one Lead, three records

When a sales rep qualifies a Lead and converts it, Salesforce creates — or matches to existing — an **Account** for the company, a **Contact** for the person, and optionally an **Opportunity** for the deal. The Lead is kept, marked converted, and it remembers the Ids of what it became (`ConvertedAccountId`, `ConvertedContactId`, `ConvertedOpportunityId`).

Those three fields are what make **funnel analytics** possible: they let you follow a single person from the first form fill to closed revenue, and measure conversion rates between the stages. You will build exactly that funnel later in the course.

## Ids, not names

Every record has an **Id**: 15 characters, case-sensitive, or 18 characters, case-insensitive. The first three characters tell you the object — `001` is an Account, `003` a Contact, `006` an Opportunity, `00Q` a Lead.

Names repeat and get edited; Ids never change. That is why every join you will build in a CRM Analytics recipe — Opportunity to Account, Lead to its converted Opportunity — matches on an Id field, never on a name.

::note{icon="i-lucide-database"}
**How CRM Analytics reads this model.** It does not query your objects live. **Data Sync** copies the objects and fields you choose into CRM Analytics on a schedule. A **recipe** then joins them on their Ids into a **dataset** — one wide, fast table at the grain you choose, such as one row per opportunity with the account's industry and region already attached. Dashboards query datasets, not objects. The next sections build that pipeline step by step.
::
