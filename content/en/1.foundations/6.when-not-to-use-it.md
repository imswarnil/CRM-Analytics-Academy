# When Not to Use It

Being able to say "this is the wrong tool, here is why, here is what to use instead" is what separates someone who knows a product from someone who can be trusted with a decision. Five cases.

## 1. The requirement is actually real-time

CRM Analytics refreshes on a schedule. Nightly is typical; hourly is common; every few minutes is possible and expensive; sub-minute is not what the product is.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-timer"}
**The conversation to have, in one question.** "What decision does this drive, and how stale can it be before it is useless?" Asked in week one it takes ten minutes. Asked in month three, after the datasets and dashboards exist, it is a project failure. Most "real time" requests come back as "this morning is fine" — and the ones that genuinely mean seconds are operational screens, which belong on a Salesforce page, a platform event, or purpose-built tooling.
::

## 2. The screen is about one record

Columnar storage is optimised for aggregating many rows, not for retrieving one whole one. "Everything about this account on one screen" is a Salesforce record page, possibly with an embedded dashboard component for the aggregate parts.

## 3. Most of the data is not in Salesforce

CRM Analytics can absolutely read Snowflake, Redshift, S3 and databases. The question is proportion and centre of gravity.

      - "You rebuild Salesforce's sharing model as row-level security, forever"

---
::

## 4. The output is a document, not a decision

Invoices, statements, contracts, paginated regulatory filings — anything where the layout is the deliverable and every row must appear. That is a document-generation problem. CRM Analytics widgets are built to aggregate and to be explored, not to paginate faithfully.

## 5. One question, once

If someone needs one number this week and nobody will ask again, build a Salesforce report. It is free, instant, live, and adequate. CRM Analytics earns its cost when *many people* need *many answers* over *joined* data, repeatedly.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-repeat"}
**The cost that surprises people is not the licence, it is the ownership.** A dataset has a schedule that can drift, a grain that has to be explained to every new builder, a recipe somebody must maintain, and a definition somebody must defend in a meeting. That is worth paying for a practice. It is absurd for a single question.
::

## The honest summary

Use CRM Analytics when the CRM is the centre of gravity, when the security model matters, when many people need self-service over joined data, and when hours-old data is fine. That describes a very large number of real go-to-market analytics problems — including every one in this course.

Use something else when the requirement is seconds, a single record, a document, a one-off, or a company whose data lives mostly elsewhere.
