# Selection Bindings

The viewer picks something; another widget reacts. This is the family worth mastering — the inputs are visible on screen, which makes them debuggable.

## Start with the title

::tip{icon="i-lucide-type"}
**If you add one binding to a dashboard, make it the title.** "Pipeline — EMEA, Q3 FY26" instead of "Pipeline". It costs a few minutes, it never breaks, and it fixes the most expensive ambiguity in analytics communication: a filtered screenshot being read as the whole picture. Assume every widget you build will end up pasted into a message with no context, because eventually one will.
::

## Dependent filters

Selecting a region narrows the rep filter to that region's reps. Worth it when the dependent list is genuinely long:

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Check the list is long enough to matter"
    description: "Forty reps narrowed to eight is a real improvement. Four segments narrowed to three is configuration nobody notices, with maintenance nobody wants."
  - title: "Decide the unselected behaviour first"
    description: "With no region chosen, does the rep filter show everyone or nothing? Showing everyone is almost always right — an empty dependent filter looks broken, and the viewer has no way to know it is waiting on something else."
  - title: "Handle the stale selection"
    description: "A rep is selected, then the region changes and that rep is no longer in the list. The dashboard must clear the stale selection rather than silently filtering to somebody who is not in scope — this is the bug that produces an empty screen with a filter the viewer cannot see."
  - title: "Keep the chain to two levels"
    description: "Region then rep is comprehensible. Region then team then rep then product is a configuration nobody will unpick in a year, including you."
---
::

## The empty state is the whole design

::warning{icon="i-lucide-circle-slash"}
**Every selection binding needs a defined behaviour when nothing is selected.** The three options are: show everything, show nothing, or fall back to a default. Show-everything is usually right and always the safest. Show-nothing is only acceptable when the screen explains itself — a prompt saying "select a segment to begin" — because an empty dashboard with no explanation reads as broken, and that report reaches you before the explanation does.
::

## Measure toggles

A selector that changes what a chart measures rather than what it filters: ARR or deal count, gross or net, this year or last.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "Good toggle"
    subtitle: "The same question, two units"
    icon: "i-lucide-toggle-right"
    pros:
      - "Halves the space a comparison needs"
      - "The reader chooses the view that answers their version of the question"
      - "Title binds to the toggle, so the chart always says what it shows"
    cons:
      - "Only one state is visible at a time, so a side-by-side comparison is impossible"
    verdict: "Good when the two views are alternatives, not companions."
  - title: "Bad toggle"
    subtitle: "Two questions in one widget"
    icon: "i-lucide-toggle-left"
    pros:
      - "Saves space"
    cons:
      - "The reader cannot see both, and often needed both"
      - "The screenshot shows one state with no sign the other exists"
    verdict: "Two widgets. Space is cheaper than confusion."
---
::

## Multiple selections

A viewer selects three segments rather than one. Decide, and test, what the bound widget does: filter to all three, take the first, or ignore the selection entirely. All three behaviours exist in real dashboards and only one of them is what you intended.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-check-check"}
**Test every binding with nothing selected, one thing selected, and several.** Three clicks, and it catches nearly every binding bug before anybody else meets it.
::
