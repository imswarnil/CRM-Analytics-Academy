# Bindings

Faceting is automatic. A **binding** is deliberate: it takes the state of one widget — a selection, a result, a value — and feeds it into another widget's query, title or visibility.

## The four useful patterns

    role: "measure"

  - name: "Conditional display"
    type: "use sparingly"
    role: "dimension"
    note: "A widget appears only when relevant — a risk table shown only when there are accounts at risk. Elegant, and the thing most likely to confuse the next maintainer, who cannot see the widget that is not there."

## Start with the dynamic title

If you use one binding, use this one. A dashboard that states its own filters in its titles prevents the single most common error in analytics communication: a screenshot of a filtered view being read as the whole picture.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-camera"}
**Assume every dashboard will be screenshotted and pasted into a message with no context.** A title reading "Pipeline — EMEA only, created in Q3" survives that journey intact. A title reading "Pipeline" becomes a company-wide number in someone else's argument by Thursday.
::

## Where it goes wrong

::warning{icon="i-lucide-network"}
**Bindings are logic stored in widget configuration.** They cannot be read at a glance, they do not diff, and they are not documented anywhere the next person will look. A dashboard with twenty of them works beautifully and becomes untouchable — the person who inherits it can see that changing something breaks something, but not what or why, so they build a new dashboard beside it instead. That is how an org ends up with four pipeline dashboards and an argument about which is right.
::

The practical limit is about readability rather than count:

## The rule worth adopting

::note{icon="i-lucide-file-text"}
**If a binding is not obvious from looking at the screen, write it down** — in the dashboard's description, or in the project notes beside the metric definitions. One line per binding: what feeds what, and why. This takes a minute and it is the difference between a dashboard the team owns and a dashboard one person owns.
::

## When a binding is the wrong answer

Two cases worth recognising:

- **You are rebuilding a metric.** If the binding exists to compute a definition, it belongs in the recipe. Widget-level definitions drift, and a binding makes them harder to find than plain SAQL would.
- **You are cramming two audiences onto one screen.** A dashboard that reconfigures itself for sales and for finance is usually two dashboards wearing a trench coat. Two screens, each simple, beat one clever one — and they can be shared to different audiences, which the collaboration section shows is the easier thing to govern.
