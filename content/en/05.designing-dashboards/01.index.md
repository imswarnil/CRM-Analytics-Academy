# Design for a Decision

Everything in this section rests on one sentence: **a dashboard that supports no decision is decoration.**

::note{icon="i-lucide-target"}
**The test.** Name the decision, name who makes it, and name how often. "The VP of Sales Development decides where to move headcount, monthly." If you cannot fill that sentence in, you are not designing a dashboard, you are arranging charts — and no amount of layout skill rescues it.
::

## The four-second test

Someone who has never seen the dashboard opens it. In four seconds they should be able to say what it is about and whether things are good or bad.

Four seconds is roughly one headline number, one shape, and a title. That is the entire budget for the first impression — everything else is for the person who has decided to stay.

## Subtraction is the skill

Anyone can add a widget. The person who can remove four and leave the dashboard *better* is the one worth hiring.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-scissors"}
**The question that does the work: "what would you do differently depending on what it showed?"** Asked kindly, it distinguishes a widget somebody needs from one somebody is curious about. Curiosity is legitimate and it belongs in a lens, not on the screen six people check every Monday. This single question removes more clutter than any design principle.
::

Every build in this course carries a `notInScope` list for that reason. It is not an apology for what is missing — it is the record of decisions made, and it is what makes the dashboard defensible when somebody asks why their chart is not on it.
