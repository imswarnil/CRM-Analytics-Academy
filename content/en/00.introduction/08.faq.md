# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is it really free, and what is the catch?

Yes, and the catch is advertising and sponsorship. The curriculum, the datasets and the quizzes are free and open source, there is no paid tier of lessons, and nothing is held back for a course you have to buy. The site carries ads and a sponsor slot, and that is what pays for it.

The content is public markdown in a public repository, which is the part that matters: if this site disappears tomorrow, every lesson can be read from the repo, and anyone can fork it.

## Does my free Developer org expire?

A **Developer Edition** org does not expire the way a 30-day trial does. It can be deactivated for prolonged inactivity, so log in occasionally if you want to keep the work you did in it.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-hard-drive"}
**Treat it as disposable anyway.** A practice org is not a backup. If it disappears you should be able to rebuild everything in an evening — the CSVs are downloadable and the builds are written down. That is a genuinely useful property to design for, and it is exactly how you would want a real analytics project to be recoverable.
::

## Will this get me the certification?

It covers most of what the **CRM Analytics and Einstein Discovery Consultant** exam tests: the data layer, security, dashboard design, Einstein Discovery, and the admin material in section 2. It is not organised around the exam blueprint, does not drill exam-style questions beyond the section quizzes, and spends time on things the exam does not test — attribution, metric contracts, executive design — because those are what the job is.

If the certificate is the goal, use this course for understanding and a blueprint-shaped study guide for the last two weeks. If the job is the goal, this is the better use of your time.

## Can I use my own company's data instead of Namilio's?

For the first pass, no — and this is the one place the course is opinionated about something that sounds harmless.

::note{icon="i-lucide-shield"}
**Three reasons.** First, you cannot check your work: the Namilio README states the true totals, so when your dashboard says something different you know immediately that you are wrong. Real data has no answer key. Second, the Namilio data is deliberately broken in specific ways, and those breakages are the lessons — your own data is broken in different ways, which you will not recognise yet. Third, and most practically: putting real customer data into a free Developer org is a genuinely bad idea, and depending on where you work and what the data is, may not be allowed at all.
::

Once you have finished a section, rebuilding one of its dashboards on your own company's data is the best possible exercise. Do it in a sanctioned org, not a Developer Edition one.

## Why a fictional company at all?

Because a realistic dataset has to be *deliberately* imperfect to teach anything, and no real company will let you publish theirs.

Namilio's data has a fan-out that doubles revenue if you join it carelessly, a lead score that does not predict what everyone assumes it predicts, a survey response rate of about 42% that quietly biases CSAT, a product priced per message and another priced per seat that break the same ARR formula in opposite directions, and churn that arrives as a four-month fade with no cancellation event. Each one is there because it is the most common way that metric is got wrong in real companies.

## How current is this, and what happens when Salesforce renames things again?

The product has been called Wave, Einstein Analytics, Tableau CRM and CRM Analytics. Lessons use the current name and mention older ones once where you would still meet them in an old org or an old blog post.

When something is renamed or moved, the fix is a pull request against a markdown file. The changelog lists what has changed and when. If you hit a lesson whose click path no longer matches the UI, that is a genuinely useful contribution and takes about ten minutes.

## How good are the translations?

Eleven of the twelve languages are machine-translated from English by a self-hosted pipeline, and they are re-generated whenever the English source changes. They are good enough to learn from and not as good as the English.

Arabic and Urdu render right-to-left. A lesson that has not been translated yet falls back to English rather than showing an empty page. If a translated sentence is confusing, switch to English for that paragraph — and if it is *wrong*, editing the locale file directly is a welcome pull request, because a hand-fixed translation is only overwritten when its English source actually changes.

## Do I need to sign in, and what do you store?

No. Everything — lessons, datasets, quizzes — works signed out.

Signing in adds lesson completion, a progress view, and quiz scores over time. What is stored is the lesson path, a completion flag and a score. Not your answers, not your dashboards, and nothing from your Salesforce org. **This site never connects to your org**: there is no connected app, no OAuth flow against Salesforce, and no lesson that asks for org credentials.

## Can I use this to teach a team?

Yes. It is open source, and running a section as a weekly study group is the best use of it — section 7's metric-contract lesson in particular is much better with two people who disagree.

If you build something good on it, the showcase takes community dashboard write-ups, and `/contribute` explains how. Credit goes on the wall of fame.

## I found something wrong

Please open an issue or a pull request. Wrong numbers, stale click paths, a quiz answer that is arguable, an explanation that does not land — all of it is welcome, and the repository is linked from every page's *Edit this page*.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-git-pull-request"}
**The most valuable correction** is a metric definition you disagree with. Several of this course's formulas are choices rather than facts — attribution especially, where section 8 says outright that you are picking which lie to tell. If your company defines one differently and can say why, that reasoning belongs in the lesson.
::
