# What You Will Build

A course is judged by what you have at the end of it. Here is what you will have: fourteen dashboards in your own Salesforce org, built on one company's data, each with a written brief and a metric specification you could hand to a colleague.

::note{icon="i-lucide-briefcase"}
**Why this is the right artefact.** In an interview, "I completed a CRM Analytics course" is a claim. "Here is the pipeline dashboard I built, here is the decision it supports, here is the metric definition sales and finance had to agree on, and here are the three widgets I removed and why" is evidence. The second one also happens to be the actual job.
::

## What a build actually specifies

Every build opens with a brief. This is Build 7, the lead overview, as an example of the shape:

::build-brief
---
dashboard: "Lead Overview"
audience: "The VP of Sales Development and her four team leads"
question: "Are we working the leads we have, fast enough, and are the ones we work turning into pipeline?"
decision: "Where to move headcount next month, and which source to ask marketing for more of"
cadence: "Opened daily at 9am; reviewed properly in the Monday pipeline meeting"
success: "Median speed to first touch under 30 minutes, and an honest count of leads nobody has touched"
datasets:
  - "leads.csv"
  - "reps.csv"
  - "campaigns.csv"
  - "opportunities.csv"
notInScope:
  - "Lead score accuracy — audited separately in Build 8, because auditing a score on the same screen that uses it invites people to trust it"
  - "Revenue. This dashboard stops at qualified, and the handoff build picks it up"
  - "Individual rep leaderboards. The team leads asked; the answer was no, and the lesson explains why"
---
::

Two fields there do the real teaching. **`decision`** forces the question "what changes on Monday because of this screen" — if nothing does, the dashboard should not exist. And **`notInScope`** is where design happens: anyone can add a widget, and the skill being trained is knowing which four to refuse.

## Every number gets a formula

Alongside each build, the metrics are written out. Not as a description — as a specification precise enough that two people building it independently get the same number:

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "Speed to Lead"
    formula: "median(FirstTouchAt − CreatedAt), in minutes, over leads with a first touch"
    grain: "one value per team per day"
    source: "leads.csv"
    watchout: "Excluding untouched leads flatters the number badly — an untouched lead has infinite speed to lead, not zero. Report the median alongside the untouched count, always, and never one without the other."
  - name: "Lead → Opportunity Conversion"
    formula: "count(distinct leads with a linked opportunity) ÷ count(distinct leads created), by created cohort"
    grain: "one value per source per month"
    source: "leads.csv joined to opportunities.csv on ConvertedOpportunityId"
    watchout: "Cohort by lead CREATED date, not by opportunity created date. Mixing them makes last month's conversion look terrible and this month's look impossible, and the error survives review because both numbers are individually correct."
  - name: "Qualified Pipeline Created"
    formula: "sum(Opportunity.Amount) where StageName reached 'Qualified', by the lead's created month"
    grain: "one value per source per month"
    source: "opportunities.csv joined to leads.csv"
    watchout: "Amount changes over an opportunity's life. Sum the amount AT qualification from the snapshot, not today's amount, or every historical month silently rewrites itself each time you refresh."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-scissors"}
**Why the smallest dashboard is the hardest.** By the time you reach Build 14 you have built thirteen dashboards carrying roughly 120 widgets between them, and every one of them has a stakeholder who would like it on the board. The lesson is an exercise in refusal: nine numbers survive, and the lesson spends as many words on the ones that were cut as on the ones that stayed. Anyone can add. The senior skill is subtracting, in a meeting, with the person whose widget you are removing.
::

## The dashboards you are not asked to build

Three things this course deliberately does not produce, so you are not surprised later:

- **A real-time operational screen.** CRM Analytics refreshes on a schedule, not a stream. If someone needs live call queues, this is the wrong tool and section 1 says so.
- **An embedded in-page component for every Salesforce record.** Embedding is covered once in section 6 because it matters, but the course optimises for standalone decision dashboards, which is where CRM Analytics is strongest.
- **A machine-learning model presented as truth.** Einstein Discovery appears twice: once to build a story, and once — in section 9 — to audit a lead score that turns out to predict far less than everyone assumed. Both lessons end with a check, not a launch.
