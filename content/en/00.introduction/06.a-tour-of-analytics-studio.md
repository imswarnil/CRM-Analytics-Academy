# A Guided Tour

Fifteen minutes of clicking, with no goal other than familiarity. Section 1 introduces a dozen names — dataset, lens, recipe, dataflow, app, story — and they land far better on screens you have already seen than on screenshots.

Work the walkthrough above in your own org rather than reading this page. The prose below exists to give you the map in one piece, and to name the five places people get lost.

## The map in one paragraph

**Analytics Studio** is where you build. **Browse** is the file system, holding apps (folders with sharing), datasets (data), lenses (saved explorations) and dashboards. **Data Manager** is the back of house: connections to external systems, the scheduled sync that keeps local copies current, recipes that transform data into datasets, and the monitor that tells you when any of it failed. The **Analytics tab** inside Lightning Experience is the same content for people who only consume it. **Einstein Discovery** sits beside all of it, taking a dataset and an outcome and producing a model.

## The habit to start right now

In any lens, switch to **SAQL mode** after every change you make by clicking. You already know what the answer should be, so reading the generated query is the cheapest possible way to learn the language — and by the SAQL section, when clicks genuinely run out and you have to write SAQL by hand, it will already be familiar rather than new.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-repeat-2"}
**Twenty minutes, once.** Group a lens by a field, read the SAQL. Add a filter, read it again. Change the measure, read it again. Sort, limit, add a second grouping. That is the whole language's grammar in one sitting, learned against answers you can already verify by eye — which is the only reliable way to learn a query language.
::
