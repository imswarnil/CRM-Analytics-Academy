# How to Study This

Eighty lessons is enough material to drift in. Pick a schedule, keep one artefact, and use the two habits below. Everything else in this lesson is optional.

## The two habits that matter

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Build before you read the next lesson"
    description: "Not 'build at the weekend'. The lesson you just read is the one you can still build from memory, and building from memory is the whole point — it is the difference between recognising a technique and possessing it. If you cannot build it tonight, re-read it tomorrow and build it then, rather than moving on and accumulating a backlog you will never clear."
  - title: "Keep a build log"
    description: "One file, one entry per build: what you built, the number it produced, and the one thing that surprised you. Three lines. By Build 14 you have a document that is worth more than the dashboards — it is the thing you talk from in an interview, and it is how you notice that the same grain mistake caught you in Build 3 and Build 9."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-pen-line"}
**The build log format that works.** `Build 7 — Lead Overview. Median speed to lead 34 min; 218 leads never touched. Surprise: excluding untouched leads improved the median by 40%, which would have been a lie on a slide.` That entry took twenty seconds to write and it is a complete interview answer nine months later.
::

## Schedule B — four weeks intensive

For Track B, the GTM engineer, or anyone between jobs. Six to eight hours a week.

- **Week 1** — Sections 0, 1 and 2, plus section 3's grain and recipe lessons. Skim the rest of 3.
- **Week 2** — Section 4's SAQL lesson, section 5 in full, section 7 in full including loading the warehouse.
- **Week 3** — Section 8 entire: Builds 1 to 6. Two per sitting is realistic once the warehouse is loaded.
- **Week 4** — Section 9's Builds 7 to 9, then section 10's Build 11 (the ARR waterfall) and Build 14 (the board). Come back for the rest of 6, 9 and 10 afterwards.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-gauge"}
**The intensive schedule's failure mode** is that everything stays fresh and nothing consolidates. Four weeks of six hours teaches you to do these builds; it does not yet give you the reflex to check grain on a join you have not been told to check. Counter it by re-building one dashboard from scratch in week five, with the lesson closed.
::

## Schedule C — the two-week interview sprint

Ten hours a week, and a narrow target: interview well, with one thing to show.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Days 1–2 — ground"
    description: "Section 1 in full. Section 2's licences and security lessons. You must be able to answer 'how is access granted in CRM Analytics' without hesitating, because it is asked constantly and a wrong answer is disqualifying in a way a vague dashboard answer is not."
  - title: "Days 3–5 — data"
    description: "Section 3's grain lesson, the recipe lesson, and the combine-datasets lesson. Build them. Grain questions are the standard technical screen for this role."
  - title: "Days 6–8 — design and one build"
    description: "Section 5 in full, then Build 7 (the lead overview) end to end in your own org. One dashboard, built properly, with its brief and metric spec written out."
  - title: "Days 9–11 — the story"
    description: "Section 7's Designing for Decisions and Metric Contracts. These two give you the senior-sounding answers: what you left off the dashboard, and how two teams came to agree on a definition."
  - title: "Days 12–14 — rehearse"
    description: "All ten interview sets, answered OUT LOUD before reading the model answer. Then talk for ten minutes about your one dashboard to somebody who does not work in analytics. If they follow it, you are ready."
---
::

## The four failure modes

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Reading instead of building"
    icon: "i-lucide-book-x"
    description: "The most common by a wide margin. Reading produces the feeling of learning and the ability to recognise, which collapses the first time your own data has a duplicate key. Every hands-on lesson is 15 minutes to read and 45 to build; the 45 is the lesson."
  - title: "Collecting instead of finishing"
    icon: "i-lucide-layers"
    description: "Six half-built dashboards teach less than two finished ones. Finishing forces the questions — what do I cut, how do I label this, why do these two numbers disagree — that half-building never reaches."
  - title: "Skipping section 2 because it looks like admin"
    icon: "i-lucide-key-round"
    description: "Licences and security are where real projects stall and where interviews probe. It is also the shortest section. Skipping it is the highest-regret decision available in this course."
  - title: "Trusting a number because you built it"
    icon: "i-lucide-shield-alert"
    description: "Your own dashboards get the least scrutiny. The dataset README states the true totals for exactly this reason: check every build against it, and treat a match as the minimum bar rather than as success."
---
::

## How to use the quizzes properly

The answers ship inside the page — this is public markdown, they have to. That makes it trivially easy to cheat, and cheating a quiz you wrote nothing on costs you nothing except the information the quiz was going to give you.

Use them as a diagnostic instead: answer all of them, then look at what you got wrong and **re-read only that lesson's relevant section**. A quiz score of 4 out of 6 with two lessons re-read beats 6 out of 6 with nothing learned.

## How to use the interview questions

Out loud, before reading the model answer. There is a real gap between recognising a good answer and producing one under pressure, and reading the answers silently trains only the first. Record yourself once, early — most people discover their answers are forty percent longer than they thought and that they bury the conclusion at the end.

::note{icon="i-lucide-users"}
**If you have anyone to study with,** the single highest-value exercise in this course is disagreeing about a metric definition. Two people build the same metric from section 7's contract, independently, and compare numbers. They will differ. Finding out why is the job, compressed into an evening.
::
