# Start Here

This is a free, open-source course in **Salesforce CRM Analytics** — the analytics platform formerly sold as Einstein Analytics, Wave, and Tableau CRM. It is written to be worked through rather than watched, and it ends somewhere specific: with fourteen dashboards you built yourself, on a dataset messy enough to argue about.

::note{icon="i-lucide-target"}
**The promise.** Finish this and you can take a company's raw go-to-market data — leads, web sessions, marketing spend, opportunities, quotes, usage, support cases — and turn it into the small number of dashboards its executives will actually use. Not "you will know what a lens is." That is a means, not the point.
::

## What makes this different from the documentation

Salesforce's own documentation is excellent and you should read it. It has one structural limitation: it is organised by **feature**, because it has to be. Every page answers "what does this button do." No page answers "we have four sources of demand data and two teams who disagree about what a qualified lead is — now what."

This course is organised by **problem** instead, and pays for that with repetition: you will see the same recipe idea three times, in three situations where it behaves differently.

## The one rule

**Build it. Do not read it.**

Every hands-on lesson can be read in about fifteen minutes and built in about forty-five. The forty-five minutes is where the learning is. Reading a lesson about the fan-out problem produces a person who can define fan-out; building the join that doubles Namilio's revenue from $58M to $114M, staring at it, and then fixing it produces a person who checks the grain of every join for the rest of their career.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-hand"}
**The failure mode this course is designed against.** Watching a walkthrough end to end and feeling like you learned it. You did not — you learned to recognise it. Recognition collapses the moment your own data has a duplicate key. Every lesson therefore names a thing to break on purpose, and a thing to check afterwards.
::

## What this is not

- **Not certification cram.** It happens to cover most of what the *CRM Analytics and Einstein Discovery Consultant* exam tests, and every section ends with interview questions. But it is organised to make you employable, not to make you pass, and where those two diverge it picks the first.
- **Not a tour of every menu.** Features nobody uses in practice get a sentence and a link. Features used every day get a whole lesson.
- **Not vendor-neutral, and not vendor-flattering.** Where CRM Analytics is worse than a Tableau or a Looker or plain SQL, the lesson says so and says why you would still use it — usually because the security model is already built and your data is already in Salesforce.
- **Not a certificate.** You leave with dashboards and the reasoning behind them. In an interview, that is the better artefact.
