interview:
  - q: "How does sharing work in CRM Analytics?"
    a: "The unit of sharing is the app, not the dashboard — an app is a container holding datasets, lenses and dashboards, with three levels: viewer, editor and manager. That makes app design a governance decision rather than a filing one, and it is why one app per dashboard is a bad pattern: sharing is maintained per app, so twenty apps means twenty lists that drift. I share to groups or roles rather than named users, because named sharing decays silently every time someone joins or moves."
  - q: "Is sharing the same as security?"
    a: "No, and both are required. App sharing decides whether someone can open the screen; row-level security decides what is on it when they do. A user with neither and a user with only one both see a blank screen, which is why 'they can't see the dashboard' always needs both questions asked."
  - q: "A dashboard is accurate and fast but nobody uses it. What do you do?"
    a: "Treat it as a placement and ownership problem rather than a build problem. Is it where the decision is already made — embedded on the record page, in the Analytics tab, on screen in the meeting that already happens? Does it reach people who will never open a tool, via a subscription with a comparison and a link? Does anyone own it and is that name attached? A standalone dashboard competes with everything else in the day and mostly loses."
  - q: "When would you use a subscription rather than a dashboard?"
    a: "When the audience will not open a tool — most executives — or when the decision has a rhythm the delivery can match. The discipline is matching the rhythm to the decision rather than to the data: a daily email about a monthly decision trains people to ignore it, including in the month something actually moved. And a subscription needs a comparison, what changed, a link back and an owner's name, or it is a bare number nobody can act on."
  - q: "What is the risk with threshold alerts?"
    a: "That they fire often enough to become noise. People build a filter rule, the alert goes to a folder, and the one time it mattered it is in that folder too. If an alert fires more than a few times a month the threshold is wrong or the thing it watches is not exceptional — and either way the fix is upstream rather than a louder warning."
  - q: "Someone exports a widget to a spreadsheet every week. Is that a problem?"
    a: "It is a signal first. Usually they are recreating something the dashboard does not do, and that is the most reliable source of genuinely wanted features I have. The real risk is the spreadsheet becoming a competing source of truth — a snapshot that never refreshes, gets edited and gets quoted six weeks later against a dashboard that has since been corrected. Nobody is acting in bad faith, which makes it harder to resolve than an error."
  - q: "What does the minimum viable governance look like?"
    a: "Four things written down and one person responsible: an owner per dashboard, the metric definitions, a short note on which datasets carry which security, and a habit of deleting what nobody opens. The deleting is the part people skip and it is the highest-leverage one — four pipeline dashboards with slightly different definitions guarantee an argument that correctness cannot resolve."
  - q: "Why annotate a widget rather than explain it in a message?"
    a: "Because the explanation has to outlive the conversation. A verbal answer means the number's credibility depends on me being available, and an unexplainable number stops being used. Putting it where the number lives is how a dashboard becomes independent of the person who built it. My rule is that if I have explained something twice, it gets written down where it happened."

# Collaboration: Interview Questions

These questions distinguish a builder from an owner, and interviewers hiring for a senior role are usually listening for the second.

::note{icon="i-lucide-star"}
**The strongest answer in this section** is the one about the unused dashboard — because the instinctive answer is to redesign it, and the experienced answer is that placement and ownership are almost always the problem. Saying "I would ask where the decision is actually made" signals that you have shipped something nobody used and learned from it.
::

## The three sentences worth memorising

1. **"The app is the unit of sharing, so app design is a governance decision."**
2. **"Sharing decides whether they can open it; row-level security decides what is on it."**
3. **"Put the analytics where the decision is already being made."**
