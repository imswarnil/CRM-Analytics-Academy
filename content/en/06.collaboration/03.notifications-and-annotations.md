# Notifications and Annotations

Sharing is a *pull* feature — someone has to open the dashboard. **Notifications** are a *push* feature: the platform watches a number for you and emails you when it crosses a line you drew. For a busy business user, that difference is everything.

## Setting a notification

Notifications live at the **widget** level. Open the widget's action menu and choose **Set Notification**.

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Filter the dashboard to what you care about"
    description: "This step is not optional and not cosmetic — the notification captures the current filter context. Get the widget showing your slice of the data first."
  - title: "Open Set Notification on the widget"
    description: "Available on widgets backed by a measure — number tiles and charts are the usual candidates."
  - title: "Define the condition"
    description: "Pick the measure and a threshold: greater than, less than, equal to a value. 'Open pipeline below 2M', 'cases over 50'."
  - title: "Choose a schedule"
    description: "How often the platform evaluates the condition — hourly, daily, weekly, at a time you pick. The check runs on that cadence; the email only arrives if the condition is true."
  - title: "Decide on repeat behaviour"
    description: "Tick 'notify me only the first time the conditions are met' if you want a single alert per breach rather than one every evaluation."
---
::

That last checkbox is the difference between a useful alert and an inbox you start ignoring. If a metric sits below target for three weeks, do you want twenty-one emails or one? For most threshold alerts, the answer is one.

## Why the *user* sets this, not the designer

This is the part that trips people up, and it's the single most important idea in the lesson.

A notification **inherits the filter state of the dashboard at the moment it was created**. It doesn't just watch "the widget" — it watches *the query that widget was running for you, with your selections applied*.

Which means:

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Same widget, different alerts"
    icon: "i-lucide-git-branch"
    description: "A West-region manager filters to West and sets a notification. An East-region manager does the same on East. Identical widget, two genuinely different alerts — each watching their own numbers."
  - title: "The designer can't do it for them"
    icon: "i-lucide-user-x"
    description: "A notification you create belongs to you and watches your filters. It is a personal subscription to a condition, not a dashboard property you configure once for everyone."
---
::

So the designer's job here is *communication*, not configuration. When you hand over a dashboard, show the audience where **Set Notification** lives and make the point explicitly: filter to your world first, then set the alert.

::tip{icon="i-lucide-alert-circle"}
Each user is limited to **10 notifications**. That's a per-user cap across CRM Analytics, not per dashboard. It's generous for the intended use — a handful of metrics you genuinely need to be told about — but it does mean users should spend them deliberately rather than setting one on every tile they like the look of.
::

Notifications also fire on **mobile**. If your audience uses the CRM Analytics mobile app, an alert arrives as a push notification on their phone, which for a field-based team is often the only analytics touchpoint that reliably lands.

## Annotations

The second half of this lesson is **Annotate**, and it solves a different problem: not "tell me when a number moves" but "let's talk about this number".

Annotate is on the same widget action menu. It opens a comment thread attached to that specific widget, and posts it to **Chatter** — with two options that make it far more useful than a plain comment box:

- **Include a screenshot** of the widget as it currently looks, so the conversation has a picture of what you were seeing.
- **@mention** colleagues, which pulls them into the thread and notifies them the normal Salesforce way.

Two very different audiences use this, and both are worth mentioning when you train users:

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 2
items:
  - title: "Business discussion"
    icon: "i-lucide-message-circle"
    description: "'@Priya why did Central drop 30% in week 4?' — the question lives on the widget itself, with the visual attached, instead of in a lost email thread."
  - title: "Design review"
    icon: "i-lucide-clipboard-check"
    description: "During UAT, stakeholders annotate the widgets they want changed. You get feedback pinned to the exact widget rather than a document saying 'the chart in the middle looks wrong'."
---
::

That second use is one of the most practical tricks in this section. Running dashboard sign-off through annotations gives you an unambiguous, threaded, screenshot-backed list of change requests — and it happens inside the tool instead of alongside it.

Notifications and annotations keep people engaged with the dashboard. But sooner or later somebody wants the numbers *out* of it — into a spreadsheet. That's the next lesson: **downloading data**.
