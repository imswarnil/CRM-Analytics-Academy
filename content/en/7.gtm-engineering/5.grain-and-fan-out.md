# Grain, Joins and the Fan-Out That Ruins Dashboards

There is one bug in this discipline that gets past review, reaches an executive, and is believed. It is not a broken chart — a broken chart is obvious. It is a chart that is **wrong by a factor**, and looks completely normal.

::note{icon="i-lucide-alert-octagon"}
Namilio's real closed-won figure is **$57.9M**. In ten minutes you can build a chart that says $312M, using only correct-looking steps, and nothing in the tool will warn you.
::

## What fan-out is

A join multiplies rows when the right-hand side has more than one match. That is not a bug — it is what a join does. The bug is **aggregating a left-hand measure afterwards**.

::lesson-slides
---

  - kicker: "Step 1"
    title: "You start correct"
    icon: "i-lucide-check"
    bullets:
      - "accounts: 140 rows"
      - "Vertex Pay has ARR = $240,000"
      - "sum(ARR) = correct total"
  - kicker: "Step 2"
    title: "You add usage detail"
    icon: "i-lucide-git-merge"
    bullets:
      - "Join usage_monthly on AccountId"
      - "Vertex Pay has 3 products × 18 months"
      - "Vertex Pay is now 54 rows"
  - kicker: "Step 3"
    title: "ARR came along"
    icon: "i-lucide-copy"
    bullets:
      - "Every one of those 54 rows carries $240,000"
      - "The column is not wrong on any single row"
      - "It is repeated, which is different"
  - kicker: "Step 4"
    title: "The number explodes"
    icon: "i-lucide-trending-up"
    bullets:
      - "sum(ARR) = $12.96M for one account"
      - "Total reads ~$3.1B"
      - "No error, no warning, one plausible-looking bar chart"
---
::

The tell is that the *shape* stays believable. Segments still rank in the right order; regions still look sensible. Only the magnitude is absurd — and if the axis is unlabelled or formatted in millions, even that hides.

## Spot it in ten seconds

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Count the rows"
    description: "Before the join, note the input count. After, check it. opp_enriched must stay 677; account_360 must stay 140. A grown count is a fan-out, full stop."
  - title: "Count one known entity"
    description: "Pick one account and count its rows. It should be 1 in an account-grain dataset. If it is 54, you have your answer and your multiplier."
  - title: "Compare a total you already know"
    description: "Namilio's closed-won is $57.9M and open pipeline is $89.5M. Any chart claiming a total far from those is suspect. Keep two or three anchor numbers memorised."
  - title: "Check count() against count(distinct)"
    description: "In SAQL, count(rows) versus unique(AccountId). Equal means one row per account. Different means you are aggregating duplicates."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-anchor"}
**Keep anchor numbers.** Write the three or four totals you trust on a sticky note: 140 accounts, 677 opportunities, $57.9M won, $89.5M open. Most fan-outs are caught by a glance, not by a query — but only if you know what right looks like.
::

## The four safe patterns

Every join in this section is one of these.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "1 · Aggregate, then join"
    subtitle: "The default"
    icon: "i-lucide-combine"
    pros:
      - "Grain never changes"
      - "Row-count assertion stays valid"
      - "Works for usage, cases, line items"
    verdict: "Reach for this first. Group the many side to the grain you want, then attach it."
  - title: "2 · Filter to one, then join"
    subtitle: "For versioned tables"
    icon: "i-lucide-filter"
    pros:
      - "Simple and fast"
      - "Exactly right for quotes"
    cons:
      - "Throws away the history you filtered out"
    verdict: "Use when only the current record matters — IsFinalVersion, latest snapshot."
  - title: "3 · Join and don't aggregate"
    subtitle: "For detail tables"
    icon: "i-lucide-list"
    pros:
      - "Full detail available"
    cons:
      - "Any left-side sum is now wrong"
    verdict: "Fine for a row-level table widget. Never put a header measure on it."
---
::

And the fourth, which is the one people forget:

## Namilio's five fan-out traps

Specific to this warehouse. Each one has bitten somebody.

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "accounts × usage_monthly"
    formula: "140 → up to 2,616 rows"
    grain: "Multiplier: products × months, up to 54×"
    watchout: "The classic. Aggregate usage to the account first — sum BilledRevenue, avg UtilizationPct."
  - name: "opportunities × opportunity_products"
    formula: "677 → 1,378 rows"
    grain: "Multiplier: ~2× average, up to 5×"
    watchout: "Amount doubles. If you need a product filter AND the header amount, aggregate to a product list string first."
  - name: "opportunities × quotes"
    formula: "677 → 818 rows (for the 380 that have quotes)"
    grain: "Multiplier: up to 5 versions"
    watchout: "Filter IsFinalVersion = true. The only exception is the discount-creep analysis, which wants every version and never sums Amount."
  - name: "accounts × cases"
    formula: "140 → 1,777 rows"
    grain: "Multiplier: up to ~60 for a Strategic account"
    watchout: "Unhealthy accounts have the most cases, so the fan-out is worst exactly where ARR matters most — it over-weights your unhappiest customers."
  - name: "accounts × arr_snapshots"
    formula: "140 → 1,579 rows"
    grain: "Multiplier: up to 18 months"
    watchout: "Filter to one MonthEnd. Summing ClosingARR across all months answers a question nobody asked."
---
::

## The honest exception

Sometimes you *want* the multiplied grain — you just must not sum the header measure on it.

::note{icon="i-lucide-info"}
**Revenue by product** genuinely needs `opportunity_products`, because a product breakdown cannot exist at opportunity grain. Sum the **line-level `ARR`**, not the header `Amount`. Both columns exist for exactly this reason: the line sums to the header by construction, so `sum(line ARR)` across all products equals `sum(header Amount)` across all opportunities — and you can prove it.
::

That is the discipline in one sentence: **know which grain your measure belongs to, and only ever sum it there.**

    title: "Aggregate the many side"
