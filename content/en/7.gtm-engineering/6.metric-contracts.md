# Metric Contracts

You will be pulled into a meeting because marketing says 1,870 MQLs and sales says 1,240. Both numbers are correctly computed. Nobody's dashboard is broken.

::note{icon="i-lucide-handshake"}
**The real bug is linguistic.** Two teams used one word for two things. Your job is not to find the error — there isn't one — it is to write the definition down and get both sides to sign it.
::

## The five-field contract

Every metric in this curriculum is specified the same way. Four fields are easy. The fifth is the one that prevents the meeting.

::lesson-cards
---
columns: 3
items:
  - title: "1 · Name"
    icon: "i-lucide-tag"
    description: "Unambiguous, and different from any other metric's name. 'Qualified Leads' is not a name — qualified by whom?"
  - title: "2 · Formula"
    icon: "i-lucide-sigma"
    description: "Written so someone else can reproduce it in SAQL without asking you a question."
  - title: "3 · Grain"
    icon: "i-lucide-ruler"
    description: "What one row is, and which filters are baked in. 'Per opportunity, IsClosed = true' is a grain. 'Sales data' is not."
  - title: "4 · Source"
    icon: "i-lucide-database"
    description: "The dataset and field. Two metrics reading different tables will diverge whatever the formula says."
  - title: "5 · Watch-out"
    icon: "i-lucide-triangle-alert"
    description: "The specific way it goes wrong. This field is the contract. Without it people reproduce the formula and still get a different number."
  - title: "Where it lives"
    icon: "i-lucide-book-open"
    description: "On the dashboard itself — a text widget, or the tooltip. A definition in a wiki nobody opens is not a contract."
---
::

## Three Namilio metrics, specified

::metric-spec
---
items:
  - name: "MQL Count"
    formula: "count() where IsMQL = true"
    grain: "Per lead, by MQLDate — not CreatedDate"
    source: "lead_funnel"
    watchout: "Date choice is the whole argument. By CreatedDate a lead that took 21 days to qualify lands in the wrong month, and marketing's monthly number will never match sales'. Always aggregate a stage metric by that stage's own date."
  - name: "Win Rate"
    formula: "count(IsWon = true) / count(IsClosed = true)"
    grain: "Per opportunity, closed deals only"
    source: "opp_enriched"
    watchout: "Excluding open deals is correct. Including them counts open pipeline as a loss and understates the rate; dividing by ALL opportunities is the most common version of this error. Also segment it — blended win rate across Self-Serve (44%) and Strategic (24%) describes nobody."
  - name: "Pipeline Coverage"
    formula: "sum(open Amount) / quota for the period"
    grain: "Per period, per segment or rep"
    source: "opp_enriched + reps"
    watchout: "Which quota? Reps' quotas sum to more than the company target on purpose (over-assignment). Coverage against rep quota and against company target are different numbers and both are legitimate — say which one you used."
---
::

## The metrics with no single right answer

These are the ones to escalate rather than decide alone. Picking quietly is how you end up defending someone else's business rule.

::compare-cols
---
items:
  - title: "\"Customer count\""
    icon: "i-lucide-users"
    pros:
      - "Any paying account: 108"
      - "Excluding free Self-Serve: 108"
      - "With a commit contract: ~71"
      - "Named logos only: ~31"
    cons:
      - "All four are defensible"
    verdict: "Finance, marketing and the board each want a different one. Publish all four, labelled."
  - title: "\"Pipeline created\""
    icon: "i-lucide-plus-circle"
    pros:
      - "By CreatedDate: what marketing generated"
      - "By StageEnteredDate: what sales qualified"
      - "Including expansion: what the business will bill"
    cons:
      - "Swing is large enough to change a forecast"
    verdict: "Pick per audience and never blend them in one chart."
---
::

::tip{icon="i-lucide-message-square"}
**The sentence that ends the meeting.** *"We have two correct numbers because we have two definitions. Here they are side by side. Which one do we want on the board deck?"* That reframes it from a data problem — which is yours — to a business decision, which is theirs.
::

## Make the contract visible

::lesson-steps
---
items:
  - title: "Put the formula in the widget"
    description: "A text widget under each KPI with the one-line formula. Costs you 20 minutes for the dashboard and removes most incoming questions."
  - title: "Name the date field in the title"
    description: "'MQLs by MQL Date' rather than 'MQLs'. Ugly, and it has ended more arguments than any chart I have built."
  - title: "Show the filters that are on"
    description: "If Self-Serve is excluded, say so on the face of the dashboard. A hidden filter is the difference between a trusted dashboard and an abandoned one."
  - title: "Date-stamp the definition"
    description: "When a definition changes — and it will — the old charts are not wrong, they are historical. Say when it changed so trend lines can be read honestly."
---
::

## The anti-pattern

A "single source of truth" dashboard with one number per metric and no definitions, built to stop the arguments. It does not stop them; it relocates them to a meeting where you are not present, and the number gets quoted with nobody able to say what it includes.

::note{icon="i-lucide-x-circle"}
**One number with no definition is less useful than two numbers with definitions.** The second version tells the reader a choice was made. The first pretends none was.
::
